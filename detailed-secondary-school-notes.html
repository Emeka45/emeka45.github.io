HOW TO USE THIS LIBRARY

A deeper study guide for JSS1–SSS3

This library is designed as a learning resource, not merely a list of definitions. Read the explanation, work through examples, attempt the practice questions without looking at the answer, then review your mistakes. Combine these notes with your school's current scheme of work, prescribed textbooks, teacher guidance and official examination materials.

JSS1 — Build the foundations

At JSS1, concentrate on accurate understanding. Mathematics should develop number sense, fractions, decimals, percentages, introductory algebra, measurement and geometry. English Studies should strengthen grammar, comprehension, vocabulary, composition and summary skills. Basic Science should connect observation with explanation, while Social Studies and Civic Education develop understanding of family, community, citizenship, values and responsibility.

For every new concept ask: What does it mean? How do I recognise it? Where is it used? For formulas, identify every symbol and unit before substituting numbers.

JSS2 — Connect ideas and solve problems

JSS2 becomes more application-focused. Ratio, proportion, percentage, algebra and geometry can appear together in Mathematics. Science should be studied through cause and effect, while English should move from identifying grammar to deliberately using it in clear writing. Create your own examples instead of only copying examples from the note.

JSS3 — Consolidate and prepare

JSS3 should consolidate earlier work while preparing for senior secondary study and relevant examinations. Use an error notebook: record the question, your wrong method, the correct method and a similar question you can solve independently. This turns mistakes into a revision resource.

SSS1 — Move from recall to explanation

Senior-secondary work requires more precise terminology and developed reasoning. Science students should connect definitions with experiments, variables, graphs and calculations. Arts and social-science students should connect concepts with evidence and context. Commercial students should practise calculations while understanding the principles behind them. In every subject, write complete explanations rather than isolated keywords.

SSS2 — Integrate topics

Build relationships between topics rather than treating them as separate islands. Mathematics supports Physics and Economics; Biology connects with health and agriculture; English supports every subject because students must interpret questions and communicate answers precisely. Make a topic map showing prerequisites, key terms or formulas, common question types and related topics.

SSS3 — Active revision and examination accuracy

Attempt past questions under realistic time limits, check your answers using reliable solutions or teacher feedback, and record recurring weaknesses. For calculation subjects, show working clearly. For essay subjects, answer the exact command word: define, explain, compare, distinguish, evaluate and discuss require different forms of response.

Research and verification habit: If a note concerns a current law, examination rule, admission requirement, scientific finding or other changeable fact, verify it against the responsible official organisation before relying on it. These notes are a study aid; current official publications remain authoritative.

A universal revision method

  • Preview: scan headings and identify what you already know.
  • Learn: explain one concept at a time in your own words.
  • Practise: answer questions without looking at the worked example.
  • Check: identify the exact reason an answer was wrong.
  • Retrieve: close the note and reproduce the key ideas from memory.
  • Space: revisit topics over increasing intervals rather than cramming once.
  • Mix: practise related but different problem types so you learn when each method applies.
