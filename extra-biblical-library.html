📜 APOCRYPHA · PSEUDEPIGRAPHA · EARLY CHRISTIANITY

Explore the texts outside the familiar canon.

A dedicated reading guide for readers curious about the Book of Enoch, Jubilees, the Apocrypha, Ethiopian biblical books, ancient Jewish writings and early Christian texts. The aim is to make the texts easier to find while being honest about their history and status.

Important distinction: there is no single list of “books removed from the Bible.” Jewish, Protestant, Catholic, Eastern Orthodox, Ethiopian Orthodox and other traditions have different canons. Some works were canonical in particular traditions; others were read by early Christians, survived only in fragments, or were never part of a major Bible canon. This library labels those differences instead of presenting a rumour as settled fact. Read scholarly context ↗

📖 The Enoch collection

Begin here if you are interested in the Watchers, giants, heavenly journeys, calendars and apocalyptic visions. These are distinct works, not simply different chapters of one universally accepted Bible book.

ANCIENT JEWISH APOCALYPTIC WORK · ETHIOPIAN CANON

1 Enoch (Ethiopic Enoch)

The best-known Enochic work; a collection of writings on the Watchers, heavenly visions, cosmic order and judgment. Preserved complete in Ge’ez and canonical in the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church.

RELATED ANCIENT WORK

2 Enoch (Slavonic Enoch)

A separate Enochic ascent narrative preserved principally in Slavonic manuscripts. Its dating and textual history are debated; it is not the same text as 1 Enoch.

LATER JEWISH MYSTICAL WORK

3 Enoch (Hebrew Enoch)

A later mystical work associated with Rabbi Ishmael and the heavenly figure Metatron; it should not be confused with 1 Enoch or presented as a book universally removed from the Bible.

🧭 How to use this library

Read the text, check which manuscript or translation it comes from, and compare how different religious traditions classify it. “Apocrypha,” “deuterocanonical,” “pseudepigrapha,” and “early Christian writings” are related labels, but they do not mean exactly the same thing. Some modern translations are copyrighted, so this hub links to legitimate reading editions instead of copying texts without permission.
