Former NSW Police Officer Goes on Trial in Sydney for Alleged Double Murder

The trial of 31-year-old former New South Wales police officer Beau Lamarre-Condon has commenced in Sydney, with the accused pleading not guilty to the murders of his ex-partner and his new boyfriend.

The trial of 31-year-old former New South Wales police officer Beaumont Lamarre-Condon has commenced in Sydney, with the accused pleading not guilty to two charges of domestic violence-related murder and one of aggravated break and enter. As reported by BBC News, the proceedings are expected to run for three months.

Prosecution Opening and Alleged Motive

During his opening address, prosecutor Brett Hatfield SC told the court that Lamarre-Condon was driven by an "obsessive and jealous infatuation" with his ex-partner, 26-year-old TV presenter Jesse Baird. According to the prosecution, this infatuation manifested in compulsive behavior that escalated after Baird ended all contact in November 2023. Prosecutors allege that Lamarre-Condon used a friend's phone to send messages claiming he had cancer, stalked Baird, and tampered with his car following the break-up.

The trial's jury selection involved a 15-member panel chosen under Justice Natalie Adams, with three extra alternates included as a contingency for sickness or unforeseen circumstances.

Events of the Alleged Murders

The prosecution alleges that on the day of the killings, Lamarre-Condon set an alarm for 04:30 local time and messaged a friend before switching to a burner phone. He allegedly picked up a rental car and waited outside Baird's home in the Sydney suburb of Paddington for three hours while disguised as an Uber Eats rider.

Utilizing a key he had previously obtained, Lamarre-Condon allegedly entered the home. Hatfield stated that Lamarre-Condon shot Baird in the kitchen and subsequently killed Baird's new partner, 29-year-old Qantas flight attendant Luke Davies, in the bedroom as Davies attempted to call emergency services. Prosecutors argue that the attack on Baird was planned and that Davies was killed to eliminate him as a witness.

Cover-Up and Investigation

Following the alleged killings, the accused reportedly attempted to cover up the crime by sending messages from Baird's mobile phone. The couple's bodies were later discovered hidden in surfboard bags in a rural area 200 kilometers away, eight days after the incident.

Police involvement began after bloodied items belonging to Baird and Davies were found in a bin in Sydney's south. Lamarre-Condon subsequently handed himself in at a police station and was charged. He was later dismissed from the New South Wales Police force.

Additionally, the court heard that the aggravated break and enter charge stems from an August 2023 incident where Lamarre-Condon allegedly broke into Baird's home to steal a phone and wallet. Prosecutors also noted that Lamarre-Condon had previously offered to plead guilty to a single charge of manslaughter regarding Baird's death, but the offer was rejected.
