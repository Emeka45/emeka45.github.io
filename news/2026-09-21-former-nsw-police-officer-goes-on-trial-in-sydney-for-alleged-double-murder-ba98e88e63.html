Former NSW Police Officer Goes on Trial in Sydney for Alleged Double Murder

The trial of 31-year-old former New South Wales police officer Beau Lamarre-Condon has commenced in Sydney, with the accused pleading not guilty to the murders of his ex-partner and his new boyfriend.

The trial of 31-year-old former New South Wales police officer Beaumont Lamarre-Condon has commenced in Sydney, with the accused pleading not guilty to charges including domestic violence-related murder and aggravated break and enter. Reported details of the case are available via BBC News and The Guardian.

Prosecution Opening and Alleged Motive

During the opening address on Monday, prosecutor Brett Hatfield SC told the court that Lamarre-Condon was driven by an obsessive and jealous infatuation with his ex-partner, 26-year-old TV presenter Jesse Baird. According to the prosecution, this behavior escalated after Baird ended all contact in November 2023. Prosecutors claim Lamarre-Condon subsequently used a friend's phone to falsely claim he had cancer, stalked Baird, and tampered with his car.

The aggravated break and enter charge stems from an earlier incident in August 2023, where the accused allegedly broke into Baird's home to steal his phone and wallet. Additionally, the court heard that Lamarre-Condon had previously offered to plead guilty to a single charge of manslaughter relating to Baird's death, but that offer was rejected.

The Alleged Killings and Cover-Up

The trial, which is expected to run for three months, focuses on the deaths of Baird and his new partner, 29-year-old Qantas flight attendant Luke Davies. Prosecutors allege that on the day of the killings, Lamarre-Condon switched to a burner phone, picked up a rental car, and waited outside Baird's Paddington home for three hours while disguised as an Uber driver. He had previously obtained a key to the property and purchased a surfboard bag to dispose of the body.

The court heard that Lamarre-Condon shot Baird in the kitchen after entering the home. He then allegedly killed Davies in the bedroom as Davies attempted to call emergency services, acting to eliminate him as a witness. Following the incident, the accused allegedly attempted to cover up the crimes by sending messages from Baird's mobile phone.

Investigation and Trial Proceedings

Police launched an investigation after bloodied items belonging to the victims were discovered in a bin in southern Sydney. Lamarre-Condon subsequently handed himself in at a police station and was charged. Authorities located the couple's bodies hidden in surfboard bags in a rural area 200km away eight days after the alleged killings. Lamarre-Condon was dismissed from the New South Wales Police in the weeks following his arrest.

Before opening statements, a 15-member jury was selected under Justice Natalie Adams. Three extra jurors were included as a contingency for sickness or unforeseen circumstances, expanding the standard 12-person jury panel.
