Former NSW Police Officer Goes on Trial in Sydney for Alleged Double Murder

The trial of 31-year-old former New South Wales police officer Beau Lamarre-Condon has commenced in Sydney, with the accused pleading not guilty to the murders of his ex-partner and his new boyfriend.

The trial of 31-year-old former New South Wales police officer Beaumont Lamarre-Condon has commenced in Sydney, with the accused pleading not guilty to charges including domestic violence-related murder and aggravated break and enter (BBC News). The proceedings, expected to run for three months, center on the deaths of Lamarre-Condon's ex-partner, 26-year-old TV presenter Jesse Baird, and Baird's new partner, 29-year-old Qantas flight attendant Luke Davies.

Prosecution Opening and Allegations

During the opening address on Monday, prosecutor Brett Hatfield SC told the court that Lamarre-Condon was driven by an "obsessive and jealous infatuation" following a brief romantic relationship with Baird. The barrister alleged that the accused engaged in compulsive behaviors that escalated after Baird ended all contact in November 2023, which included stalking his ex-lover, tampering with his car, and using a friend's phone to send messages claiming he had cancer.

The prosecution alleges that the murder of Baird was planned in advance. According to the court, Lamarre-Condon set an alarm for 04:30 local time on the day of the killings, switched to a burner phone, rented a car, and waited outside Baird's Paddington home for three hours disguised as an Uber driver (The Guardian). Prosecutors claim he used a previously obtained key to enter the property, using his work gun to shoot Baird in the kitchen before killing Davies in the bedroom as Davies attempted to call emergency services. Hatfield stated that Davies was killed to "eliminate him as a witness".

Cover-Up Attempts and Discovery

Following the alleged killings, Lamarre-Condon attempted to cover up the crime by sending messages from Baird's mobile phone and hiding the victims' bodies in surfboard bags in a rural area 200 kilometers away (BBC News). Police investigations began after bloodied items belonging to the couple were discovered in a bin in Sydney's south. Lamarre-Condon subsequently handed himself in at a police station, and authorities located the couple's bodies eight days after the incident.

The court also heard that Lamarre-Condon had previously offered to plead guilty to a single charge of manslaughter regarding Baird's death, but the offer was rejected. In addition to the murder charges, he faces an aggravated break and enter charge concerning an alleged August 2023 incident where he broke into Baird's home to steal a phone and wallet. Lamarre-Condon was dismissed from the New South Wales Police in the weeks following his arrest.

Trial Setup

Before the opening statements, a 15-member jury was empaneled under Justice Natalie Adams. Three extra jurors were selected beyond the standard 12-person requirement to act as a contingency for potential illness or other unexpected developments throughout the anticipated three-month trial (BBC News).
