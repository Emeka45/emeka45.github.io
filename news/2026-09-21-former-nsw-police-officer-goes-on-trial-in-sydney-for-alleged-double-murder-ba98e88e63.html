Former NSW Police Officer Goes on Trial in Sydney for Alleged Double Murder

The trial of 31-year-old former New South Wales police officer Beau Lamarre-Condon has commenced in Sydney, with the accused pleading not guilty to the murders of his ex-partner and his new boyfriend.

The trial of 31-year-old former New South Wales police officer Beaumont Lamarre-Condon has commenced in Sydney, with the accused pleading not guilty to two charges of domestic violence-related murder, one count of aggravated break and enter, and using his work gun in the killings. The legal proceedings, covered extensively via reports such as BBC News and The Guardian, are expected to run for three months.

Prosecution Opening and Alleged Obsession

During the opening address on Monday, prosecutor Brett Hatfield SC detailed that Lamarre-Condon was driven by an "obsessive and jealous infatuation" with his ex-partner, 26-year-old TV presenter Jesse Baird. According to the crown, this infatuation manifested in compulsive behaviors that escalated after Baird ended all contact in November 2023. The court heard that the accused previously used a friend's phone to falsely claim he had cancer, stalked Baird, and tampered with his car.

The aggravated break and enter charge stems from an earlier August 2023 incident where Lamarre-Condon allegedly broke into Baird's home to steal his phone and wallet. Additionally, prosecutor Hatfield revealed that the accused had previously offered to plead guilty to a single charge of manslaughter regarding Baird's death, but the offer was rejected by authorities.

The Day of the Alleged Murders

Prosecutors allege that on the day of the killings, Lamarre-Condon woke early, texted a friend about updating them later, switched to a burner phone, and picked up a rental car. Dressed disguised as an Uber driver, he allegedly waited outside Baird’s home in the Sydney suburb of Paddington for three hours, having previously purchased a surfboard bag to dispose of the body.

Using a key he had previously obtained, Lamarre-Condon allegedly entered the property and shot Baird in the kitchen. He then targeted Baird's new partner, 29-year-old Qantas flight attendant Luke Davies, in the bedroom as Davies attempted to call emergency services. Hatfield told the court that the primary motive for killing Davies was to "eliminate him as a witness."

Cover-Up and Police Discovery

Following the alleged murders, Lamarre-Condon attempted to conceal the crime, which included sending misleading text messages from Baird's mobile phone. The victims' bodies were ultimately found hidden inside surfboard bags in a rural area 200 kilometers away, eight days after the incident occurred. Police were initially alerted when bloodied belongings of Baird and Davies were discovered in a bin in southern Sydney, prompting Lamarre-Condon to hand himself in at a police station. He was subsequently dismissed from the New South Wales Police force.

Jury and Court Proceedings

Before opening statements began, a 15-member jury was selected under Justice Natalie Adams. While juries in the jurisdiction typically consist of 12 people, three extra members were added as a contingency for potential sickness or other unforeseen circumstances throughout the lengthy three-month trial.
