Former NSW Police Officer Goes on Trial in Sydney for Alleged Double Murder

The trial of 31-year-old former New South Wales police officer Beau Lamarre-Condon has commenced in Sydney, with the accused pleading not guilty to the murders of his ex-partner and his new boyfriend.

The trial of 31-year-old former New South Wales police officer Beaumont Lamarre-Condon has commenced in Sydney, with the accused pleading not guilty to charges including domestic violence-related murder and aggravated break and enter. Reported details of the case are available via BBC News.

Opening Address and Prosecution Claims

During the opening address on Monday, prosecutor Brett Hatfield SC detailed allegations that Lamarre-Condon was driven by a "jealous infatuation" with his ex-partner, 26-year-old television presenter Jesse Baird. According to the prosecution, the accused developed an obsessive pattern of behavior following a brief romantic relationship, which intensified after Baird ended all contact in November 2023. Court proceedings heard that Lamarre-Condon allegedly stalked Baird, used a friend's phone to send messages claiming he had cancer, and tampered with his car.

Further allegations outline that Lamarre-Condon planned the attacks ahead of time. On the day of the incident, he allegedly set an alarm for 04:30 local time, switched to a burner phone, and rented a vehicle. Prosecutor Hatfield stated that the former officer waited outside Baird's home in the Sydney suburb of Paddington for three hours while disguised as an Uber Eats rider, having previously obtained a key and purchased a surfboard bag to dispose of a body.

The Alleged Killings and Cover-Up

Prosecutors told the jury that Lamarre-Condon used his work-issued firearm to shoot Baird in the kitchen. He then allegedly killed Baird's new partner, 29-year-old Qantas flight attendant Luke Davies, in the bedroom while Davies was attempting to call emergency services. The prosecution asserted that Davies was targeted specifically to eliminate him as a witness.

In the hours and days following the alleged killings, Lamarre-Condon attempted to conceal the crimes, which included sending text messages from Baird's mobile phone. The victims' bodies were ultimately discovered hidden in surfboard bags within a rural area 200 kilometers away, eight days after the incident. Police investigations initially began after blood-stained items belonging to Baird and Davies were located in a bin in southern Sydney, prompting Lamarre-Condon to hand himself in at a police station.

Trial Logistics and Legal Context

The trial is presided over by Justice Natalie Adams and is expected to last for three months. A 15-member jury was selected ahead of the opening statements, featuring three extra alternate jurors to account for potential sickness or unforeseen circumstances. Additional background on the proceedings is outlined by The Guardian.

The charges against Lamarre-Condon include two counts of domestic violence-related murder and one count of aggravated break and enter, the latter relating to an unrelated incident in August 2023 where he allegedly broke into Baird's home to steal a phone and wallet. Additionally, the court heard that Lamarre-Condon had previously offered to plead guilty to a single charge of manslaughter regarding Baird's death, but the offer was rejected. He was dismissed from the New South Wales Police force in the weeks following his arrest and charges.
