Former NSW Police Officer Goes on Trial in Sydney for Alleged Double Murder

The trial of 31-year-old former New South Wales police officer Beau Lamarre-Condon has commenced in Sydney, with the accused pleading not guilty to the murders of his ex-partner and his new boyfriend.

The trial of 31-year-old former New South Wales police officer Beaumont Lamarre-Condon has commenced in Sydney, with the accused pleading not guilty to two charges of domestic violence-related murder and one of aggravated break and enter. Additional details of the case have been reported by BBC News.

Trial Opening and Prosecution Claims

In his opening address on Monday, prosecutor Brett Hatfield SC told the court that Lamarre-Condon was driven by a "jealous infatuation" with his ex-partner, 26-year-old TV presenter Jesse Baird. The barrister described an obsessive pattern of behavior that escalated after Baird ended all contact in November 2023. According to the prosecution, Lamarre-Condon used a friend's phone to falsely claim he had cancer, stalked Baird, and tampered with his car.

The trial, expected to run for three months, focuses on the deaths of Baird and his new partner, 29-year-old Qantas flight attendant Luke Davies. Prosecutors allege that Lamarre-Condon used his work gun to kill the couple at a home in the Sydney suburb of Paddington in 2024. The accused allegedly entered the property using a key he had previously obtained, shooting Baird in the kitchen and subsequently killing Davies in the bedroom as Davies attempted to call emergency services.

Cover-Up and Police Investigation

The court heard that Lamarre-Condon planned the attack and targeted Davies to eliminate him as a witness. On the day of the alleged killings, prosecutors stated that the accused set an alarm for 04:30 local time, switched to a burner phone, picked up a rental car, and waited outside Baird's home for three hours disguised as an Uber driver. He had previously purchased a surfboard bag to dispose of Baird's body.

In the days following the incident, Lamarre-Condon allegedly attempted to cover up the crimes by sending messages from Baird's mobile phone. The victims' bodies were eventually found hidden in surfboard bags in a rural area 200 kilometers away, eight days after the killings took place. Police initially became involved after bloodied items belonging to Baird and Davies were discovered in a bin in Sydney's south. Lamarre-Condon subsequently handed himself in at a police station and was charged, after which he was sacked by the New South Wales Police.

Additional Charges and Court Proceedings

The aggravated break and enter charge stems from an incident in August 2023, where Lamarre-Condon allegedly broke into Baird's home to steal his phone and wallet. The prosecution also disclosed that Lamarre-Condon had previously offered to plead guilty to a single charge of manslaughter regarding Baird's death, but the offer was rejected.

Before the opening statements, Justice Natalie Adams presided over the selection of a 15-member jury. Three extra jurors were chosen beyond the standard twelve as a contingency for sickness or unforeseen circumstances.
