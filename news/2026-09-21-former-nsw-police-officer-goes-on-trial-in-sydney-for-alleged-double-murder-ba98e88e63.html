Former NSW Police Officer Goes on Trial in Sydney for Alleged Double Murder

The trial of 31-year-old former New South Wales police officer Beau Lamarre-Condon has commenced in Sydney, with the accused pleading not guilty to the murders of his ex-partner and his new boyfriend.

The trial of 31-year-old former New South Wales police officer Beaumont Lamarre-Condon has commenced in Sydney, with the accused pleading not guilty to two charges of domestic violence-related murder and one of aggravated break and enter. As reported by BBC News, the proceedings are expected to run for three months before a 15-member jury overseen by Justice Natalie Adams.

Prosecution Opening and Alleged Obsession

During the opening address on Monday, prosecutor Brett Hatfield SC outlined that Lamarre-Condon was driven by an "obsessive and jealous infatuation" with his former partner, 26-year-old TV presenter Jesse Baird. The relationship had ended when Baird cut off all contact in November 2023, prompting what prosecutors describe as a series of compulsive behaviors, including stalking, tampering with Baird's car, and sending messages claiming to have cancer.

The charges also incorporate an earlier incident from August 2023, where Lamarre-Condon allegedly broke into Baird's home to steal a phone and wallet. Furthermore, the court heard that the accused had previously offered to plead guilty to a single charge of manslaughter regarding Baird's death, but the offer was rejected.

Events of the Alleged Killings

According to the prosecution, Lamarre-Condon planned the attacks meticulously. On the day of the alleged murders, he set his alarm for 04:30 local time, switched to a burner phone, rented a car, and waited outside Baird's Paddington home for three hours while disguised as an Uber Eats rider. He had reportedly purchased surfboard bags beforehand to transport the bodies.

Using a previously obtained key, Lamarre-Condon entered the residence and allegedly shot Baird in the kitchen. He then shot Baird's new partner, 29-year-old Qantas flight attendant Luke Davies, in the bedroom as Davies attempted to call emergency services. Prosecutors stated that Davies was killed specifically to eliminate him as a witness.

Investigation and Aftermath

Following the killings, the accused allegedly attempted to cover up the crime by sending messages from Baird's mobile phone. The investigation began after blood-stained items belonging to both victims were discovered in a waste bin in southern Sydney. Lamarre-Condon subsequently handed himself in at a police station and was charged, while authorities located the couple's bodies hidden in surfboard bags in a rural area 200 kilometers away eight days after the incident.

In the weeks following his arrest and charges, Lamarre-Condon was dismissed from the New South Wales Police Force. Additional details of the case and ongoing coverage can be followed via The Guardian.
