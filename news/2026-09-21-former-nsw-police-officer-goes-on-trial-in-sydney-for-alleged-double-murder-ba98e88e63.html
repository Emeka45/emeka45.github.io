Former NSW Police Officer Goes on Trial in Sydney for Alleged Double Murder

The trial of 31-year-old former New South Wales police officer Beau Lamarre-Condon has commenced in Sydney, with the accused pleading not guilty to the murders of his ex-partner and his new boyfriend.

The trial of 31-year-old former New South Wales police officer Beau Lamarre-Condon has commenced in Sydney, with the accused pleading not guilty to charges including domestic violence-related murder and aggravated break and enter. As reported by BBC News, the legal proceedings center on the deaths of Lamarre-Condon's former partner, 26-year-old television presenter Jesse Baird, and Baird's new partner, 29-year-old Qantas flight attendant Luke Davies.

Prosecution Opening and Alleged Obsession

During the opening address on Monday, prosecutor Brett Hatfield SC told the court that Lamarre-Condon was driven by an "obsessive and jealous infatuation" with Baird following the conclusion of their brief romantic relationship. The prosecution alleged that this obsession manifested in compulsive behavior after Baird cut off contact in November 2023, including stalking, tampering with Baird's car, and sending messages claiming to have cancer.

Further details emerged regarding an earlier incident in August 2023, which forms the basis of an aggravated break and enter charge alleging Lamarre-Condon broke into Baird's home to steal a phone and a wallet. Mr. Hatfield also noted that the accused had previously offered to plead guilty to a single charge of manslaughter regarding Baird's death, though that offer was rejected.

Events of the Alleged Murders

Outlining the timeline of the day of the killings, the prosecution alleged that Lamarre-Condon woke early, switched to a burner phone, rented a car, and waited outside Baird's Paddington home for three hours disguised as an Uber Eats rider. Having previously obtained a key and purchased a surfboard bag to later conceal the victims' bodies, the accused allegedly entered the property and shot Baird in the kitchen.

According to the prosecution, Lamarre-Condon then shot Davies in the bedroom as Davies attempted to call emergency services, acting to eliminate him as a witness. Following the incident, the accused allegedly attempted to cover up the crime by sending text messages from Baird's mobile phone.

Investigation and Trial Details

Police launched an investigation after bloodied items belonging to Baird and Davies were discovered in a rubbish bin in southern Sydney. Lamarre-Condon subsequently handed himself in at a police station and was charged, while authorities located the couple's bodies hidden in surfboard bags in a rural area 200 kilometers away eight days after the killings. Lamarre-Condon was dismissed from the New South Wales Police force shortly after being charged.

Presided over by Justice Natalie Adams, the trial is expected to last for three months. A 15-member jury was selected prior to the opening statements, including three alternate jurors to account for potential sickness or unforeseen circumstances during the lengthy proceedings.
