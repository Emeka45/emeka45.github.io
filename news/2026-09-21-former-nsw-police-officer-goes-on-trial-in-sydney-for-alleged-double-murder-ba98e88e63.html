Former NSW Police Officer Goes on Trial in Sydney for Alleged Double Murder

The trial of 31-year-old former New South Wales police officer Beau Lamarre-Condon has commenced in Sydney, with the accused pleading not guilty to the murders of his ex-partner and his new boyfriend.

The trial of 31-year-old former New South Wales police officer Beaumont Lamarre-Condon has commenced in Sydney, with the accused pleading not guilty to charges including domestic violence-related murder and aggravated break and enter (BBC News). The high-profile proceedings are expected to run for three months before Justice Natalie Adams, with a 15-member jury selected as a contingency for unforeseen circumstances.

Prosecution Opening and Allegations of Obsession

During the opening address on Monday, prosecutor Brett Hatfield SC detailed the Crown's case, alleging that Lamarre-Condon was driven by an obsessive and jealous infatuation with his former partner, 26-year-old television presenter Jesse Baird. According to the prosecution, this infatuation escalated significantly after Baird ended all contact with Lamarre-Condon in November 2023. The court heard that the accused engaged in various compulsive behaviors during this period, which included using a friend's phone to falsely claim he had cancer, stalking Baird, and tampering with Baird's vehicle.

Additionally, Lamarre-Condon faces an aggravated break and enter charge stemming from an August 2023 incident, where he allegedly broke into Baird's home to steal a phone and wallet. Prosecutors also noted that the accused had previously offered to plead guilty to a single charge of manslaughter regarding Baird's death, but that offer was rejected.

Events of the Alleged Killings

The prosecution outlined a carefully planned sequence of events leading up to the deaths of Baird and his new partner, 29-year-old Qantas flight attendant Luke Davies. On the day of the alleged murders, Lamarre-Condon reportedly set an alarm for 04:30 local time and sent a text message to a friend indicating he would update them later with no sign of his intentions. He then allegedly switched to a burner phone, acquired a rental car, and waited outside Baird's Paddington home for three hours disguised as an Uber Eats driver, having previously purchased a surfboard bag to conceal a body (BBC News, The Guardian).

Using a key he had previously obtained, Lamarre-Condon entered the property and allegedly shot Baird in the kitchen using his work firearm. When Davies attempted to use his phone to call emergency services for help, he was likewise shot and killed in the bedroom. Hatfield argued that the primary motive for killing Davies was to eliminate him as a witness.

Cover-Up and Investigation

Following the alleged killings, the accused attempted to conceal the crimes, which included sending messages from Baird's mobile phone. The victims' bodies were ultimately discovered hidden in surfboard bags in a rural area 200 kilometers away, eight days after the incident. The police investigation began after bloodied personal items belonging to both men were located in a bin in southern Sydney. Lamarre-Condon subsequently handed himself in at a police station, where he was formally charged, and was later dismissed from the New South Wales Police force.
