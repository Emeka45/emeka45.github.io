Lagos State Orders Immediate Relocation of Residents in Flood-Prone Areas Following NiHSA Alert

Following a fresh seven-day flood alert issued by the Nigeria Hydrological Services Agency, the Lagos State Government has instructed residents living in low-lying and flood-prone communities to evacuate immediately.

Following a fresh seven-day flood alert issued by the Nigerian Hydrological Services Agency (NiHSA), the Lagos State Government has instructed residents living in low-lying and flood-prone communities to relocate immediately to higher ground. The directive aims to prevent the loss of lives and property as water levels rise across vulnerable regions.

Vulnerable Areas and Affected Locations

According to details shared by the Lagos Commissioner for the Environment and Water Resources, Tokunbo Wahab, in a statement released by the ministry’s Director of Public Affairs, Kunle Adeshina, the NiHSA advisory impacts Lagos and 14 other states. Vulnerable areas explicitly highlighted within Lagos include:

  • Eti-Osa
  • Lagos Island
  • Oke-Ira Nla in Ajah
  • Kirikiri
  • Ilaje Waterfront Jetty and Jetty Terminal B

Additionally, communities along the Ogun River and major waterways have been advised to exercise heightened caution between September 19 and 25. Specifically, settlements downstream of Isheri Olofin—such as Isheri North, Kara, Itowolo, Ajegunle, Majidun, Ogolonto, and parts of Agboyi—have been placed on high alert due to rainfall intensity and rising water levels in connected river systems.

Government Directive and Safety Warnings

State authorities have urged residents not to wait until floodwaters rise before taking action, emphasizing that early preparation and temporary relocation are crucial. Commissioner Wahab noted that the state is treating the advisory with utmost seriousness and has intensified monitoring across identified riverine and low-lying locations. Residents residing near rivers, lagoons, wetlands, and drainage channels are advised to monitor official forecasts and relocate temporarily when flooding becomes imminent.

The NiHSA report warned that rising river levels could inundate vulnerable communities, affecting critical public and private facilities including schools, healthcare centres, markets, farms, and places of worship. More than 8,000 communities and 4,500 schools across 15 states face potential flood risks during this period.

Mitigation and Response Measures

The Lagos State Government has called on local governments, emergency management agencies, community leaders, and citizens to activate early-warning systems and move people, livestock, and valuables to safer locations. Authorities have committed to maintaining routine cleaning and monitoring of drainage infrastructure across the state.

Residents are strongly discouraged from dumping refuse into drains, canals, and waterways, as blocked channels exacerbate flooding. Furthermore, the public has been cautioned against walking or driving through flooded roads, bridges, and fast-moving water, with the government reassuring citizens that further updates will be provided as the situation evolves.
