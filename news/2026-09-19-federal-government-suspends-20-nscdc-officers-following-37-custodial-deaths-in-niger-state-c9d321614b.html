Federal Government Suspends 20 NSCDC Officers Following 37 Custodial Deaths in Niger State

The Federal Government has suspended 20 Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps officers and the Niger State Commandant over the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody, prompting widespread calls for a transparent investigation from political bodies like the Allied Peoples Movement.

The Federal Government has taken decisive administrative action following the tragic deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners while in the custody of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC) in Niger State. Minister of Interior Olubunmi Tunji Ojo announced the immediate suspension of 20 NSCDC officers alongside the Niger State Commandant, Suberu Aniviye, as investigations into the incident commence.

Government Action and Independent Panel

The suspensions follow a directive from President Bola Tinubu for a comprehensive and thorough investigation into the events leading up to the fatalities, which occurred on Thursday, September 17, 2026. According to official reports, the affected personnel include the state commandant as well as 19 other officers involved in various capacities such as station guard, arrest operations, investigations, intelligence, legal duties, and guard responsibilities.

To ensure accountability, the Federal Government constituted a 10-member independent investigative committee. Chaired by retired Department of State Services Deputy Director General Jonathan Kure, with former Nigerian Law School Director General Prof. Isa Hayatu Chiroma serving as secretary, the panel includes notable figures such as retired AIG Hosea Hassan Karma, Prof. Olayinka Buhari, and human rights activist Deji Adeyanju. Additional stakeholders represented on the committee include the Minna Emirate Council, the Niger State Government, the Miners Association of Nigeria through National Secretary Liman Sulaiman, Blueprint journalist Zainab Suleiman Okino, and public affairs analyst George Agbakahi.

The committee has been tasked with establishing the true identities of the deceased, uncovering the circumstances surrounding their arrests and detentions, and determining the direct cause of the fatalities. Furthermore, the panel is mandated to evaluate issues of responsibility, complicity, negligence, and misconduct, while proposing appropriate recommendations including potential compensation and preventative safeguards. The panel operates with a two-week timeframe to submit its findings to the Minister of Interior, who has warned against any attempts to tamper with records, obstruct witnesses, or conceal material evidence.

Background and Political Reactions

The detained individuals were initially apprehended during separate security operations conducted on September 15 and 16 in M.I. Wushishi and Lukoto villages in Niger State. State NSCDC authorities indicated that the suspects were arrested in connection with illegal mining activities, though the exact medical causes of death remain under official review pending the findings of the independent panel.

The incident has triggered widespread public concern and sharp political commentary. The Allied Peoples Movement (APM), through a statement issued by its National Publicity Secretary Abubakar Yusuf, strongly condemned the deaths, describing them as entirely avoidable. The party raised concerns over allegations that 66 detainees were confined in a single facility under severe conditions, leading to intense heat and fatalities. Additionally, the APM voiced concern over reports regarding the use of ammunition and tear gas against protesters who took to the streets following the announcement of the deaths, warning against actions that could risk further harm.
