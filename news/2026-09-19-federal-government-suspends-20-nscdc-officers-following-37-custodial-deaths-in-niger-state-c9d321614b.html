Federal Government Suspends 20 NSCDC Officers Following 37 Custodial Deaths in Niger State

The Federal Government has suspended 20 Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps officers and the Niger State Commandant over the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody, prompting widespread calls for a transparent investigation from political bodies like the Allied Peoples Movement.

The Federal Government has taken decisive administrative action following a major security tragedy in Niger State, where 37 suspected illegal miners died while held in detention. In response to the fatalities, authorities have suspended 20 personnel from the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (FG Suspends 20 NSCDC Officers, Sets Up Panel Over 37 Deaths In Custody). Alongside these line personnel, the Niger State Commandant of the corps has also been relieved of duties as part of the initial disciplinary measures.

Official Response and Political Condemnation

The high-casualty incident has drawn intense public scrutiny and sharp criticism from political organizations. Notably, the Allied Peoples Movement has stepped forward to publicly condemn the loss of 37 lives in custodial care (APM condemns death of 37 in NSCDC custody, demands transparent probe). The political party emphasized the gravity of the situation and insisted that authorities conduct a thorough, open, and fully transparent inquiry to establish accountability.

Investigation Panel Established

To address the circumstances surrounding the deaths, the government has established a dedicated investigative panel tasked with reviewing operational protocols and determining responsibility within the detention facility. While administrative suspensions have already taken effect, further actions are expected to hinge on the findings of the newly formed investigative committee as public pressure mounts for justice and institutional reform.
