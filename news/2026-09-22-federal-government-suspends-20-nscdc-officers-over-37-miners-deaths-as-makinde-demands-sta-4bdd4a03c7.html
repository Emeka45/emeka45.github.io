Federal Government Suspends 20 NSCDC Officers Over 37 Miners' Deaths as Makinde Demands State Control

The Federal Government has suspended 20 Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps officers alongside the Niger State Commandant following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody, prompting Oyo State Governor Seyi Makinde to renew calls for states to control mining operations.

The Federal Government has suspended 20 officers of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC) alongside the Niger State Commandant, Suberu Aniviye, following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody. The incident, which occurred on Thursday, September 17, 2026, has prompted high-level administrative responses and renewed political debates regarding the constitutional framework of the nation's mining sector.

Federal Government Response and Investigation Panel

Following a directive from President Bola Tinubu for a comprehensive inquiry, Minister of Interior Olubunmi Tunji Ojo announced the suspensions on Saturday, September 19, 2026. The suspended personnel include the Niger State Commandant as well as 20 other officers involved in station guard, arrest, investigation, intelligence, legal, and guard duties.

To investigate the tragedy, the Federal Government constituted a 10-member independent committee. Chaired by retired Department of State Services Deputy Director General Jonathan Kure, with former Nigerian Law School Director General Prof. Isa Hayatu Chiroma serving as secretary, the panel's mandate is to:

  • Establish the identities of the 37 deceased individuals.
  • Examine the circumstances surrounding their arrest and detention.
  • Determine the exact cause and circumstances of their deaths.
  • Assess responsibility, complicity, negligence, and misconduct.
  • Recommend appropriate actions, compensation where applicable, and preventive measures.

The committee, which also includes representatives from the Minna Emirate Council, the Niger State Government, security experts, human rights advocates, and media practitioners, has been granted a two-week timeframe to submit its report to the Minister of Interior. Tunji Ojo explicitly warned that any interference, destruction of evidence, or intimidation of witnesses would be treated as a severe offense.

Political Reactions and Calls for State Control

The custody deaths have reignited conversations concerning federal oversight and mineral resource governance. Speaking during the Makinde/Daura 2027 Presidential Town Hall meeting in Katsina on Tuesday, September 22, 2026, Allied Peoples Movement presidential candidate and Oyo State Governor Seyi Makinde called for the devolution of mining responsibilities to state governments (The Punch).

Makinde argued that state administrations would manage the sector more effectively if granted greater control, while the Federal Government retained supervisory oversight. Pointing to the Exclusive Legislative List currently enshrined in the Nigerian Constitution, Makinde stressed that systemic issues within the industry require structural reform, addressing both the supply side and the demand side that legitimizes informal operations.

As the independent investigative panel commences its work with a two-week deadline, the final cause of death for the 37 detainees remains pending official confirmation from the ongoing probe.
