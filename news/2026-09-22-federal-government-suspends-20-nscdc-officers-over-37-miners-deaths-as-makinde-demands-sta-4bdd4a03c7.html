Federal Government Suspends 20 NSCDC Officers Over 37 Miners' Deaths as Makinde Demands State Control

The Federal Government has suspended 20 Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps officers alongside the Niger State Commandant following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody, prompting Oyo State Governor Seyi Makinde to renew calls for states to control mining operations.

The Federal Government has taken decisive administrative action by suspending 20 officers of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC), including the Niger State Commandant. This development comes in the wake of the tragic deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners who died while in custody, an incident that has drawn widespread national attention and sparked debate over institutional accountability and resource management in Nigeria.

In response to the incident, authorities have established an investigative panel to probe the circumstances surrounding the fatalities. Details regarding how the 37 individuals lost their lives while detained have brought security protocols and custodial management under intense scrutiny. The suspension of the high-ranking state commandant and multiple personnel underscores the gravity of the ongoing inquiry.

Governor Makinde Calls for Mining Devolution

Amid the fallout from the tragedy, Oyo State Governor Seyi Makinde has publicly weighed in on the structural challenges facing the solid minerals sector. As reported by The Punch, Governor Makinde asserted that sub-national governments would perform more effectively in managing mineral resources and regulating mining activities if control were devolved to the states.

The Governor's stance highlights the ongoing constitutional debate regarding the exclusive control of mineral resources by the federal government versus the operational proximity and oversight capacity of state governments. Incidents involving unauthorized or informal mining operations frequently expose regulatory gaps and security vulnerabilities at the local level.
