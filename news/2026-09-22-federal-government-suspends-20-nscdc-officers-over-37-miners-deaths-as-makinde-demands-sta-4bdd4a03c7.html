Federal Government Suspends 20 NSCDC Officers Over 37 Miners' Deaths as Makinde Demands State Control

The Federal Government has suspended 20 Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps officers alongside the Niger State Commandant following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody, prompting Oyo State Governor Seyi Makinde to renew calls for states to control mining operations.

Federal Government Suspends Officers Following Custody Deaths

The Federal Government has taken swift administrative action following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners who died while in the custody of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC) in Niger State. Minister of Interior Olubunmi Tunji Ojo announced the suspension of 20 NSCDC personnel alongside the Niger State Commandant, Suberu Aniviye, in connection with the tragedy (The Reporter Media).

The suspensions stem from an incident that occurred on Thursday, September 17, 2026. President Bola Tinubu subsequently ordered a comprehensive investigation into the circumstances surrounding the arrests, detentions, and eventual deaths of the 37 individuals (The Reporter Media).

Investigation Panel Constituted

To probe the incident thoroughly, the Federal Government established a 10-member independent investigative committee. Chaired by Jonathan Kure, a retired Deputy Director General of the Department of State Services, the committee counts Prof. Isa Hayatu Chiroma, former Director General of the Nigerian Law School, as its secretary (The Reporter Media).

Additional members include retired AIG Hosea Hassan Karma, Prof. Olayinka Buhari, representatives from the Minna Emirate Council and the Niger State Government, Liman Sulaiman of the Miners Association of Nigeria, human rights lawyer Deji Adeyanju, journalist Zainab Suleiman Okino, and public affairs analyst George Agbakahi (The Reporter Media).

The panel's mandate includes:

  • Establishing the identities of the deceased miners.
  • Investigating the circumstances of their arrest, detention, and subsequent deaths.
  • Determining accountability, negligence, and any potential misconduct or complicity.
  • Recommending appropriate disciplinary actions, compensation where applicable, and preventive safeguards.

The panel has been given a timeframe of two weeks to conclude its findings and submit its report to the Minister of Interior (The Reporter Media). The suspended officers include personnel tied to guard duties, intelligence, legal assignments, arrests, and investigations (The Reporter Media).

Governor Makinde Renews Calls for State Control Over Mining

In response to the tragedy, Oyo State Governor and Allied Peoples Movement presidential candidate Seyi Makinde renewed public calls for the devolution of mining responsibilities to state governments (The Punch). Speaking during a town hall meeting in Katsina, Makinde argued that state governments are better positioned to manage the sector effectively if granted greater control (The Punch).

Highlighting that mining is currently listed on the Exclusive Legislative List of the Nigerian Constitution, Makinde proposed transferring primary administrative duties to the states while maintaining overarching federal oversight (The Punch). Pointing to the loss of young lives seeking livelihoods, he emphasized that systemic supply and demand pressures surrounding informal mining require structural policy reforms (The Punch).
