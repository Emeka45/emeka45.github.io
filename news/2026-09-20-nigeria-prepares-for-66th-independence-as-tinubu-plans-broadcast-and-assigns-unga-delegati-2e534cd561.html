Nigeria Prepares for 66th Independence as Tinubu Plans Broadcast and Assigns UNGA Delegation to Shettima

President Bola Tinubu is scheduled to address Nigerians on October 1 as part of a low-key programme for the nation's 66th Independence anniversary, alongside directing Vice President Kashim Shettima to lead the Nigerian delegation to the 81st United Nations General Assembly.

President Bola Tinubu is set to address Nigerians on October 1, 2026, as part of a low-key programme arranged for the nation's 66th Independence Anniversary. Alongside preparations for the domestic milestone, the administration has finalized international representations, with Vice President Kashim Shettima mandated to lead the Nigerian delegation to the 81st Session of the United Nations General Assembly in New York, United States (The Reporter; Punch).

Low-Key Plans for the 66th Independence Anniversary

To coordinate events leading up to the national day, the Federal Government established an Inter-Ministerial Committee chaired by the Secretary to the Government of the Federation, Senator George Akume. At the inaugural meeting, Akume was represented by the Permanent Secretary of the Political and Economic Affairs Office, Bekearedebo Augusta Warrens. Akume noted that the anniversary offers citizens a chance to reflect on the legacies and sacrifices of the founding figures who secured independence from colonial rule, while also highlighting the administration's reform efforts amidst current socio-economic challenges (The Reporter).

Due to limited time before October 1, the SGF instructed the committee to maintain a low-key schedule. The lined-up events begin with a World Press Conference on September 24, followed by a Juma’at Service on September 25 and a Church Service on September 27. An Independence Day Public Lecture is scheduled for September 30, culminating in President Tinubu’s nationwide broadcast on October 1 (The Reporter).

The organizing committee brings together high-ranking officials including the Ministers of Finance and Coordinating Minister of the Economy, Information and National Orientation, Interior, Foreign Affairs, Federal Capital Territory, and Budget and Economic Planning, alongside the National Security Adviser and presidential advisers. Security and law enforcement heads, such as the Inspector General of Police, the Director General of the Department of State Services, and the Commander of the Guards Brigade, are also included (The Reporter).

Vice President Shettima to Lead UNGA Delegation

In international engagements, Vice President Kashim Shettima will represent President Tinubu and head Nigeria's delegation to the 81st Session of the United Nations General Assembly in New York (Punch). This directive follows speculation regarding the president's attendance (Punch), given that Tinubu departed Abuja on August 30 for a three-week European leave spanning London and France.

According to a statement issued by Stanley Nkwocha, Senior Special Assistant to the President on Media and Communications (Office of the Vice President), Shettima will lead a high-level team including Governor Siminalayi Fubara of Rivers State, Governor Dauda Lawal of Zamfara State, ministers, and other senior officials (Punch).

The General Debate runs from September 22 to 28, 2026, under the theme “Restoring trust, managing transformation: A United Nations that delivers for all” (Punch). During the sessions, Vice President Shettima is expected to:

  • Deliver Nigeria's national statement detailing the country's priorities on peace, security, economic growth, and international cooperation (Punch).
  • Participate in high-level discussions covering climate action and the Right to Development (Punch).
  • Engage in bilateral meetings with foreign leaders, international organizations, and development partners (Punch).

The Presidency noted that these diplomatic engagements will support President Tinubu's economic diplomacy, foster strategic partnerships, and advocate for reforms in the global financial architecture (Punch). Shettima is scheduled to return to Nigeria upon the conclusion of his engagements in New York (Punch).
