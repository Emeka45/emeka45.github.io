Federal Government Suspends 20 NSCDC Officers Over Custodial Deaths of 37 Miners in Niger State

The Federal Government has suspended 20 personnel of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps, alongside the Niger State Commandant, following the tragic deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners held in a detention cell. The development has triggered strong demands for accountability and a comprehensive inquiry from mining leadership.

The Federal Government has taken decisive administrative action by suspending 20 officers of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC), including the Niger State Commandant. This high-level suspension follows the troubling deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners who were kept inside an NSCDC detention facility in Niger State, as detailed by The Reporter.

Alongside the suspensions, authorities have established an investigative panel to uncover the circumstances surrounding the fatalities. The incident has drawn intense public scrutiny regarding the welfare and processing of detainees held by security agencies in connection with mineral resource operations.

Demands for Independent Investigation

In response to the tragedy, the leadership of the mining sector has stepped up pressure for transparency. According to reports from The Punch, the president of the National Artisanal Mining Cooperative Society has formally demanded an independent and thorough probe into how 37 individuals lost their lives while in state custody.

The association's call highlights growing concerns over custodial safety and the handling of artisanal and small-scale miners, who frequently find themselves targeted in government crackdowns against informal resource extraction.

Next Steps

With the investigative panel now set up and multiple personnel barred from active duty, attention shifts to the findings of the inquiry. Stakeholders across the security and mining sectors await official updates on accountability measures and institutional reforms aimed at preventing similar incidents in future detention operations.
