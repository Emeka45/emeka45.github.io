Federal Government Suspends 20 NSCDC Officers Over Custodial Deaths of 37 Miners in Niger State

The Federal Government has suspended 20 personnel of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps, alongside the Niger State Commandant, following the tragic deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners held in a detention cell. The development has triggered strong demands for accountability and a comprehensive inquiry from mining leadership.

The Federal Government has suspended 20 personnel of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC), alongside the Niger State Commandant, following the tragic deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners held in a detention cell. The developments have triggered strong demands for accountability and a comprehensive inquiry from mining leadership, as detailed in reports by The Reporter and The Punch.

Background to the Arrests and Custodial Incident

The sequence of events began when NSCDC mining marshals conducted raids on mining sites in the Wushishi-Lukoto area, located west of Minna, the Niger State capital. The operations, carried out on September 15 and 16, 2026, resulted in the arrest of scores of individuals accused of engaging in illegal mining activities. However, early in the morning on Thursday, September 17, 2026, 37 of the detained individuals were discovered dead inside an overcrowded NSCDC detention facility in Minna.

Government Response and Suspension of Officers

Following a directive from President Bola Tinubu for a comprehensive investigation, Minister of Interior Olubunmi Tunji Ojo announced sweeping administrative actions. A total of 20 NSCDC officers—encompassing personnel involved in station guard, arrest, investigation, intelligence, legal, and guard duties—were suspended. Additionally, Niger State Commandant Suberu Aniviye was suspended separately. The Minister directed all relevant officers to fully cooperate with investigators, preserve all records and material evidence, and warned that any obstruction or attempt to conceal evidence would be treated as a serious offense.

Establishment of the Independent Investigation Panel

To examine the tragedy, the Federal Government constituted a 10-member independent investigative committee. The panel is chaired by Jonathan Kure, a retired Department of State Services Deputy Director General, with Prof. Isa Hayatu Chiroma, former Director General of the Nigerian Law School, serving as secretary. Other notable members include retired Assistant Inspector General Hosea Hassan Karma, Prof. Olayinka Buhari, representatives from the Minna Emirate Council and the Niger State Government, Liman Sulaiman (National Secretary of the Miners Association of Nigeria), human rights lawyer Deji Adeyanju, journalist Zainab Suleiman Okino, and public affairs analyst George Agbakahi.

The committee is mandated to establish the identities of the deceased, investigate the circumstances surrounding their arrest and detention, determine the cause of death, ascertain any culpability, negligence, or misconduct, and recommend appropriate actions, including compensation where applicable. The panel has been granted a timeframe of two weeks to complete its assignment and submit its report.

Reactions from Mining Leadership and Stakeholders

The incident has drawn profound condemnation and sorrow from the mining sector. Abdullahi Dantasidi, National President of the National Artisanal and Small-Scale Mining Cooperative Society, expressed deep shock during an interview in Keffi Local Government Area, Nasarawa State. Emphasizing the vital contributions of artisanal miners to national economic development, Dantasidi lamented the loss of young lives and criticized the conditions under which the miners were held. He called upon the Federal Government to prioritize the security, welfare, and integration of artisanal operators, while also urging thorough investigations to bring any culpable parties to justice.
