Federal Government Suspends NSCDC Niger Commandant and 20 Officers Over 37 Custodial Deaths

The Federal Government has taken swift administrative action by suspending the Niger State Commandant and 20 personnel of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps following the tragic deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners held in their custody in Minna.

The Federal Government has taken swift administrative action by suspending the Niger State Commandant and 20 personnel of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps following the tragic deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners held in their custody in Minna.

Background and Arrests

The victims were among scores of individuals detained during security operations conducted on September 15 and 16 around the Lt Gen Mohammed Inuwa Wushishi Estate in Minna, the Niger State capital. The state commandant at the time, Suberu Aniviye, reported that scores of detained suspects were discovered dead in the early hours of Thursday, September 17, following a suspected outbreak of disease. However, the federal authorities have not yet announced a final cause of death, leaving the official determination to an ongoing inquiry.

Government Intervention and Suspensions

Following a directive from President Bola Tinubu for a comprehensive investigation, Minister of Interior Olubunmi Tunji Ojo announced the suspension of the Niger State Commandant alongside 20 other NSCDC officers. The suspended personnel include those involved in station guard, arrest, investigation, intelligence, legal, and general guard duties.

To ensure accountability, the Minister constituted a 10-member independent investigative committee. The panel is mandated to establish the identities of the deceased, investigate the circumstances of their arrest and detention, determine the root causes of the fatalities, assess issues of negligence or misconduct, and recommend appropriate actions including compensation and preventive measures.

Investigation Panel and Next Steps

The investigative committee is chaired by retired Department of State Services Deputy Director General Jonathan Kure, with former Nigerian Law School Director General Prof. Isa Hayatu Chiroma serving as secretary. Other prominent members include retired AIG Hosea Hassan Karma, Prof. Olayinka Buhari, representatives from the Minna Emirate Council and the Niger State Government, Miners Association of Nigeria National Secretary Liman Sulaiman, human rights lawyer Deji Adeyanju, journalist Zainab Suleiman Okino, and public affairs analyst George Agbakahi.

The panel has been granted a two-week timeframe to complete its assignment, visit relevant locations, review official documents, and submit its report to the Minister of Interior. Meanwhile, human rights organisations such as the International Human Rights Commission have begun compiling separate lists of the deceased using records from the General Hospital, Minna, as families come forward to report missing relatives.
