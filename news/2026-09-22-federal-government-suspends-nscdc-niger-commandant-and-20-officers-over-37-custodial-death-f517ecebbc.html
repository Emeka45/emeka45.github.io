Federal Government Suspends NSCDC Niger Commandant and 20 Officers Over 37 Custodial Deaths

The Federal Government has taken swift administrative action by suspending the Niger State Commandant and 20 personnel of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps following the tragic deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners held in their custody in Minna.

The Federal Government has suspended the Niger State Commandant of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC), Suberu Aniviye, along with 20 other personnel following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody. The swift administrative action comes in the wake of the tragedy in Minna, which has drawn widespread attention and prompted top-level intervention.

Background and Arrests

The victims were among scores of suspects apprehended during operations conducted on September 15 and 16, 2026, around the Lt Gen Mohammed Inuwa Wushishi Estate in Minna, the Niger State capital, as detailed by Punch Newspapers. Initially, Suberu Aniviye reported that scores of the detained suspects were found dead in the early hours following a suspected outbreak of disease. However, the exact cause of death remains unconfirmed pending further official review.

Government Intervention and Investigative Panel

Following a directive by President Bola Tinubu for a comprehensive inquiry, Minister of Interior Olubunmi Tunji Ojo announced the suspension of the 20 officers—who were involved in station guard, arrest, investigation, intelligence, legal, and guard duties—on Saturday, September 19, 2026, according to The Reporter.

The Minister also constituted a 10-member independent investigative committee chaired by retired Department of State Services Deputy Director General Jonathan Kure, with former Nigerian Law School Director General Prof. Isa Hayatu Chiroma serving as secretary. Other members include retired AIG Hosea Hassan Karma, Prof. Olayinka Buhari, representatives of the Minna Emirate Council and the Niger State Government, Miners Association of Nigeria National Secretary Liman Sulaiman, human rights lawyer Deji Adeyanju, journalist Zainab Suleiman Okino, and public affairs analyst George Agbakahi.

The committee is tasked with establishing the identities of the deceased, investigating the circumstances of their arrest and detention, determining the cause of death, assessing responsibility, complicity, negligence, or misconduct, and recommending appropriate actions, including compensation where applicable.

Identification and Next Steps

Efforts to identify all victims have progressed through human rights organizations and medical records. Awaisu Giwa, an International Peace Ambassador at the International Human Rights Commission, stated that an initial list was compiled from the records of the General Hospital, Minna, with additional names subsequently provided by relatives of missing victims. Families are utilizing the registry to report missing relatives, and the compiled names are expected to aid in facilitating family compensation.

The investigative panel has been given a two-week timeframe to complete its assignment and submit its report to the Minister of Interior. Meanwhile, authorities have warned against any interference, stating that attempts to conceal evidence, intimidate witnesses, or obstruct the probe will be treated as serious offences.
