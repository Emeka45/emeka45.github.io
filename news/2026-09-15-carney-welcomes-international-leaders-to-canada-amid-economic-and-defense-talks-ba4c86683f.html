Carney Welcomes International Leaders to Canada Amid Economic and Defense Talks

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney has hosted significant diplomatic and economic events, including welcoming Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy to discuss enhanced defense support and drone supplies.

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney has hosted significant diplomatic and economic events, including welcoming Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy to discuss enhanced defense support and drone supplies, as detailed by The Guardian. Alongside these security talks, the prime minister has pursued a major push to attract global investors amid rising economic and geopolitical uncertainty.

Economic Strategy and Investment Summit in Toronto

During a high-profile summit in Toronto convening more than 100 major investors representing over C$120tn of assets, Prime Minister Carney pitched Canada as a safe harbour "perfectly situated to build in the new global economic order." As reported by BBC News, the prime minister pledged to secure C$1tn ($720bn; £530bn) in investments over the next five years to reduce Canada's reliance on the United States. The summit ultimately generated nearly C$500bn in new investments across critical infrastructure, energy, defence, and digital technology.

To woo financiers, Ottawa offered more than 160 projects ranging from data centres to pipelines and port expansions. Officials dangled new tax incentives, including a mega deduction measure, and promised faster project approvals. Carney stated that Ottawa aims for a one-year review timeline to provide predictability for major proposed developments.

Infrastructure Changes and Domestic Pushback

Among the core announcements was a plan to permit private investment in four of Canada's main airports located in Toronto, Vancouver, Montreal, and Calgary. While the federal government will retain ownership of the underlying land and assets, the proposal aims to raise billions of dollars for transport infrastructure. However, the plan drew criticism from labour unions and advocacy groups. Lily Chang of the Canadian Labour Congress criticized the move during a trade war, while Nick Barry-Shaw of the Council of Canadians characterized the event as a "privatisaton summit." Demonstrators similarly protested outside the opening night gala, accusing the government of selling off public resources.

Artificial intelligence infrastructure also emerged as a major point of interest due to Canada's cold climate, vast space, and clean energy grid. Aidan Gomez, cofounder of AI firm Cohere, noted the country's capacity to build extensive data centre networks.

Trade Tensions and International Pivots

The economic push comes against the backdrop of strained relations with the United States. Following the sudden collapse of trade talks, both nations have traded words and imposed retaliatory tariffs. Despite new US tariffs coming into effect and additional bans on goods like alcohol and motorcycles pending, Carney emphasized that Canada remains a vital partner to its southern neighbor.

Looking beyond North America, Carney planned to travel to Europe to address the EU parliament and initiate discussions to build a unique security and economic alliance, signaling a broader diversification of trade toward Europe and Asia.
