Carney Welcomes International Leaders to Canada Amid Economic and Defense Talks

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney has hosted significant diplomatic and economic events, including welcoming Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy to discuss enhanced defense support and drone supplies, as detailed by The Guardian.

Economic Summit and Investment Push in Toronto

Prime Minister Mark Carney convened a major summit in Toronto, pitching Canada as a "safe harbour" for global investors amid ongoing geopolitical and economic uncertainty. Pitching to more than 100 major investors controlling over C$120tn in assets, the prime minister pledged to bring in C$1tn ($720bn; £530bn) in investments over the next five years to reduce Canada's heavy economic reliance on the United States. According to reporting from BBC News, the prime minister's office noted that the summit generated nearly C$500bn in new investments across critical sectors such as infrastructure, digital technology, energy, and defence.

During the gathering, Ottawa offered more than 160 projects for potential buyers—ranging from data centres and pipelines to port expansions. Officials dangled new tax incentives, including a "mega deduction" measure, and promised faster project approvals. Carney stated that the government aims to establish a one-year review timeline to offer predictability for major proposed developments.

Airport Privatisation Proposals and Public Pushback

Among the key announcements was a plan to permit private investment in four of Canada's major airports located in Toronto, Vancouver, Montreal, and Calgary. While the federal government would retain ownership of the underlying land and assets, private capital would be integrated into operations to raise billions of dollars for reinvestment in broader transport infrastructure.

The proposal drew sharp criticism from labour unions and advocacy groups. Representatives from the Canadian Labour Congress and the Council of Canadians argued that handing profitable public infrastructure over to private investors during a trade dispute amounts to selling off public resources.

Protestors similarly gathered outside the opening night gala at a downtown museum, accusing the government of organizing a "great Canadian sell-off" of public infrastructure to corporate interests.

Trade Tensions and International Alignment

The economic push comes against the backdrop of strained relations with the United States. Following the sudden collapse of trade talks, the two nations have engaged in tit-for-tat tariffs and verbal disputes. Despite new American tariffs coming into effect and additional bans anticipated, Carney emphasized that Canada will remain an important partner to the United States while simultaneously seeking deeper partnerships with Europe, Asia, and the Middle East.

As part of this broader international pivot, Carney is scheduled to travel to Europe to address the EU parliament and initiate discussions aimed at building a unique security and economic alliance with the bloc.
