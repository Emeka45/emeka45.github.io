Carney Welcomes International Leaders to Canada Amid Economic and Defense Talks

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney has hosted significant diplomatic and economic events, including welcoming Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy to discuss enhanced defense support and drone supplies.

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney has hosted significant diplomatic and economic events, including welcoming Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy to discuss enhanced defense support and drone supplies, as reported by The Guardian.

Economic Resilience and Global Investor Summit

Prime Minister Mark Carney has pitched Canada as a "safe harbour" for some of the world's largest investors amid a backdrop of geopolitical and economic uncertainty. Speaking to more than 100 major investors in Toronto, Carney emphasized that Canada is perfectly situated to build within a new global economic order. The prime minister has pledged to secure C$1tn ($720bn; £530bn) in investments over a five-year period as part of an effort to reduce the country's reliance on the United States.

During the summit, Ottawa presented more than 160 projects ranging from data centres to pipelines and port expansions. Officials also introduced new tax incentives, including a "mega deduction" measure, alongside commitments to accelerate project approvals. Carney stated that the government aims to establish a predictable one-year review timeline for major proposals.

Airport Privatisation Proposals

A central announcement from the summit involved plans to permit private investment in four major Canadian airports located in Toronto, Vancouver, Montreal, and Calgary. While the federal government would retain ownership of the underlying land and assets, the proposal aims to generate billions of dollars for reinvestment into broader transport infrastructure, including regional facilities.

The plan has drawn criticism from labor unions and advocacy groups. Lily Chang of the Canadian Labour Congress argued that transferring profitable public infrastructure to private investors during a trade dispute is counterproductive, while Nick Barry-Shaw of the Council of Canadians characterized the initiative as a country-selling event.

AI Infrastructure and US Relations

Artificial intelligence infrastructure emerged as a major focal point, driven by Canada's cold climate, spatial availability, and clean energy grid. According to the prime minister's office, the summit generated nearly C$500bn in new investments across critical infrastructure, digital technology, energy, and defense sectors.

The economic push coincides with ongoing trade tensions with the United States following the sudden collapse of trade talks. Tit-for-tat tariffs have been enacted, and additional restrictions are anticipated. Despite these tensions, Carney addressed American executives directly in Toronto, affirming that Canada remains committed to its longstanding partnership and deep historical ties with the US.

Future Steps and European Pivot

Following the domestic summits, Carney is scheduled to travel to Europe to address the European Parliament and initiate discussions aimed at establishing a unique security and economic alliance with the European Union. Economic analysts and banking executives note that while Canada's geographic proximity ensures a continued alignment with the US, exploring trade diversification across Europe and Asia represents a crucial strategic expansion.
