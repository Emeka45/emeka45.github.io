Carney Welcomes International Leaders to Canada Amid Economic and Defense Talks

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney has hosted significant diplomatic and economic events, including welcoming Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy to discuss enhanced defense support and drone supplies.

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney has hosted significant diplomatic and economic events, including welcoming Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy to discuss enhanced defense support and drone supplies, as detailed by The Guardian. Alongside security talks concerning Ukraine, Carney has focused heavily on shifting Canada's economic posture amidst geopolitical tensions, international trade disputes, and ongoing friction with the United States.

Attracting Global Investment in Toronto

As reported by BBC News, Prime Minister Carney convened a major summit in Toronto to pitch Canada as a secure global hub for wealthy investors and major financiers representing over C$120tn in assets. Carney pledged to bring in C$1tn ($720bn; £530bn) in investments over the subsequent five years to reduce Canada's traditional economic reliance on the US. During the summit, the prime minister's office noted that nearly C$500bn in new investments were generated across critical infrastructure, energy, defence, and digital technology.

To lure international buyers, Ottawa presented more than 160 projects ranging from data centres and port expansions to pipelines, alongside new tax incentives like a "mega deduction" measure. Furthermore, Carney promised greater regulatory predictability, targeting a one-year review timeline for major proposed projects. Artificial intelligence infrastructure proved particularly attractive to investors, aided by Canada's cold climate, expansive space, and clean energy grid, according to Cohere cofounder Aidan Gomez.

Infrastructure Plans and Domestic Criticisms

A notable announcement emerging from the summit was Carney's proposal to permit private investment in the operations of four primary Canadian airports located in Toronto, Vancouver, Montreal, and Calgary, while the federal government retains ownership of the underlying land and assets. The plan aims to raise billions of dollars to reinvest in transport infrastructure.

However, these initiatives drew sharp pushback from labor unions and advocacy groups. Representatives from the Canadian Labour Congress and the Council of Canadians criticized the measures during protests outside a downtown museum hosting the opening night gala, arguing that handing profitable public infrastructure over to private investors during a trade dispute amounts to selling off the country rather than nation-building.

Navigating US Relations and European Pivots

The economic push comes against the backdrop of a deteriorating trade relationship with the United States. Following the sudden collapse of trade talks, the two nations have engaged in tit-for-tat tariffs and verbal sparring, with new US tariffs taking effect while further bans loom. Despite the friction, Carney reached out directly to American executives during the Toronto summit, emphasizing that Canada remains a vital neighbor and partner.

Looking beyond North America, Carney's administration is actively pursuing deeper international partnerships. Following the domestic economic summits, the prime minister planned to travel to Europe to address the EU parliament and initiate discussions aimed at building a unique security and economic alliance with the bloc.
