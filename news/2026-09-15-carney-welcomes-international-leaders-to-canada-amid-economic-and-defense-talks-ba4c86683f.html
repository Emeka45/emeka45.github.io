Carney Welcomes International Leaders to Canada Amid Economic and Defense Talks

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney has hosted significant diplomatic and economic events, including welcoming Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy to discuss enhanced defense support and drone supplies.

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney has hosted high-level diplomatic and economic events, including welcoming international leaders like Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy to discuss enhanced defense support and drone supplies, as reported by The Guardian.

Economic Strategy and Investor Summit

Amid geopolitical and economic uncertainty, Prime Minister Carney convened a major summit in Toronto to pitch Canada as a "safe harbour" for global investors. Carney told more than 100 major investors that Canada is ideally positioned for the changing global economic order, pledging to attract C$1tn ($720bn; £530bn) in investments over the next five years. The strategy aims to reduce the country's reliance on the United States following a breakdown in trade talks and the implementation of tit-for-tat tariffs.

The prime minister's office noted that the summit generated nearly C$500bn in new investments across critical infrastructure, digital technology, energy, and defense sectors. Over 160 projects were showcased, ranging from data centres and pipelines to port expansions. Ottawa also introduced new tax incentives, such as a "mega deduction," and promised faster project approvals with a one-year review timeline to provide greater predictability.

Infrastructure Changes and Public Debate

A notable announcement from the summit included a plan to permit private investment in four of Canada's main airports located in Toronto, Vancouver, Montreal, and Calgary. While the federal government will retain ownership of the underlying land and assets, the move drew criticism from labor unions and advocacy groups. Critics, including representatives from the Canadian Labour Congress and the Council of Canadians, argued that handing profitable public infrastructure to private entities during a trade dispute is detrimental to workers and passengers.

Additionally, Canada's cold climate, vast space, and clean energy grid drew strong interest for artificial intelligence infrastructure, such as data centres, according to industry leaders.

US Relations and European Pivot

Despite ongoing trade disputes and new US tariffs taking effect, Carney emphasized that Canada and the United States remain close neighbors and vital partners. At the same time, the Canadian government is actively pursuing deeper economic and security ties with Europe and Asia. Carney's itinerary includes traveling to Europe to address the EU parliament and initiate discussions to build a unique security and economic alliance, diversifying trade relationships beyond North America.
