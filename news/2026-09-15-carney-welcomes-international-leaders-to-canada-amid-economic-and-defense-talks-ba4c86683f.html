Carney Welcomes International Leaders to Canada Amid Economic and Defense Talks

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney has hosted significant diplomatic and economic events, including welcoming Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy to discuss enhanced defense support and drone supplies, as reported by The Guardian.

Economic Strategy and Investor Summit in Toronto

Amid geopolitical and economic uncertainty, Prime Minister Mark Carney has pitched Canada as a "safe harbour" for the world's largest investors. Convening a major summit in Toronto with more than 100 major investors representing over C$120tn of assets, Carney pledged to bring in C$1tn ($720bn; £530bn) in investments over the next five years. The strategy seeks to reduce Canada's reliance on the US following collapsed trade talks and subsequent tit-for-tat tariffs.

During the summit, Ottawa offered more than 160 projects ranging from data centres and port expansions to pipelines. Carney also introduced new tax incentives, such as a "mega deduction" measure, and promised faster project approvals with a targeted one-year review timeline. According to the prime minister's office, the summit generated nearly C$500bn in new investments across critical infrastructure, digital technology, energy, and defence sectors, with particular interest in AI infrastructure due to Canada's cold climate, space, and clean energy grid.

Airport Privatisation and Domestic Criticism

A notable announcement from the summit was a plan to allow private investment in four of Canada's main airports in Toronto, Vancouver, Montreal, and Calgary. While the federal government would retain ownership of the underlying land and assets, the proposal aims to raise billions to reinvest in transport infrastructure. However, labour unions and advocacy groups strongly criticized the move. Lily Chang of the Canadian Labour Congress called it the wrong move during a trade war, while Nick Barry-Shaw of the Council of Canadians labeled the gathering a "privatisation summit." Hundreds of protesters also gathered outside the opening night gala at a downtown museum to condemn the administration's economic policies.

US Relations and European Pivot

Despite ongoing trade disputes and new US tariffs coming into effect, Carney emphasized that Canada remains committed to being the US's most important partner in many key areas. At the same time, Carney is heading to Europe to address the EU parliament and initiate discussions to build a unique security and economic alliance, signaling a broader diversification of trade toward Europe and Asia.
