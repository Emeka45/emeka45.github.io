Carney Welcomes International Leaders to Canada Amid Economic and Defense Talks

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney has hosted significant diplomatic and economic events, including welcoming Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy to discuss enhanced defense support and drone supplies.

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney has been actively hosting major diplomatic and economic events, including welcoming Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy to discuss enhanced defense support and drone supplies, as reported by The Guardian.

Economic Summit and Investment Push in Toronto

Building on his diplomatic engagements, Prime Minister Mark Carney hosted a high-profile summit in Toronto to pitch Canada as a secure global economic hub amid ongoing international uncertainty. Speaking to more than 100 major investors representing over C$120tn in assets, Carney pledged to attract C$1tn ($720bn; £530bn) in investments over the next five years. The strategy aims to reduce Canada's traditional economic reliance on the United States as trade disputes persist.

During the summit, Ottawa presented more than 160 projects spanning data centres, pipelines, and port expansions. To draw buyers, the government introduced new tax incentives, including a "mega deduction" measure, and promised faster project approvals with a targeted one-year review timeline to offer greater regulatory predictability.

Infrastructure, AI, and Privatisation Debates

The Prime Minister's office noted that the summit generated nearly C$500bn in new investments across critical infrastructure, digital technology, energy, and defense sectors. Artificial intelligence infrastructure drew significant interest due to Canada's cold climate, spatial capacity, and clean energy grid.

A major announcement from the summit included a plan to permit private investment in the operations of four major Canadian airports in Toronto, Vancouver, Montreal, and Calgary, while the federal government retains ownership of the underlying land. The proposal faced criticism from labor unions and advocacy groups, with representatives from the Canadian Labour Congress and the Council of Canadians arguing that handing public infrastructure over to private investors during a trade dispute is counterproductive.

US Relations and European Pivot

Amid a backdrop of collapsed trade talks, tit-for-tat tariffs, and a war of words with the United States, Carney addressed American executives directly in Toronto, emphasizing that Canada remains a steadfast neighbor and vital partner. Concurrently, hundreds of protestors gathered outside the opening night gala to voice concerns over foreign ownership of Canadian resources.

Following the domestic summit, Carney prepared to travel to Europe to address the EU parliament and initiate discussions aimed at building a unique security and economic alliance with the bloc, diversifying Canada's trade partnerships overseas.
