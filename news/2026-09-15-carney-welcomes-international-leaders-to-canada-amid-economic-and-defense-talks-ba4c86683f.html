Carney Welcomes International Leaders to Canada Amid Economic and Defense Talks

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney has hosted significant diplomatic and economic events, including welcoming Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy to discuss enhanced defense support and drone supplies.

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney has hosted significant diplomatic and economic events, welcoming international leaders while navigating major trade shifts. Amid ongoing economic and defense talks, the prime minister convened a major investor summit in Toronto to position Canada as a "safe harbour" for global capital amid geopolitical and economic uncertainty, as detailed by BBC News.

Economic Strategy and Investment Push

During the summit with more than 100 major investors controlling over C$120tn in assets, Prime Minister Carney pledged to secure C$1tn ($720bn; £530bn) in investments over the next five years. The push aims to reduce Canada's reliance on the United States following a collapse in trade talks and the implementation of tit-for-tat tariffs. More than 160 projects were offered to financiers, spanning data centres, pipelines, port expansions, and artificial intelligence infrastructure. Aidan Gomez, cofounder of AI firm Cohere, noted that Canada's cold climate, spatial capacity, and clean energy grid make it attractive for AI infrastructure projects like data centres.

To accelerate development, Ottawa introduced new tax incentives, including a "mega deduction" measure, alongside a targeted one-year review timeline to provide project predictability. Additionally, Carney announced a plan to allow private investment in operations across four major Canadian airports in Toronto, Vancouver, Montreal, and Calgary, while the federal government retains ownership of the underlying land and assets.

Domestic Criticism and International Outreach

The economic strategy drew sharp pushback domestically. Labour unions and advocacy groups criticized the initiatives during protests outside the opening night gala at a downtown museum. Lily Chang of the Canadian Labour Congress condemned the move to hand profitable public infrastructure over to private investors during a trade dispute, while Nick Barry-Shaw of the Council of Canadians characterized the event as a "privatisation summit."

Despite trade tensions with the United States—highlighted by new US tariffs taking effect and upcoming bans on goods like alcohol and motorcycles—Carney emphasized that Canada remains committed to being the US's most important partner in many key areas. Meanwhile, the prime minister is scheduled to head to Europe next to address the EU parliament and pursue discussions to build a unique security and economic alliance, building on diplomatic engagements that also recently included hosting Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy to discuss enhanced defense support and drone supplies, as reported by The Guardian.
