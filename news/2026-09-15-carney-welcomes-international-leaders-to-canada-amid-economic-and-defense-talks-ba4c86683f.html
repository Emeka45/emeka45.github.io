Carney Welcomes International Leaders to Canada Amid Economic and Defense Talks

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney has hosted significant diplomatic and economic events, including welcoming Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy to discuss enhanced defense support and drone supplies.

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney has hosted significant diplomatic and economic events, including welcoming Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy to discuss enhanced defense support and drone supplies, as detailed by The Guardian.

Wooing Global Investors Amid Economic Shifts

Prime Minister Mark Carney has pitched Canada as a "safe harbour" for major international investors in an era defined by economic and geopolitical uncertainty. Speaking to more than 100 prominent investors in Toronto, Carney pledged to bring in C$1tn ($720bn; £530bn) in investments over a five-year period to reduce the country's reliance on the United States. The high-stakes summit, which featured financiers representing over C$120tn of assets, offered more than 160 projects ranging from data centres to pipelines and port expansions.

To streamline development, Ottawa introduced new tax incentives—including a "mega deduction" measure—and promised faster approvals with a target one-year review timeline for major proposed projects. The prime minister's office noted that the summit generated nearly C$500bn in new investments across critical infrastructure, digital technology, energy, and defense.

Airport Privatisation and Domestic Pushback

Among the major announcements was a plan to permit private investment in four of Canada's primary airports located in Toronto, Vancouver, Montreal, and Calgary. While the federal government will retain ownership of the underlying land and assets, private capital will be allowed into operations to raise funds for transport infrastructure. However, the proposal faced sharp criticism from labour unions and advocacy groups. Lily Chang of the Canadian Labour Congress condemned the move as handing profitable public infrastructure over to private investors during a trade war, while Nick Barry-Shaw of the Council of Canadians labelled the gathering a "privatisation summit."

Protesters also gathered outside the opening night gala at a downtown museum, accusing the government of organizing a "great Canadian sell-off" of public resources to corporate interests, with some noting that a substantial portion of the invited buyers were American.

Navigating US Relations and the European Pivot

The economic push comes against the backdrop of strained relations with the United States. Following the sudden collapse of trade talks, the two nations have engaged in tit-for-tat tariffs and verbal disputes, with new US tariffs taking effect and further bans anticipated. Despite these tensions, Carney emphasized that Canada remains a vital partner to its southern neighbor.

Looking outward, Carney prepared to travel to Europe to address the EU parliament and initiate discussions to build a unique security and economic alliance, highlighting a broader strategy to diversify trade toward Europe and Asia as reported by BBC News.
