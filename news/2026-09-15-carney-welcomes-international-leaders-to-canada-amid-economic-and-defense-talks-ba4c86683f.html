Carney Welcomes International Leaders to Canada Amid Economic and Defense Talks

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney has hosted significant diplomatic and economic events, including welcoming Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy to discuss enhanced defense support and drone supplies.

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney has hosted significant diplomatic and economic events, including welcoming Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy to discuss enhanced defense support and drone supplies, as reported by The Guardian. Amid an era of geopolitical and economic uncertainty, Carney has actively positioned Canada as a "safe harbour" for global investors and international partners.

Attracting Global Investment in Toronto

During a major economic summit in Toronto, Carney addressed more than 100 major investors representing over C$120tn in assets, emphasizing that Canada is "perfectly situated to build in the new global economic order." The prime minister pledged to attract C$1tn ($720bn; £530bn) in investments over the next five years to reduce the country's reliance on the United States. The prime minister's office noted that the summit generated nearly C$500bn in new investments across critical sectors such as critical infrastructure, digital technology, energy, and defense.

To woo financiers, Ottawa offered more than 160 projects ranging from data centres to pipelines and port expansions. Additionally, the government dangled new tax incentives, such as a "mega deduction" measure, and promised faster project approvals. Carney stated that Ottawa will target a one-year review timeline to provide predictability for major proposed projects.

Airport Privatization and Domestic Pushback

Among the key announcements from the summit was a plan to allow private investment in four of Canada's main airports in Toronto, Vancouver, Montreal, and Calgary. While the federal government would retain ownership of the underlying land and assets, the proposal aims to raise billions of dollars to reinvest in transport infrastructure, including smaller regional airports.

The plan drew sharp criticism from labor unions and advocacy groups. Lily Chang of the Canadian Labour Congress criticized the move during a trade dispute, stating that handing public infrastructure over to private investors is the wrong move. Protesters outside the summit's opening night gala similarly accused the government of organizing a "great Canadian sell-off" of public resources.

AI Infrastructure and Trade Tensions

Canada has also drawn substantial interest for its AI infrastructure capabilities, bolstered by its cold climate, open space, and clean energy grid suitable for data centres, according to Cohere cofounder Aidan Gomez. These economic pushes occur against a backdrop of trade friction with the United States. Following the sudden collapse of trade talks, the two nations have engaged in tit-for-tat tariffs and a war of words, with new US tariffs taking effect and further bans looming.

Despite these tensions, Carney emphasized that Canada remains a vital partner to the US. Meanwhile, as detailed by BBC News, Carney is also looking outward, preparing to head to Europe to address the EU parliament and initiate discussions to build a unique security and economic alliance with the bloc.
