Carney Welcomes International Leaders to Canada Amid Economic and Defense Talks

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney has hosted significant diplomatic and economic events, including welcoming Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy to discuss enhanced defense support and drone supplies.

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney has hosted significant diplomatic and economic events, welcoming international leaders including Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy to discuss enhanced defense support and drone supplies, as reported by The Guardian.

Economic Summit and Investment Push in Toronto

Amid geopolitical and economic uncertainty, Prime Minister Carney convened a major investment summit in Toronto to pitch Canada as a "safe harbour" for the world's richest investors. Carney told more than 100 major investors that Canada is ideally positioned to build within the new global economic order. The prime minister has pledged to secure C$1tn ($720bn; £530bn) in investments over a five-year period to reduce Canada's reliance on the United States. According to BBC News, the summit generated nearly C$500bn in new investments across sectors such as critical infrastructure, digital technology, energy, and defense.

During the event, Ottawa offered more than 160 projects ranging from data centres to pipelines and port expansions. Additionally, the government dangled new tax incentives, such as a "mega deduction" measure, and promised faster project approvals with a targeted one-year review timeline to bring greater predictability.

Infrastructure Plans and Domestic Pushback

A central announcement from the summit involved Carney's plan to permit private investment in four of Canada's major airports located in Toronto, Vancouver, Montreal, and Calgary. Under the proposal, the federal government would retain ownership of the underlying land and assets while allowing private capital into airport operations to help fund broader transport infrastructure.

The privatization proposals drew criticism from labor unions and advocacy groups. Lily Chang of the Canadian Labour Congress argued that handing profitable public infrastructure to private investors during a trade dispute is counterproductive, while critics from groups like the Council of Canadians and Dogwood accused the administration of selling off public resources and infrastructure to corporate interests.

US Trade Tensions and the European Pivot

The economic push comes against a backdrop of deteriorating trade relations with the United States. Following the sudden collapse of trade talks, tit-for-tat tariffs and new trade restrictions have been implemented by both nations, with no formal talks expected to resume immediately. Despite the tensions, Carney emphasized that Canada will remain an important partner to the United States.

Following the domestic summit, Carney prepared to travel to Europe to address the EU parliament and initiate discussions aimed at building a unique security and economic alliance with the bloc, as Canada seeks to diversify its trade and economic partnerships.
