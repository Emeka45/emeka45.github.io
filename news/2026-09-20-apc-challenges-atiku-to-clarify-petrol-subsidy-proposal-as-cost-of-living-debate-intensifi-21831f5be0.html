APC Challenges Atiku to Clarify Petrol Subsidy Proposal as Cost of Living Debate Intensifies

The All Progressives Congress has challenged former Vice President Atiku Abubakar to provide a detailed fiscal and legal explanation for his proposed production subsidy aimed at lowering petrol prices, following Atiku's public call for President Bola Tinubu to adopt his intervention strategy.

The All Progressives Congress Presidential Campaign Council has challenged former Vice President Atiku Abubakar to provide a detailed fiscal and legal explanation for his proposed production subsidy aimed at lowering petrol prices. The demand follows Atiku's public call for President Bola Tinubu to adopt his intervention strategy to ease the cost of living.

Calls for Intervention and Government Response

In an address regarding fuel prices, palliatives, and the cost of living, Atiku urged President Tinubu to reduce petrol and diesel prices by adopting a production subsidy for locally refined petroleum products. As reported by The Reporter, Atiku stated that the administration could even rename the policy and take credit for it if it would help alleviate the economic burden on citizens. He argued that the impact of high fuel costs had extended into transportation, food, manufacturing, and general household spending.

In response, the APC-PCC issued a statement on Sunday, September 20, 2026, questioning the legal, fiscal, and practical basis of the proposal as detailed by Punch Newspapers. Council spokesman Dele Alake highlighted Section 205(1) of the Petroleum Industry Act 2021, which dictates that wholesale and retail prices of petroleum products must rely on unrestricted free-market pricing conditions. The Nigerian Midstream and Downstream Petroleum Regulatory Authority also noted that it does not fix pump prices or issue administrative pricing templates unless statutory conditions for intervention are met, confirming that no market failure has been declared.

Fiscal Concerns and Policy Contradictions

The APC-PCC challenged Atiku to clarify whether refiners receiving the subsidy would be bound to sell at prescribed prices, and if so, how that would operate under the PIA without creating an unenforceable mechanism where consumers still pay market rates. Furthermore, the council estimated that based on current refinery throughput and domestic supply figures, the proposed subsidy could cost between N17 trillion and N21 trillion annually depending on the discount size and volume coverage.

Alake also contrasted Atiku's current stance with his previous support for downstream deregulation, recalling a November 2022 speech at the Lagos Business School where Atiku described the old petrol subsidy as fraudulent and pledged to complete its removal. The APC pointed to Atiku's August 25, 2026 post on X where he stated he would restore the subsidy in another form, pressing him to explain how his new framework would prevent past issues of smuggling, scarcity, and fiscal losses.

Administration Alternatives and Next Steps

Contrasting with Atiku's proposition, the ruling party emphasized the Tinubu administration's focus on compressed natural gas and electric mass transit alternatives to lower transportation costs. According to the APC-PCC, over 120,000 vehicles have been converted to CNG, contributing to reduced fares across various states including Borno, Niger, Kaduna, Adamawa, and Abia. The administration maintains that the deregulated market has successfully encouraged domestic refining investments, pointing to the Dangote Petroleum Refinery operating at high capacity.

The APC-PCC concluded by urging Atiku to publish a comprehensive policy document and independent legal analysis. Until such details are provided, the council dismissed the production-subsidy plan as an uncosted promise lacking a clear operational framework.
