APC Challenges Atiku to Clarify Petrol Subsidy Proposal as Cost of Living Debate Intensifies

The All Progressives Congress has challenged former Vice President Atiku Abubakar to provide a detailed fiscal and legal explanation for his proposed production subsidy aimed at lowering petrol prices, following Atiku's public call for President Bola Tinubu to adopt his intervention strategy.

The All Progressives Congress Presidential Campaign Council has challenged former Vice President Atiku Abubakar to provide a comprehensive legal, fiscal, and operational breakdown of his proposed production subsidy for locally refined petrol (Punch Newspapers). The political sparring follows Atiku's public appeal to President Bola Tinubu to adopt his intervention strategy as a means to lower pump prices and ease broader living costs (The Reporter).

The Subsidy Proposal and Public Debate

Atiku recently urged the federal administration to reconsider its approach to fuel pricing, suggesting that the government could even rename his intervention strategy and take credit for it if doing so would alleviate economic pressures on citizens (The Reporter). His plan entails a transparent production subsidy targeted exclusively at petroleum products refined domestically and sold within the country, backed by a fixed annual spending ceiling, National Assembly approval, and independent audits.

However, the APC-PCC, through its spokesman Dele Alake, contended that the proposal leaves vital operational questions unanswered (Punch Newspapers). Alake questioned whether refineries receiving public support would be bound to sell at fixed rates, and how such price controls could legally operate alongside the provisions of the Petroleum Industry Act 2021.

Legal, Regulatory, and Financial Concerns

Citing Section 205(1) of the PIA, the APC-PCC emphasized that wholesale and retail petroleum prices are meant to rely on unrestricted free-market conditions (Punch Newspapers). The Nigerian Midstream and Downstream Petroleum Regulatory Authority further noted that it does not routinely fix pump prices or issue administrative pricing templates unless statutory market failure conditions are formally declared.

The ruling party's campaign council also highlighted potential fiscal implications, estimating that discounting crude for domestic refiners could cost between N17 trillion and N21 trillion annually, which would directly diminish revenues accruing to federal, state, and local governments (Punch Newspapers). Alake challenged Atiku to clarify funding sources, subsidy rates, and specific safeguards against smuggling and product diversion.

Shifting Positions and Alternative Administration Policies

The APC-PCC drew attention to Atiku's past support for complete downstream deregulation, recalling statements he made at the Lagos Business School in November 2022 labeling the old subsidy regime fraudulent, alongside a social media post in August 2026 (Punch Newspapers). The council questioned why the former vice president now advocates returning to a subsidy format after previously championing reform frameworks.

In contrast to Atiku's proposal, the federal government has continued to promote alternative palliatives aimed at reducing transportation expenses, such as compressed natural gas vehicle conversions and electric mass transit options (Punch Newspapers). While acknowledging ongoing cost-of-living pressures driven in part by international crude market fluctuations, the APC maintains that maintaining a deregulated downstream sector remains essential for encouraging domestic refining investments, such as those at the Dangote Petroleum Refinery (Punch Newspapers).
