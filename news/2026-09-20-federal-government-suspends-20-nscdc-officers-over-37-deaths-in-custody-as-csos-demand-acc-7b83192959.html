Federal Government Suspends 20 NSCDC Officers Over 37 Deaths in Custody as CSOs Demand Accountability

The Federal Government has suspended 20 Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps officers and the Niger State Commandant following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody, prompting civil society organisations to demand further investigations and the identity of the mining site owner.

The Federal Government has suspended 20 officers of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC), alongside the Niger State Commandant, following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in the Corps' custody. The tragic incident occurred on Thursday, September 17, 2026, prompting widespread national reactions and demands for accountability from civil society organisations (The Reporter).

Government Action and Independent Investigation Panel

In response to the deaths, Minister of Interior Olubunmi Tunji Ojo announced the suspensions on Saturday following a directive from President Bola Tinubu for a comprehensive investigation. The suspended personnel include the Niger State Commandant, Suberu Aniviye, as well as 20 other officers involved in station guard, arrest, investigation, intelligence, legal, and guard duties.

To ensure transparency, the Federal Government constituted a 10-member independent investigative committee chaired by Jonathan Kure, a retired Department of State Services Deputy Director General, with Prof. Isa Hayatu Chiroma serving as secretary. Other prominent figures on the panel include retired AIG Hosea Hassan Karma, Prof. Olayinka Buhari, representatives from the Minna Emirate Council and the Niger State Government, Miners Association of Nigeria National Secretary Liman Sulaiman, human rights lawyer Deji Adeyanju, journalist Zainab Suleiman Okino, and public affairs analyst George Agbakahi.

The committee has been mandated to establish the identities of the deceased victims, investigate the circumstances of their arrest and detention, determine the cause of death, and identify any negligence or misconduct. The panel has two weeks to submit its report and recommend appropriate actions, including potential compensation (The Reporter).

Civil Society Demands and Controversy Over Site Ownership

The incident has intensified calls from civil society organisations (The Punch) for a broader inquiry into illegal mining operations in Niger State. Debo Adeniran, Executive Director of the Centre for Anti-Corruption and Open Leadership, urged authorities to prosecute both the site owners and the monitors who failed in their oversight duties. Anthony Adejuwon, Team Lead of Urban Alert, also emphasized the need to trace the true ownership of the mining site and ensure accountability under the Mining Act.

Controversy arose when a survivor, Malam Babawo Tudun Nasera, alleged that the gold-mining site belonged to former military Head of State, General Ibrahim Babangida (retd.). However, Babangida's aide, Dr. Danladi Abdulhameed, firmly rejected the claim, issuing a statement describing the allegation as false, baseless, and entirely without foundation (The Punch).

Meanwhile, the Socio-Economic Rights and Accountability Project (SERAP) petitioned United Nations Special Rapporteurs to intervene, demanding full accountability for the 37 lives lost in custody and any excessive use of force against protesters in Minna (The Punch).
