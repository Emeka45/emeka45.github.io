Federal Government Suspends 20 NSCDC Officers Over 37 Deaths in Custody as CSOs Demand Accountability

The Federal Government has suspended 20 Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps officers and the Niger State Commandant following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody, prompting civil society organisations to demand further investigations and the identity of the mining site owner.

The Federal Government has suspended 20 officers of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC) and the Niger State Commandant following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody (FG Suspends 20 NSCDC Officers, Sets Up Panel Over 37 Deaths In Custody). The incident, which occurred on Thursday, September 17, 2026, has sparked widespread calls for accountability from civil society organisations and international bodies.

Government Action and Investigation Panel

Minister of Interior Olubunmi Tunji Ojo announced the suspension of the personnel—including those involved in station guard, arrest, investigation, intelligence, legal, and guard duties, alongside Niger State Commandant Suberu Aniviye—following a directive by President Bola Tinubu for a comprehensive investigation (FG Suspends 20 NSCDC Officers, Sets Up Panel Over 37 Deaths In Custody). A 10-member independent investigative committee has been constituted, chaired by retired Department of State Services Deputy Director General Jonathan Kure, with former Nigerian Law School Director General Prof. Isa Hayatu Chiroma serving as secretary.

The committee's mandate includes identifying the deceased, examining the circumstances of their arrest, detention, and subsequent deaths, determining negligence or complicity, and recommending appropriate actions and compensation. The panel has been given two weeks to submit its report (FG Suspends 20 NSCDC Officers, Sets Up Panel Over 37 Deaths In Custody).

Civil Society Demands Accountability and Site Ownership Clarification

In the wake of the tragedy, civil society organisations have demanded that investigations look beyond the immediate custody conditions to identify and prosecute the owners of the illegal mining site (Niger tragedy: CSOs demand site owner’s identity). Controversy emerged after a survivor claimed the gold-mining site belonged to former military Head of State General Ibrahim Babangida (retd.), an allegation promptly denied by Babangida's aide, Dr. Danladi Abdulhameed, who dismissed the connection as completely false and baseless.

Groups such as the Centre for Anti-Corruption and Open Leadership, represented by Debo Adeniran, and Urban Alert, led by Anthony Adejuwon, stressed that illegal miners and economic saboteurs must face full legal consequences, and that licensed corporate entities violating regulations under the Mining Act must be held accountable (Niger tragedy: CSOs demand site owner’s identity). Meanwhile, the Socio-Economic Rights and Accountability Project (SERAP) has appealed to United Nations Special Rapporteurs to intervene, demanding full transparency regarding the deaths in custody and reported use of force in Minna (Niger tragedy: CSOs demand site owner’s identity).
