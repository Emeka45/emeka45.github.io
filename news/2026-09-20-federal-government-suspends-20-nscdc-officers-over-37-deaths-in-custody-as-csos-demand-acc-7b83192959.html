Federal Government Suspends 20 NSCDC Officers Over 37 Deaths in Custody as CSOs Demand Accountability

The Federal Government has suspended 20 Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps officers and the Niger State Commandant following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody, prompting civil society organisations to demand further investigations and the identity of the mining site owner.

The Federal Government has suspended 20 officers of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC) and the Niger State Commandant, Suberu Aniviye, following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in the Corps’ custody. Minister of Interior Olubunmi Tunji Ojo announced the suspensions on Saturday alongside the formation of a 10-member independent investigative committee following a directive from President Bola Tinubu.

Details of the Custodial Incident and Investigation

The tragic incident occurred on Thursday, September 17, 2026. The newly constituted independent panel has been mandated to establish the identities of the deceased individuals, examine the circumstances surrounding their arrest and detention, and determine the exact cause of death. The committee is also tasked with identifying any culpability, negligence, or misconduct among personnel, and recommending appropriate actions, including compensation where applicable.

The suspended personnel include officers involved in station guard, arrest, investigation, intelligence, legal, and guard duties. Minister Tunji Ojo warned that any attempts to conceal evidence, intimidate witnesses, or obstruct the probe would be treated as a serious offense, directing all relevant authorities to fully cooperate with the panel.

The investigative committee is chaired by Jonathan Kure, a retired Deputy Director General of the Department of State Services, with former Nigerian Law School Director General Prof. Isa Hayatu Chiroma serving as secretary. Other members include retired AIG Hosea Hassan Karma, Prof. Olayinka Buhari, representatives of the Minna Emirate Council and the Niger State Government, Liman Sulaiman of the Miners Association of Nigeria, human rights lawyer Deji Adeyanju, journalist Zainab Suleiman Okino, and public affairs analyst George Agbakahi. The panel has been given two weeks to complete its assignment and submit its report.

Civil Society and Stakeholder Demands

In the wake of the tragedy, civil society organisations have pressed for broader accountability, demanding that investigations extend beyond the detention facility to uncover the ownership of the illegal mining site. Executive Director of the Centre for Anti-Corruption and Open Leadership, Debo Adeniran, asserted that individuals engaging in illegal mining are economic saboteurs and called for the arrest and prosecution of site owners alongside negligent monitoring officials. Similarly, Anthony Adejuwon, Team Lead at Urban Alert, emphasized that investigators must identify those behind the site's operations and hold licensed corporate entities accountable for regulatory violations under the Mining Act.

The demands for clarity followed reports quoting a survivor, Malam Babawo Tudun Nasera, who alleged that the gold-mining site belonged to former military Head of State General Ibrahim Babangida (retd.). However, Dr. Danladi Abdulhameed, an aide to the former leader, issued a statement strongly denying any connection, describing the claims as false and baseless. An NSCDC spokesperson noted that the corps did not know the actual owner of the land, while a security source indicated that unregulated mineral extraction by powerful individuals remains a significant driver of insecurity and banditry in the region.

International Appeals and Next Steps

Meanwhile, the Socio-Economic Rights and Accountability Project (SERAP), through its Deputy Director Kolawole Oluwadare, has appealed to United Nations Special Rapporteurs to intervene. SERAP urged the UN to demand answers from Nigerian authorities regarding the 37 deaths in custody and alleged lethal force used against protesters in Minna, stressing that every custodial death requires a transparent, independent, and thorough investigation.

As the 10-member panel commences its work, the Federal Government's next steps depend heavily on the findings due within two weeks, which are expected to guide further legal actions, institutional reforms, and potential disciplinary measures across the security apparatus.
