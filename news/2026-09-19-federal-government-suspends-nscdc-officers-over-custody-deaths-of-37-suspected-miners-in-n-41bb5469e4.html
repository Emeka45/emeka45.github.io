Federal Government Suspends NSCDC Officers Over Custody Deaths of 37 Suspected Miners in Niger State

The Federal Government has suspended 20 Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC) officers, including the Niger State Commandant, following the tragic deaths of 37 individuals held in custody on suspicion of illegal mining. Meanwhile, the Nigerian Bar Association has demanded a transparent investigation into the incident and the financiers behind the operations.

The Federal Government has suspended 20 officers of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC), including the Niger State Commandant, following the tragic deaths of 37 individuals held in custody on suspicion of illegal mining. The incident, which occurred in Minna, Niger State, was reported in the early hours of Thursday, September 17, 2026. While Reuters reported that 33 bodies had been deposited at the General Hospital, Minna mortuary, Niger State Governor Mohammed Umaru Bago confirmed a total of 37 deaths.

Government Action and Investigation Panel

Following a directive by President Bola Tinubu for a comprehensive and transparent investigation, Minister of Interior Olubunmi Tunji Ojo announced the suspensions on Saturday. The suspended personnel include the Niger State Commandant, Suberu Aniviye, alongside 20 other officers involved in station guard, arrest, investigation, intelligence, legal, and guard duties.

To oversee the inquiry, the Minister constituted a 10-member independent investigative committee chaired by retired Department of State Services Deputy Director General Jonathan Kure, with former Director General of the Nigerian Law School Prof. Isa Hayatu Chiroma serving as secretary. Other members include retired AIG Hosea Hassan Karma, Prof. Olayinka Buhari, representatives of the Minna Emirate Council and Niger State Government, Miners Association of Nigeria National Secretary Liman Sulaiman, lawyer and human rights activist Deji Adeyanju, Blueprint journalist Zainab Suleiman Okino, and public affairs analyst George Agbakahi.

The panel has been tasked with establishing the identities of the deceased, investigating the circumstances of their arrest and detention, determining the exact cause of death, and assessing any responsibility, complicity, negligence, or misconduct. The committee, which has two weeks to submit its report, is also empowered to recommend appropriate actions, compensation where applicable, and preventative measures. Minister Tunji Ojo warned that any attempt to destroy or conceal evidence, intimidate witnesses, or obstruct the probe would be treated as a serious offense.

Reactions and Calls for Broader Accountability

The incident has drawn widespread attention and demands for accountability. The Nigerian Bar Association (NBA), through its President Oyinkansola Badejo-Okusanya, SAN, called for a thorough, independent, transparent, and credible investigation into the deaths. While emphasizing that the deceased were suspects who had not been tried or convicted, the NBA urged authorities to look beyond labourers at mining sites and investigate the owners, financiers, and controllers who ultimately profit from illegal mining operations.

The NBA also highlighted concerns regarding facility conditions, overcrowding, lack of ventilation, and reports that some detainees cried out for help while struggling to breathe. Furthermore, the association raised concerns over reports that children might have been among those detained and urged forensic and medical investigations to preserve evidence and protect surviving detainees and witnesses. The NBA has established its own independent team, headed by Jonathan Gunu Taidi, SAN, to engage families and relevant authorities.
