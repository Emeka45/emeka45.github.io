Federal Government Probes 37 Deaths in Custody, Suspends NSCDC Officers in Niger State

The Federal Government has launched an independent investigative panel and suspended 20 Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps officers, including the Niger State Commandant, following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody.

The Federal Government has taken decisive action following the tragic deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners who died while in the custody of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC) in Niger State. As part of the response, authorities have inaugurated an independent investigative committee and pledged that there will be no cover-up in uncovering the circumstances surrounding the fatalities.

Alongside the establishment of the probe panel, the government announced the immediate suspension of 20 NSCDC officers connected to the incident. This disciplinary measure also affects the Niger State Commandant of the corps, ensuring that those in leadership positions at the time of the incident step aside to allow for an unhindered and transparent inquiry.

Independent Probe and Accountability

The newly formed independent committee has been mandated to thoroughly investigate how 37 individuals lost their lives while being held by the security agency. Government officials have emphasized that transparency is paramount, reassuring the public that the investigation will be conducted thoroughly and that any personnel found culpable will face appropriate administrative and legal consequences.

The incident has drawn significant public attention to detention conditions and institutional accountability within security formations, particularly regarding operations involving illegal mining suspects. Further developments are expected as the investigative panel commences its hearings and reviews the sequence of events that led to the fatalities in Niger State.
