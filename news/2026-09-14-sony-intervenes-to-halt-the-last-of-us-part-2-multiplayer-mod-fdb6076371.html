Sony Intervenes to Halt The Last of Us Part 2 Multiplayer Mod

Development on an anticipated multiplayer modification for The Last of Us Part 2 has been abruptly stopped after PlayStation requested the creators to cease their work.

Development on an anticipated multiplayer modification for The Last of Us Part 2 has been abruptly stopped after PlayStation requested the creators to cease their work. The project, which had been in development since January, was scheduled to be released publicly in September 2026 before receiving a formal legal notice.

Sony Intervention and Cancellation

Modder Speclizer announced the shutdown on X on Sept. 13, 2026, stating that they had received a letter on behalf of Sony Interactive Entertainment requesting that the modification not be released. According to reports from Eurogamer, the PvP modification for the PC version of the game was halted following direct contact from PlayStation.

While the exact contents of Sony's letter were not publicly detailed, industry observers and fans speculated that the intervention was prompted by the project's monetization. Access to the mod during its development phase was placed behind a paywall via a Patreon account, which typically infringes on intellectual property rights and triggers corporate enforcement.

Features and Scope of the Fan Project

The ambitious fan-made project aimed to deliver a comprehensive multiplayer experience. Footage shared by the creators earlier in the year—including a 25-minute gameplay video released in June and an August update titled "We're getting closer"—revealed that the mod incorporated listening mechanics, unique player perks, and maps absent from the base single-player campaign.

The gameplay design sought to recreate elements reminiscent of the original The Last of Us Factions mode, which emphasized tension, tactical stealth, and team-based matches. The nine months of dedicated development ultimately went unpublished, leaving supporters disappointed.

Wider Context of The Last of Us Multiplayer

The cancellation arrives against a backdrop of official multiplayer turbulence for the franchise. In late 2023, developer Naughty Dog officially canceled its standalone multiplayer project, The Last of Us Online, after a troubled development cycle that began in 2020. That official title was intended to be significantly larger than previous multiplayer iterations, aligning with Sony's broader, yet largely unsuccessful, push into live-service games.

With official multiplayer ambitions shelved by the studio and fan-driven modifications halted by legal requests, options for cooperative or competitive multiplayer experiences within the universe remain extremely limited.
