Lagos State Directs Immediate Relocation Amid Fresh Flood Alert

Following a fresh seven-day flood alert issued by the Nigerian Hydrological Services Agency (NiHSA), the Lagos State Government has directed residents living in low-lying and flood-prone communities to relocate immediately to safer ground. The directive aims to prevent the loss of lives and property as heavy rainfall and rising water levels threaten vulnerable settlements.

The Commissioner for the Environment and Water Resources, Tokunbo Wahab, disclosed the directive in a statement issued by the ministry’s Director of Public Affairs, Kunle Adeshina. According to the state government, the advisory affects Lagos alongside 14 other states across Nigeria, including Imo, Cross River, Delta, Ebonyi, Benue, Anambra, Akwa Ibom, Edo, Enugu, Bayelsa, Kogi, Abia, Taraba, and Rivers.

Vulnerable Areas and High-Risk Zones

Within Lagos, vulnerable locations identified by authorities include Eti-Osa, Lagos Island, Oke-Ira Nla in Ajah, Kirikiri, Ilaje Waterfront Jetty, and Jetty Terminal B. Furthermore, communities situated along the Ogun River and other major waterways have been advised to exercise heightened caution due to rising water levels in connected river systems affecting downstream areas.

Wahab specifically listed Isheri North, Kara, Itowolo, Ajegunle, Majidun, Ogolonto, and parts of Agboyi—along with other settlements downstream of Isheri Olofin—as areas that should maintain a high state of alert between September 19 and 25. The NiHSA report warned that the rising river levels could inundate vulnerable communities and disrupt critical public facilities, such as schools, healthcare centres, markets, farms, and places of worship.

Government Measures and Safety Guidelines

Emphasizing that the state is treating the advisory with utmost seriousness, Wahab noted that monitoring has been intensified across riverine, low-lying, and flood-prone locations. He urged residents not to wait until floodwaters rise before taking action, noting that early preparation and temporary relocation are vital.

“We are urging Lagosians, particularly those living in flood-prone and low-lying areas, not to take this warning lightly. When the signs point to danger, the safest decision is to move early. No property is worth risking a life,” Wahab stated.

The commissioner called on local governments, emergency management agencies, community leaders, and residents to activate early-warning systems and relocate people, livestock, and valuables to secure locations. He also appealed to the public to support flood mitigation efforts by avoiding the dumping of refuse into drains, canals, and waterways, and cautioned against walking or driving through flooded roads and fast-moving water.
