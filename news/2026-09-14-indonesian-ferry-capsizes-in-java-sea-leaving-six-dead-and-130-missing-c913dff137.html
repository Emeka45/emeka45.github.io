Indonesian Ferry Capsizes in Java Sea, Leaving Six Dead and 130 Missing

A ferry traveling between Java and Borneo capsized in severe weather, prompting a large-scale search and rescue operation.

A large-scale search and rescue operation is underway in the Java Sea after an Indonesian passenger ferry capsized in severe weather conditions, leaving at least six people dead and 130 others missing. Authorities confirmed that 107 people have been rescued following the disaster involving the 390-foot (119m) Virgo Transport 8 ship.

Timeline and Journey Details

According to maritime monitoring data, the ferry departed from Surabaya at 10:33 on Saturday, heading for the South Kalimantan city of Banjarmasin. The vessel was scheduled to complete its journey on Sunday at 14:00. However, contact was lost after the ship encountered severe weather in the Java Sea.

The last reported position of the ferry placed it approximately 150km (93 miles) south of Banjarmasin around 02:00 on Sunday (18:00 GMT on Saturday). Authorities received the initial distress report shortly after, at 04:10. There were 213 passengers and 30 crew members on board at the time of the incident.

Rescue Operations and Response

A joint task force comprising Indonesia's search and rescue agency, the navy, maritime police, and other organizations has been deployed to coordinate the response. Operations involve 280 personnel utilizing ships and a helicopter to search the area. Additionally, all vessels operating in the Java Sea have been placed on alert to report any signs of survivors.

Amid the ongoing search, families have gathered at Trisakti Port in Banjarmasin and Tanjung Perak Port in Surabaya to seek information about their loved ones. Some passengers had managed to communicate prior to the disaster; relatives shared accounts of frightening conditions and heavy rocking against severe waves before contact was lost. Meanwhile, some survivors have begun returning ashore, with individuals such as freight driver Ahmad Supandi rescued by the passing vessel MV Haida.

Context of Marine Transport in Indonesia

With an archipelago spanning more than 17,000 islands, ferries serve as a vital form of transport across Indonesia. Despite their widespread use, maritime accidents remain a recurring safety concern. Government data indicates that 111 ship accidents were officially recorded between 2020 and 2026. Recent incidents include the sinking of the KM Nurul Salsa in waters west of Polassi Island in the Selayar Islands Regency, as well as a separate ferry fire off Madura island.
