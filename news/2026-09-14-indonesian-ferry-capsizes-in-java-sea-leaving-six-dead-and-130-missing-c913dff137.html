Indonesian Ferry Capsizes in Java Sea, Leaving Six Dead and 130 Missing

A ferry traveling between Java and Borneo capsized in severe weather, prompting a large-scale search and rescue operation.

A large-scale search and rescue operation is underway in the Java Sea after the passenger ferry Virgo Transport 8 capsized in severe weather, leaving six people dead and 130 missing. Authorities reported that 107 people have been rescued following the disaster, which prompted a coordinated response from a joint task force involving Indonesia's search and rescue agency, the navy, and maritime police.

Timeline and Voyage Details

According to maritime monitoring data, the 390-foot (119-meter) ferry departed from Surabaya at 10:33 on Saturday, bound for the South Kalimantan city of Banjarmasin. On board were 213 passengers and 30 crew members. Officials received the initial distress report at 04:10 on Sunday. The vessel was last reported to be approximately 150km (93 miles) south of Banjarmasin around 02:00 on Sunday (18:00 GMT on Saturday) after encountering bad weather during its journey across the Java Sea.

Search and Rescue Response

Following the disaster, ships and a helicopter were deployed to the area, with 280 personnel participating in the ongoing operation. Authorities have alerted all vessels in the Java Sea region to report any signs of survivors or passengers in the water. An operations post has been established in the Trisakti Port area to coordinate the response and assist families awaiting news.

Among those rescued was Ahmad Supandi, a freight driver aboard the ship who was later picked up by the vessel MV Haida. However, anxiety remains high for many families gathered at Trisakti Port in Banjarmasin and Tanjung Perak Port in Surabaya, as they anxiously seek information regarding missing relatives and crew members.

Context of Maritime Transport in Indonesia

Comprising more than 17,000 islands, the Indonesian archipelago relies heavily on ferries as a common form of transport. Despite their prevalence, marine accidents remain a recurring concern. Government data indicates that 111 ship accidents were recorded between 2020 and 2026. Recent incidents include the sinking of the KM Nurul Salsa in July in waters west of Polassi Island, as well as a separate ferry fire off Madura island that killed five people the previous month.
