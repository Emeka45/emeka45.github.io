Indonesian Ferry Capsizes in Java Sea, Leaving Six Dead and 130 Missing

A ferry traveling between Java and Borneo capsized in severe weather, prompting a large-scale search and rescue operation.

A passenger ferry traveling between Java and Borneo capsized in the Java Sea after encountering severe weather, leaving six people dead and 130 others missing, according to BBC News. Authorities confirmed that 107 people have been rescued from the disaster involving the 390-foot (119-meter) vessel.

Voyage Details and Incident Timeline

The ship, named Virgo Transport 8, was carrying 213 passengers and 30 crew members. It departed from Surabaya at 10:33 on Saturday and was scheduled to arrive in the South Kalimantan city of Banjarmasin on Sunday at 14:00. Contact was lost as the vessel faced bad weather, and maritime monitoring data indicated it was last reported approximately 150km (93 miles) south of Banjarmasin around 02:00 on Sunday (18:00 GMT on Saturday). Authorities received the initial distress report at 04:10.

Search and Rescue Operations

A joint task force comprising Indonesia's search and rescue agency, the navy, maritime police, and other parties was immediately deployed. A total of 280 personnel, alongside ships and a helicopter, are taking part in the ongoing search and rescue operation. All vessels operating in the Java Sea area have been alerted to watch for and report any signs of survivors or passengers.

Families Await News at Ports

Operations posts and information centers have been set up at Trisakti Port in Banjarmasin and Tanjung Perak Port in Surabaya, where anxious family members have gathered to seek news about their loved ones. Rusmilawati, a Banjarmasin resident, went to Trisakti Port looking for information regarding eight relatives who were returning from a trip to visit Islamic scholars' tombs in Java. She noted that the family had last heard from a relative via a WhatsApp status update describing frightening waves and heavy rocking just prior to the disaster.

Among those confirmed safe was Ahmad Supandi, a freight driver aboard the ferry who was rescued by the vessel MV Haida. Broader Maritime Context in Indonesia

Indonesia's vast archipelago relies heavily on ferries across its more than 17,000 islands, but marine transport accidents remain a recurring safety challenge. Government records indicate that 111 ship accidents took place between 2020 and 2026. Recent incidents include the sinking of the KM Nurul Salsa in waters west of Polassi Island in the Selayar Islands Regency, South Sulawesi, which left three dead and 14 missing, as well as a separate ferry fire off Madura island that killed five people the previous month.
