Indonesian Ferry Capsizes in Java Sea, Leaving Six Dead and 130 Missing

A ferry traveling between Java and Borneo capsized in severe weather, prompting a large-scale search and rescue operation.

A large-scale search and rescue operation is underway in the Java Sea after an Indonesian passenger ferry capsized in severe weather, leaving six people dead, 107 rescued, and another 130 people missing. Authorities are utilizing ships, a helicopter, and a joint task force consisting of 280 personnel from the navy, Indonesia's search and rescue agency, and maritime police to locate survivors.

Voyage Details and Incident Timeline

The 390ft (119m) vessel, identified as the Virgo Transport 8, was carrying 213 passengers and 30 crew members. Data from maritime monitoring sites indicates the ship departed from Surabaya at 10:33 on Saturday and was scheduled to arrive at the South Kalimantan city of Banjarmasin on Sunday at 14:00.

Contact was lost after the ship encountered bad weather during its journey between Java and Borneo. The vessel's last reported position was approximately 150km (93 miles) south of Banjarmasin at around 02:00 on Sunday, with authorities receiving the initial distress report at 04:10.

Impact on Families and Rescue Reactions

Operations posts and information centers have been established at Trisakti Port in Banjarmasin and Tanjung Perak Port in Surabaya as anxious families gather to await news of their loved ones. Rusmilawati, a Banjarmasin resident, went to the port seeking information about eight relatives who were aboard the ship returning from visiting Islamic scholar tombs in Java. She noted that a relative had shared a concerning WhatsApp status update before contact was lost, describing frightening waves and heavy rocking.

Other families received mixed outcomes. Misnah experienced relief after discovering her husband, freight driver Ahmad Supandi, survived the disaster after being rescued by the vessel MV Haida. Meanwhile, relatives of crew members, such as Fredy—whose youngest sibling Stevie Manuhua worked on the ship as an onboard keyboard player—remoyed anxiously awaiting updates amid the ongoing search efforts.

Context of Maritime Transport in Indonesia

Indonesia is an extensive archipelago comprising more than 17,000 islands where ferries serve as a vital form of transport. However, maritime accidents remain a persistent safety concern. Government records indicate that 111 ship accidents took place between 2020 and 2026. Recent incidents include the sinking of the KM Nurul Salsa in July in waters west of Polassi Island in the Selayar Islands Regency, which resulted in three deaths and 14 missing individuals, alongside a ferry fire off Madura island that killed five people the previous month.
