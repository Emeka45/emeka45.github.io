Indonesian Ferry Capsizes in Java Sea, Leaving Six Dead and 130 Missing

A ferry traveling between Java and Borneo capsized in severe weather, prompting a large-scale search and rescue operation.

A large-scale search and rescue operation is underway in the Java Sea after an Indonesian passenger ferry capsized in severe weather, leaving six people dead and 130 others missing. Authorities confirmed that 107 people have been rescued following the disaster involving the Virgo Transport 8.

Vessel Details and Timeline

The 390ft (119m) ferry was carrying 213 passengers and 30 crew members when it departed from Surabaya at 10:33 on Saturday, heading for the South Kalimantan city of Banjarmasin. According to data from maritime monitoring sites, the ship was scheduled to arrive on Sunday at 14:00.

Contact was lost after the vessel encountered bad weather. Its last reported position was approximately 150km (93 miles) south of Banjarmasin around 02:00 on Sunday. Authorities received the initial distress report at 04:10.

Search and Rescue Operations

A joint task force has been established, comprising Indonesia's search and rescue agency, the navy, maritime police, and other parties. Ships and a helicopter have been deployed to the scene, with 280 personnel actively participating in the operation. All vessels operating in the Java Sea area have been placed on alert to report any signs of survivors.

Some passengers have been picked up by other vessels in the area. For instance, freight driver Ahmad Supandi survived after being rescued by the vessel MV Haida.

Families Await News at Ports

An operations post for handling the accident was set up at Trisakti Port in Banjarmasin, where anxious families have gathered to seek information about their loved ones. Rusmilawati, a Banjarmasin resident, visited the port to look for eight relatives who were returning from a trip to visit Islamic scholars' tombs in Java. She noted that a relative's final WhatsApp status update described frighteningly high waves and heavy rocking.

Families of crew members have similarly gathered at Tanjung Perak Port in Surabaya. Fredy, whose youngest sibling Stevie Manuhua worked aboard the ship as an onboard keyboard player, reported having no news regarding his brother's whereabouts.

Context of Maritime Transport in Indonesia

Comprising more than 17,000 islands, the Indonesian archipelago relies heavily on ferries as a standard form of transport. However, marine accidents remain a persistent challenge. Government figures indicate that 111 ship accidents occurred between 2020 and 2026. Recent incidents include the sinking of the KM Nurul Salsa in July in waters west of Polassi Island, and a separate ferry fire off Madura island last month that resulted in five deaths.
