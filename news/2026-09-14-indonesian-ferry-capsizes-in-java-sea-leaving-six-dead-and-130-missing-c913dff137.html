Indonesian Ferry Capsizes in Java Sea, Leaving Six Dead and 130 Missing

A ferry traveling between Java and Borneo capsized in severe weather, prompting a large-scale search and rescue operation.

A large-scale search and rescue operation is underway in the Java Sea following the capsizing of an Indonesian passenger ferry, BBC News reports. Authorities confirmed that six people have died and 107 have been rescued, while another 130 individuals remain missing after contact was lost with the vessel during severe weather conditions.

Journey and Incident Timeline

The ship, named the Virgo Transport 8, was traveling from Surabaya to the South Kalimantan city of Banjarmasin. According to data from maritime monitoring sites, the 390-foot (119-meter) ferry departed Surabaya at 10:33 on Saturday and was scheduled to arrive in Banjarmasin at 14:00 on Sunday.

The vessel encountered bad weather and was last reported to be approximately 150km (93 miles) south of Banjarmasin around 02:00 on Sunday (18:00 GMT on Saturday). Authorities received the initial distress report at 04:10.

Rescue Operations and Deployment

A joint task force incorporating Indonesia's search and rescue agency, the navy, maritime police, and other parties has been established to handle the accident. Operations are being supported by ships, a helicopter, and 280 personnel. Furthermore, all vessels operating within the Java Sea area have been alerted to watch for and report any signs of passengers.

Impact on Families and Relatives

Families of passengers and crew members have gathered at ports to await updates. At Trisakti Port in Banjarmasin, local resident Rusmilawati sought information regarding eight family members who were on board returning from a trip to visit Islamic scholars' tombs in Java. She noted that one relative had posted a WhatsApp status update prior to the incident, writing about frighteningly high waves and heavy rocking.

Amid the anxiety, some families received positive news; Misnah learned that her husband, Ahmad Supandi, a freight driver aboard the ship, had survived after being rescued by the vessel MV Haida. Meanwhile, relatives at Tanjung Perak Port in Surabaya, including Fredy—whose youngest sibling Stevie Manuhua worked on the vessel as a keyboard player—continued seeking information on missing loved ones.

Context of Maritime Transport in Indonesia

Comprising more than 17,000 islands, the Indonesian archipelago relies heavily on ferries as a standard form of public and commercial transport. However, marine transport accidents occur periodically. Government data indicates that 111 ship accidents were recorded between 2020 and 2026, including incidents earlier in the year such as the sinking of the KM Nurul Salsa in the Selayar Islands Regency and a ferry fire off Madura island.
