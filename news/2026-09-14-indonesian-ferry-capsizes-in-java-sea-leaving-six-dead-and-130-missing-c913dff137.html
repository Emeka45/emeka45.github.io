Indonesian Ferry Capsizes in Java Sea, Leaving Six Dead and 130 Missing

A ferry traveling between Java and Borneo capsized in severe weather, prompting a large-scale search and rescue operation.

At least six people have died and 107 others have been rescued following the capsizing of an Indonesian passenger ferry in the Java Sea, according to authorities. An intensive search and rescue operation is currently underway for another 130 individuals who remain missing after contact was lost with the vessel during severe weather conditions.

The Voyage and Distress Call

The ship, named the Virgo Transport 8, was operating a route from Surabaya to the South Kalimantan city of Banjarmasin as reported by BBC News. Maritime monitoring data indicates that the 390-foot (119-meter) vessel departed Surabaya at 10:33 on Saturday and was scheduled to arrive in Banjarmasin at 14:00 on Sunday.

Authorities received the initial distress report at 04:10 on Sunday. The ferry was last reported approximately 150 km (93 miles) south of Banjarmasin around 02:00. At the time of the incident, the manifest recorded 213 passengers and 30 crew members on board. Among the passengers was a relative of Banjarmasin resident Rusmilawati, who noted that a final WhatsApp status update from a family member on the ship read: "The waves are really frightening tonight. Or is this just what it's normally like travelling by ship, rocking and crashing against the waves?"

Search and Rescue Operations

A joint task force incorporating Indonesia’s search and rescue agency, the navy, and maritime police has been deployed to the area. The operation involves 280 personnel utilizing ships and a helicopter. Furthermore, all vessels navigating the Java Sea have been placed on alert to report any signs of survivors.

While many families await news at Trisakti Port in Banjarmasin and Tanjung Perak Port in Surabaya, some passengers have been accounted for. Misnah, another local resident, learned that her husband Ahmad Supandi, a freight driver aboard the ship, survived after being rescued by the vessel MV Haida.

Broader Context of Maritime Incidents in Indonesia

Spanning over 17,000 islands, the Indonesian archipelago heavily relies on ferries for everyday transport, though marine accidents have occurred periodically. Government figures indicate that 111 ship accidents took place between 2020 and 2026. Recent incidents include the sinking of the KM Nurul Salsa in waters west of Polassi Island in South Sulawesi, which left three dead and 14 missing, as well as a ferry fire off Madura island that claimed five lives.
