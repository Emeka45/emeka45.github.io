Indonesian Ferry Capsizes in Java Sea, Leaving Six Dead and 130 Missing

A ferry traveling between Java and Borneo capsized in severe weather, prompting a large-scale search and rescue operation.

At least six people have died and 130 others remain missing after an Indonesian passenger ferry capsized in the Java Sea while encountering severe weather. Authorities confirmed that 107 people have been rescued following the accident involving the Virgo Transport 8 ship.

Timeline and Voyage Details

The 390-foot (119-meter) ferry departed from Surabaya at 10:33 on Saturday, bound for the South Kalimantan city of Banjarmasin. Maritime monitoring data indicated the ship was scheduled to arrive on Sunday at 14:00. Contact was lost after the vessel encountered bad weather, and authorities received an initial distress report at 04:10 on Sunday.

The ship was last reported to be roughly 150 kilometers (93 miles) south of Banjarmasin at approximately 02:00 on Sunday. Aboard the vessel were 213 passengers and 30 crew members.

Search and Rescue Operations

A large-scale rescue operation involving 280 personnel has been deployed to the scene, utilizing ships and a helicopter. All vessels navigating the Java Sea area have been alerted to report any signs of passengers.

A joint task force has been established, bringing together Indonesia's search and rescue agency, the navy, maritime police, and other organizations. Operations posts have been set up at Trisakti Port in Banjarmasin and Tanjung Perak Port in Surabaya, where anxious family members have gathered to seek information about their loved ones.

Among those waiting at Trisakti Port was Rusmilawati, who sought news regarding eight relatives traveling back after visiting Islamic scholar tombs in Java. She noted that a relative had posted a WhatsApp status update before the disaster, writing, "The waves are really frightening tonight."

Other families shared moments of relief and ongoing anguish. Misnah learned that her husband, Ahmad Supandi, a freight driver on board, had survived after being rescued by the vessel MV Haida. Meanwhile, relatives of crew members, such as Fredy—whose youngest sibling Stevie Manuhua worked on the ship as a keyboard player—reported having received no information regarding their whereabouts.

Context of Marine Transport in Indonesia

The incident highlights ongoing safety concerns within the Indonesian archipelago, which spans more than 17,000 islands and relies heavily on ferries for transport. Marine transport accidents are frequent; government figures recorded 111 ship accidents between 2020 and 2026. Recent incidents include the sinking of the KM Nurul Salsa in waters west of Polassi Island in July and a ferry fire off Madura island the previous month.
