Indonesian Ferry Capsizes in Java Sea, Leaving Six Dead and 130 Missing

A ferry traveling between Java and Borneo capsized in severe weather, prompting a large-scale search and rescue operation.

A large-scale search and rescue operation is underway in the Java Sea after an Indonesian passenger ferry capsized in severe weather, leaving at least six people dead and 130 others missing. Authorities have confirmed that 107 people have been rescued following the disaster involving the Virgo Transport 8 ship, as detailed in reports regarding Indonesian rescue crews searching for people after the ferry hit bad weather.

Timeline and Journey Details

The 390-foot (119-meter) vessel was carrying a total of 243 individuals, comprising 213 passengers and 30 crew members. According to data from maritime monitoring sites, the ship departed from Surabaya at 10:33 on Saturday and was scheduled to arrive at the South Kalimantan city of Banjarmasin on Sunday at 14:00. Contact was lost after the ship encountered bad weather in the Java Sea. The final reported position placed the vessel about 150km (93 miles) south of Banjarmasin around 02:00 on Sunday (18:00 GMT on Saturday). Authorities received the initial distress report at 04:10.

Search and Rescue Response

A joint task force combining Indonesia's search and rescue agency, the navy, maritime police, and other organizations has deployed ships and a helicopter to the scene. A total of 280 personnel are participating in the operation, and all vessels navigating the Java Sea have been placed on alert to report any signs of passengers. Some survivors have already been accounted for; for instance, freight driver Ahmad Supandi was rescued from the waters by the vessel MV Haida.

Families Await News at Ports

An operations post has been established in the Trisakti Port area in Banjarmasin, where anxious families have gathered to seek information. Context of Maritime Transport in Indonesia

Comprising more than 17,000 islands, the Indonesian archipelago heavily relies on ferries as a common form of transport. However, marine transport accidents remain a recurring safety concern, with government data showing that 111 ship accidents were recorded between 2020 and 2026. Recent incidents include the sinking of the KM Nurul Salsa in waters west of Polassi Island in July and a separate ferry fire off Madura island the previous month.
