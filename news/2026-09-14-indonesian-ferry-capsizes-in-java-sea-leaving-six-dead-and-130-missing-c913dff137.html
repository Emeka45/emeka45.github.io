Indonesian Ferry Capsizes in Java Sea, Leaving Six Dead and 130 Missing

A ferry traveling between Java and Borneo capsized in severe weather, prompting a large-scale search and rescue operation.

Six people have died and 107 have been rescued after an Indonesian passenger ferry capsized in the Java Sea while encountering severe weather, according to BBC News. A large-scale search and rescue operation involving 280 personnel, ships, and a helicopter is currently underway to locate 130 people who remain missing.

Voyage Details and Incident Timeline

The 390-foot (119m) ship, named Virgo Transport 8, was carrying 213 passengers and 30 crew members. Data from maritime monitoring sites indicates the ferry departed from Surabaya at 10:33 on Saturday, heading toward the South Kalimantan city of Banjarmasin, with a scheduled arrival time of Sunday at 14:00.

Contact was lost after the vessel encountered bad weather. It was last reported to be approximately 150km (93 miles) south of Banjarmasin at around 02:00 on Sunday (18:00 GMT on Saturday). Authorities received the initial distress report at 04:10.

Rescue Operations and Impact on Families

A joint task force comprising Indonesia's search and rescue agency, the navy, maritime police, and other parties has been established to handle the accident. An operations post was set up in the Trisakti Port area in Banjarmasin, where anxious families gathered to seek information.

Resident Rusmilawati traveled to the port to look for eight relatives returning from Java. One relative had sent a WhatsApp status update before communication was lost, writing about frightening waves and the ship rocking heavily. Meanwhile, other families experienced relief, such as Misnah, who learned that her husband, freight driver Ahmad Supandi, survived after being rescued by the vessel MV Haida.

At Tanjung Perak Port in Surabaya, relatives of crew members—including Fredy, whose youngest sibling worked aboard as a keyboard player—have also been seeking news regarding their loved ones' whereabouts.

Context of Maritime Transport in Indonesia

Ferries are a common and essential form of transport across Indonesia's archipelago of more than 17,000 islands, but marine transport accidents continue to pose safety challenges. Government figures show that 111 ship accidents were recorded between 2020 and 2026. Recent incidents include the sinking of KM Nurul Salsa in July in waters west of Polassi Island in Selayar Islands Regency, South Sulawesi, and a separate ferry fire off Madura island last month that resulted in five deaths.
