Indonesian Ferry Capsizes in Java Sea, Leaving Six Dead and 130 Missing

A ferry traveling between Java and Borneo capsized in severe weather, prompting a large-scale search and rescue operation.

A large-scale search and rescue operation is underway in the Java Sea after an Indonesian passenger ferry capsized in severe weather, leaving six people dead and 130 others missing. Authorities reported that 107 people have been rescued following the disaster involving the Virgo Transport 8.

Voyage Timeline and Distress Call

According to maritime monitoring data, the 390-foot (119m) vessel departed from Surabaya at 10:33 on Saturday, heading toward the South Kalimantan city of Banjarmasin, with a scheduled arrival time of Sunday at 14:00. The ferry carried 213 passengers and 30 crew members. Contact was lost after the ship encountered bad weather, and authorities received the initial distress report at 04:10 on Sunday. The ship was last reported approximately 150km (93 miles) south of Banjarmasin around 02:00 on Sunday (18:00 GMT on Saturday), as reported by BBC News.

Search and Rescue Operation

A joint task force consisting of Indonesia's search and rescue agency, the navy, maritime police, and other parties has been mobilized. Deployments include ships, a helicopter, and 280 personnel. All vessels operating in the Java Sea area have been placed on alert to report any signs of passengers. An operations post has been established in the Trisakti Port area to coordinate the response and assist families.

Families Await News at Ports

Anxious relatives have gathered at ports in Banjarmasin and Surabaya to seek information regarding their loved ones. Rusmilawati, a Banjarmasin resident, traveled to Trisakti Port to track the status of eight family members who were returning from visiting Islamic scholars' tombs in Java. She noted that a relative's final WhatsApp status update described frighteningly high waves and heavy rocking.

Other families shared stories of relief and anguish. Misnah learned that her husband, Ahmad Supandi—a freight driver aboard the ship—survived after being rescued by the vessel MV Haida. Meanwhile, relatives of crew members, such as Fredy, whose youngest sibling Stevie Manuhua worked on board as a keyboard player, waited for updates amid ongoing uncertainty.

Context of Maritime Transport in Indonesia

Comprising more than 17,000 islands, the Indonesian archipelago relies heavily on ferries as a standard form of transport, though marine accidents occur periodically. Government records indicate that 111 ship accidents took place between 2020 and 2026. Recent maritime incidents include the sinking of the KM Nurul Salsa in July in waters west of Polassi Island, which left three dead and 14 missing, as well as a ferry fire off Madura island that killed five people the previous month.
