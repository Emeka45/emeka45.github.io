Federal Government Suspends 20 NSCDC Officers as NBA Investigates Deaths of 37 Miners in Niger State

The Federal Government has suspended 20 Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps officers, including the Niger State Commandant, following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody. Concurrently, the Nigerian Bar Association has established an investigative team to probe the incident.

The Federal Government has suspended 20 officers of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC), including the Niger State Commandant Suberu Aniviye, following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners while in the custody of the agency in Minna, Niger State. The incident, which occurred on Thursday, September 17, 2026, has also drawn the intervention of the Nigerian Bar Association (NBA), which has established an independent investigative team.

Government Action and Investigative Panel

Minister of Interior Olubunmi Tunji Ojo announced the suspensions and the formation of a 10-member independent investigative committee following a directive from President Bola Tinubu for a comprehensive inquiry into the tragedy. The suspended personnel include officers involved in station guard, arrest, investigation, intelligence, legal, and guard duties, alongside the separately suspended state commandant.

The high-level investigative panel is chaired by Jonathan Kure, a retired Deputy Director General of the Department of State Services, with Prof. Isa Hayatu Chiroma, former Director General of the Nigerian Law School, serving as secretary. Other members include retired AIG Hosea Hassan Karma, Prof. Olayinka Buhari, representatives of the Minna Emirate Council and the Niger State Government, Liman Sulaiman representing the Miners Association of Nigeria, human rights activist Deji Adeyanju, journalist Zainab Suleiman Okino, and public affairs analyst George Agbakahi.

The committee has been mandated to determine the identities of the deceased, investigate the circumstances of their arrest and detention, identify the scientific cause of death, and establish any responsibility, complicity, negligence, or misconduct. The panel has two weeks to submit its report and has been granted powers to visit locations, access necessary documents, receive public memoranda, and co-opt experts. The Minister warned that any attempt to conceal evidence, intimidate witnesses, or obstruct the probe would face severe penalties.

Nigerian Bar Association Demands Accountability

Concurrently, the Nigerian Bar Association announced it would actively monitor and investigate the incident, emphasizing its institutional watchdog role. NBA President Oyinkansola Badejo-Okusanya constituted an independent probe team led by Senior Advocate of Nigeria and former NBA General Secretary Jonathan Gunu Taidi.

The NBA stressed that entering government custody alive and failing to return demands urgent answers, noting that the suspects had not been convicted of any crime and retained the presumption of innocence. The association called for thorough inquiries into facility conditions, overcrowding, ventilation, medical availability, and the specific circumstances leading up to the deaths. Furthermore, the NBA urged authorities to investigate reports that children were among the detainees and emphasized that law enforcement must trace not only the laborers at the mining sites but also those who financed, organized, and profited from the illegal mining operations.
