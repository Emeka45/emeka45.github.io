Federal Government Suspends 20 NSCDC Officers as NBA Investigates Deaths of 37 Miners in Niger State

The Federal Government has suspended 20 Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps officers, including the Niger State Commandant, following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody. Concurrently, the Nigerian Bar Association has established an investigative team to probe the incident.

Federal Government and Independent Panels Launch Probe

The Federal Government has suspended 20 officers of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC), including the Niger State Commandant, following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in the Corps' custody in Minna, Niger State. The Minister of Interior, Olubunmi Tunji Ojo, announced the suspensions and constituted a 10-member independent investigative committee following a directive from President Bola Tinubu.

The incident occurred on Thursday, September 17, 2026. The 10-member investigative panel is chaired by retired Department of State Services Deputy Director General Jonathan Kure, with former Nigerian Law School Director General Prof. Isa Hayatu Chiroma serving as secretary. Other committee members include retired AIG Hosea Hassan Karma, Prof. Olayinka Buhari, representatives of the Minna Emirate Council and the Niger State Government, Miners Association of Nigeria National Secretary Liman Sulaiman, human rights lawyer Deji Adeyanju, Blueprint journalist Zainab Suleiman Okino, and public affairs analyst George Agbakahi.

The panel has been mandated to establish the identities of the deceased, determine the cause of death, investigate the circumstances of their arrest and detention, and review issues of responsibility, complicity, negligence, and misconduct. The committee, which has two weeks to submit its report, is also tasked with recommending appropriate actions, compensation where applicable, and preventive measures. Minister Tunji Ojo warned that any attempt to destroy evidence, intimidate witnesses, or obstruct the probe would be treated as a serious offense.

Nigerian Bar Association Demands Transparency

Concurrently, the Nigerian Bar Association (NBA) announced it has constituted an independent investigative team led by Senior Advocate of Nigeria and former NBA General Secretary Jonathan Gunu Taidi, SAN. NBA President Oyinkansola Badejo-Okusanya, SAN, stated that the association would actively fulfill its institutional watchdog role regarding the tragedy.

The NBA stressed that the deaths of 37 individuals who entered government custody alive demand rigorous answers. The association called for forensic and medical investigations, protection for surviving detainees and material witnesses, and full public disclosure of findings. Furthermore, the NBA urged authorities to investigate allegations of overcrowding, poor ventilation, and the overall treatment of detainees preceding their deaths.

The bar association also emphasized that the scope of the investigation should extend beyond the physical laborers at the mining sites to uncover the ownership, financing, and commercial control of the illegal mining operations. Highlighting that the deceased were unconvicted suspects, the NBA raised particular concern over reports that children might have been among the detained and deceased individuals, calling for an official verification of their ages and identities.
