Federal Government Suspends 20 NSCDC Officers Over Deaths of 37 Suspected Miners in Niger State Custody

The Federal Government has taken action by suspending 20 Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps officers and the Niger State Commandant following the controversial deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody, prompting widespread condemnation and demands for transparency.

The Federal Government has suspended 20 officers of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC) and the Niger State Commandant following the controversial deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custodial detention. The incident, which has sparked widespread condemnation and calls for accountability, occurred in Minna, Niger State, after the individuals were arrested during operations against illegal mining conducted on September 15 and 16, 2026.

Government Action and Investigation Panel

Minister of Interior Olubunmi Tunji-Ojo announced the suspensions on Saturday, September 19, 2026, alongside the formation of a 10-member independent investigative committee. The decisive action followed a directive from President Bola Tinubu for a comprehensive inquiry into the tragedy, which took place on Thursday, September 17, 2026.

The newly established panel is tasked with determining the identities of the deceased, investigating the circumstances surrounding their arrest, and clarifying the cause of death. Furthermore, the committee will examine issues of institutional responsibility, complicity, negligence, and misconduct, while recommending appropriate disciplinary measures, compensation where applicable, and safeguards to prevent future occurrences.

The investigation is chaired by retired Department of State Services Deputy Director General Jonathan Kure, with former Nigerian Law School Director General Prof. Isa Hayatu Chiroma acting as secretary. Other notable committee members include retired AIG Hosea Hassan Karma, Prof. Olayinka Buhari, representatives from the Minna Emirate Council and the Niger State Government, Miners Association of Nigeria National Secretary Liman Sulaiman, human rights lawyer Deji Adeyanju, Blueprint journalist Zainab Suleiman Okino, and public affairs analyst George Agbakahi. The panel has been given a two-week window to complete its assignment and submit its report.

Suspended Personnel and Directives

The suspended personnel include the Niger State Commandant, Suberu Aniviye (also referred to in reports as Suberu Siyaka Aniviye), and 20 other officers involved in station guard, arrest, investigation, intelligence, legal, and guard duties. Minister Tunji-Ojo instructed all relevant NSCDC leadership and officers to fully cooperate with the probe, warning that any obstruction, witness intimidation, or tampering with records and material evidence would be dealt with severely.

Public Reactions and Regional Responses

The deaths have drawn intense public backlash from civil society and youth organizations. Youths from the North-Central geopolitical zone, operating under the umbrella of the North-Central Indigenous Youth Council (NCIYC) and led by Board of Trustees Chairman Dr. JM Abbah, labeled the incident barbaric, inhuman, and unacceptable. The council criticized initial claims of a suspected disease outbreak as an insensitive attempt to cover up institutional failures, demanding transparent forensic autopsies and the prosecution of culpable officers.

Regional stakeholders have similarly commended state and federal responses. The NCIYC praised Niger State Governor Mohammed Bago for confirming the incident, establishing a parallel State Government Committee of Inquiry, ordering full forensic examinations, declaring a three-day mourning period, and canceling a scheduled political rally. The youth body also acknowledged the intervention of Minister of Solid Minerals Development Dr. Dele Alake and urged broader security reforms, including the deployment of a Joint Mining Protection Task Force, formalization of artisanal miners into cooperatives, and digital surveillance enhancements.

As independent investigators and security agencies continue their parallel inquiries, stakeholders across the country maintain a strong demand for transparency, public disclosure of final autopsy findings, and strict adherence to human rights standards in custodial facilities.
