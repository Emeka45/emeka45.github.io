South African Woman Sentenced to Prison in Nigeria for Heroin Trafficking as ActionSA Praises Verdict

A South African woman has been sentenced to prison in Nigeria for trafficking heroin, drawing praise from ActionSA president Herman Mashaba for the decisive legal action.

A 38-year-old South African woman has been sentenced to 25 years in prison by a Federal High Court in Abuja for importing 5.75 kilogrammes of heroin into Nigeria. The ruling has drawn praise from ActionSA leadership, who commended the decisive legal action taken by Nigerian authorities.

Arrest and Court Proceedings

The defendant, Will Jessica Ann, was arrested on July 6, 2026, by operatives of the National Drug Law Enforcement Agency (NDLEA) during the inward clearance of passengers on Qatar Airways Flight QR1433 arriving from Doha. She had been travelling with her three-year-old son and journeyed from Cambodia through Doha before arriving at the Nnamdi Azikiwe International Airport in Abuja.

During the operation, Ann initially denied travelling with checked-in luggage. However, NDLEA operatives established that two suitcases containing 14 large blocks of heroin bore baggage tags matching the claim tags attached to her passport. She subsequently admitted ownership of the bags, claiming she had forgotten she checked them in.

In August, Ann was arraigned before Justice Obiora Egwuatu of the Federal High Court in Abuja, where she pleaded guilty to a two-count charge bordering on conspiracy and unlawful importation under the National Drug Law Enforcement Agency Act. During court proceedings, she admitted in an extrajudicial statement to committing the offense and stated she conspired with a South African man named Jan Coenraad De Jager.

Sentencing and Judicial Remarks

During the sentencing on Thursday, Justice Egwuatu emphasized that Nigerian law is not influenced by emotions and that crime does not pay. The prosecution informed the court that the street value of heroin is approximately N18 million per kilogramme.

While given an opportunity to speak in mitigation, Ann expressed remorse, promised never to repeat the act, and claimed she did not know illicit drugs were inside the bags. Justice Egwuatu sentenced her to 15 years’ imprisonment on Count 1 and 25 years’ imprisonment on Count 2, with the sentences ordered to run concurrently. She will serve a maximum of 25 years. The judge further ruled that the heroin is to be destroyed if the convict does not appeal within the legal timeframe.

Reactions and Wider Context

Following the verdict, ActionSA president and Johannesburg mayoral candidate Herman Mashaba voiced strong support for the Nigerian judiciary's decision. Comparing the outcome to domestic challenges in his home country, Mashaba expressed gratitude to Nigeria for acting against transnational drug smuggling.

The NDLEA, led by Chairman Brig. Gen. Mohamed Buba Marwa (retd.), stated that the conviction reinforces the agency's position that Nigeria will not serve as a soft landing for international drug cartels. Additionally, the agency reported that the convict's husband, De Jager, was arrested in Johannesburg on July 27 while allegedly attempting to traffic 3.2kg of illicit drugs to Hong Kong.
