South African Woman Sentenced to Prison in Nigeria for Heroin Trafficking as ActionSA Praises Verdict

A South African woman has been sentenced to prison in Nigeria for trafficking heroin, drawing praise from ActionSA president Herman Mashaba for the decisive legal action.

A 38-year-old South African woman, Will Jessica Ann (also referred to as Jessica Will), has been sentenced to 25 years in prison by the Federal High Court in Abuja for importing 5.75 kilogrammes of heroin into Nigeria. The ruling and subsequent political reactions have drawn significant attention to international drug interdiction efforts.

Arrest and Court Proceedings

The case dates back to July 6, when the National Drug Law Enforcement Agency (NDLEA) arrested Ann at the Nnamdi Azikiwe International Airport in Abuja. She had arrived on a Qatar Airways flight from Doha, travelling with her three-year-old son.

According to the NDLEA, Ann initially denied having any checked-in luggage. However, operatives quickly established that two suitcases containing 14 large blocks of heroin bore baggage tags matching the claim tags attached to her passport. She subsequently recanted, admitting ownership of the bags and claiming she had travelled from Cambodia through Doha.

In August, Ann was arraigned before Justice Obiora Atuegwu Egwuatu, where she pleaded guilty to a two-count charge bordering on the unlawful importation of 5.75kg of heroin and conspiracy, punishable under Section 14 of the National Drug Law Enforcement Agency Act. During the proceedings, the prosecution noted that the street value of the heroin was approximately N18 million per kilogramme. Pleading for leniency, Ann expressed remorse, stated that she did not know illicit drugs were in the bags, and promised never to repeat the offense.

On Thursday, Justice Egwuatu delivered judgment, holding that a conviction legally follows a guilty plea and that the defendant had admitted to the crime in her extrajudicial statement. Stressing that Nigerian law does not respect emotions and that "crime does not pay," the judge sentenced her to 15 years’ imprisonment on the first count and 25 years on the second count. The sentences were ordered to run concurrently, meaning she will serve a maximum of 25 years. The court also ruled that the heroin is to be destroyed if no appeal is filed within the statutory period.

Broader Network and Reactions

Investigations by the NDLEA linked Ann to a transnational drug trafficking network operating along the Cambodian-South African axis alongside her husband, Jan Coenraad De Jager. De Jager was subsequently arrested in Johannesburg on July 27 while allegedly attempting to traffic 3.2kg of illicit drugs to Hong Kong.

NDLEA Chairman Brig. Gen. Mohamed Buba Marwa (retd.) stated that the conviction demonstrates the judiciary's support for the agency's anti-narcotics campaigns and reinforces Nigeria's stance against serving as a soft landing for international drug cartels.

The verdict also elicited international political commentary. ActionSA president and Johannesburg mayoral candidate Herman Mashaba praised Nigeria's decisive legal action, contrasting it unfavorably with his own country's justice system regarding transnational narcotics smugglers.
