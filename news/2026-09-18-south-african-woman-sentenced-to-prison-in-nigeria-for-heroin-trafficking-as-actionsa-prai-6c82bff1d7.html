South African Woman Sentenced to Prison in Nigeria for Heroin Trafficking as ActionSA Praises Verdict

A South African woman has been sentenced to prison in Nigeria for trafficking heroin, drawing praise from ActionSA president Herman Mashaba for the decisive legal action.

A Federal High Court in Abuja has sentenced 38-year-old South African national Jessica Will (also referred to as Will Jessica Ann) to 25 years in prison for importing 5.75 kilogrammes of heroin into Nigeria. The ruling concluded a legal case brought forward by the National Drug Law Enforcement Agency (NDLEA) following her arrest at the Nnamdi Azikiwe International Airport in Abuja.

Arrest and Legal Proceedings

The defendant was taken into custody on July 6, 2026, by NDLEA operatives during the inward clearance of passengers on Qatar Airways flight QR1433 arriving from Doha. Traveling alongside her three-year-old son, she initially denied checking in any luggage. However, security personnel discovered that two suitcases containing 14 large blocks of heroin bore baggage tags matching the claim tags attached to her passport. She subsequently recanted, admitting ownership of the bags and stating she had travelled from Cambodia through Doha.

In August, the NDLEA arraigned her before Justice Obiora Egwuatu on a two-count charge bordering on conspiracy and unlawful importation under the NDLEA Act. She entered a guilty plea, which was supported by an extrajudicial statement admitting to the offence. During sentencing on Thursday, Justice Egwuatu emphasized that Nigerian law does not operate on emotions and that crime does not pay. The judge sentenced her to 15 years' imprisonment on the first count and 25 years' imprisonment on the second count. The terms will run concurrently, meaning she will serve a maximum of 25 years behind bars. The court also ordered that the heroin be destroyed if no appeal is filed within the legally prescribed timeframe.

Broader Investigation and Official Reactions

According to the NDLEA, further investigations linked Will to a transnational drug trafficking network operating along the Cambodian-South African corridor. Authorities stated she conspired with a South African man, Jan Coenraad De Jager. De Jager was subsequently arrested in Johannesburg on July 27 while allegedly attempting to traffic 3.2kg of illicit drugs to Hong Kong.

NDLEA Chairman and Chief Executive Officer, retired Brig. Gen. Mohamed Buba Marwa, stated that the conviction demonstrates the judiciary's backing of the agency's anti-narcotics campaigns and reinforces that Nigeria will not serve as a soft landing for international cartels.

Political Reactions

The verdict drew public commentary from South African politician Herman Mashaba. The ActionSA president and Johannesburg mayoral candidate praised Nigerian authorities, contrasting their decisive legal action with what he characterized as shortcomings within South Africa's own justice system regarding transnational drug networks.
