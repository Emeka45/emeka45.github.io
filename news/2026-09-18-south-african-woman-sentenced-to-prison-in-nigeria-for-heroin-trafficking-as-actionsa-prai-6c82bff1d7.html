South African Woman Sentenced to Prison in Nigeria for Heroin Trafficking as ActionSA Praises Verdict

A South African woman has been sentenced to prison in Nigeria for trafficking heroin, drawing praise from ActionSA president Herman Mashaba for the decisive legal action.

A 38-year-old South African woman, Will Jessica Ann (also referred to as Jessica Will), has been sentenced to 25 years in prison by the Federal High Court in Abuja for importing 5.75 kilogrammes of heroin into Nigeria. The case, which drew attention from international observers and regional political figures, highlights ongoing efforts by Nigerian authorities to crack down on transnational drug trafficking networks operating through major aviation hubs.

Arrest at Nnamdi Azikiwe International Airport

The case began on 6 July, when National Drug Law Enforcement Agency (NDLEA) operatives intercepted Ms. Ann during the inward clearance of passengers on Qatar Airways flight QR 1433 arriving from Doha. According to the NDLEA, she was travelling with her three-year-old son and initially denied having any checked-in luggage.

However, airport officers quickly discovered that baggage tags on two suitcases containing 14 large blocks of heroin matched the claim tags attached to her passport. She subsequently recanted her statement, admitting ownership of the bags and claiming she had forgotten that she checked them in. Investigators stated that she had travelled from Cambodia through Doha before arriving in Abuja.

Trial and Sentencing by the Federal High Court

In August, the NDLEA arraigned Ms. Ann before Justice Obiora Egwuatu on a two-count charge bordering on conspiracy and the unlawful importation of 5.75kg of heroin, punishable under Section 14 of the National Drug Law Enforcement Agency Act, Cap N30, Laws of the Federation of Nigeria, 2004. She pleaded guilty to the charges.

During the proceedings, the prosecution noted that the street value of heroin is approximately N18 million per kilogramme and urged the court to factor this into the sentencing. In her plea for leniency, Ms. Ann expressed remorse, promised never to engage in such acts again, and maintained that she did not know illicit drugs were inside the bags.

On Thursday, Justice Egwuatu delivered his judgement, stating that a conviction naturally follows a guilty plea supported by a defendant's extrajudicial admissions. The judge emphasized that the law is not influenced by emotions and that "crime does not pay." Justice Egwuatu sentenced her to 15 years' imprisonment on the first count and 25 years' imprisonment on the second count. The sentences are to run concurrently, meaning she will serve a maximum of 25 years behind bars. Furthermore, the court ruled that the seized heroin is to be destroyed if no appeal is filed within the legally prescribed timeframe.

Broader Connections and Official Reactions

The NDLEA linked Ms. Ann to a wider transnational drug trafficking network operating along the Cambodian-South African axis. According to the agency, further intelligence-led investigations connected her to an alleged co-conspirator, Jan Coenraad De Jager, who was subsequently arrested in Johannesburg, South Africa, on 27 July while attempting to traffic 3.2kg of illicit drugs to Hong Kong.

NDLEA Chairman and Chief Executive Officer, retired Brig. Gen. Mohamed Buba Marwa, stated that the conviction demonstrates the judiciary's backing of the agency's mandate. He reiterated that the outcome reinforces the position that Nigeria will not serve as a soft landing for international drug cartels, emphasizing the role of intelligence-sharing with international partners.

The sentencing also drew commentary from regional political figures. ActionSA president and Johannesburg mayoral candidate Herman Mashaba praised the decisive legal action taken by Nigerian authorities, contrasting it with shortcomings in South Africa's domestic justice and law enforcement response to transnational smuggling operations.
