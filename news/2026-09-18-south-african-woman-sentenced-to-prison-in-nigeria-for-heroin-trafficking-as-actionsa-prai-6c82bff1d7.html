South African Woman Sentenced to Prison in Nigeria for Heroin Trafficking as ActionSA Praises Verdict

A South African woman has been sentenced to prison in Nigeria for trafficking heroin, drawing praise from ActionSA president Herman Mashaba for the decisive legal action.

A 38-year-old South African woman, Will Jessica Ann, has been sentenced by the Federal High Court in Abuja to 25 years in prison for smuggling 5.75 kilogrammes of heroin into Nigeria. The ruling, delivered by Justice Obiora Egwuatu, drew praise from ActionSA president Herman Mashaba, who commended the decisive legal outcome.

Arrest and Court Proceedings

Ann was arrested on July 6, 2026, by operatives of the National Drug Law Enforcement Agency (NDLEA) at the Nnamdi Azikiwe International Airport in Abuja. She had arrived on a Qatar Airways flight from Doha, travelling with her three-year-old son. According to investigators, Ann initially denied carrying any checked-in luggage, but officers established that two suitcases containing 14 large blocks of heroin bore baggage claim tags matching her passport. She subsequently admitted ownership of the bags.

In August, the NDLEA arraigned Ann before the court on a two-count charge bordering on conspiracy and unlawful importation under the NDLEA Act. She pleaded guilty to the charges. During the trial, the prosecution noted that the street value of heroin is approximately N18 million per kilogramme. Pleading for leniency, Ann expressed remorse, promised not to repeat the offense, and claimed she was unaware that illicit drugs were inside the bags.

Justice Egwuatu stated that the law is settled that a conviction follows a guilty plea and emphasized that judicial decisions are not swayed by emotions. He sentenced her to 15 years’ imprisonment on the first count and 25 years on the second count, with the terms to run concurrently, resulting in a maximum prison sentence of 25 years. The judge also ordered the destruction of the heroin if the convict fails to appeal within the legally prescribed period.

Connections to Transnational Drug Networks

According to the NDLEA, Ann stated that she had traveled from Cambodia through Doha prior to reaching Abuja. Investigations further linked her to an alleged transnational drug trafficking syndicate along the Cambodian-South African corridor, involving a male partner identified as Jan Coenraad De Jager. Authorities reported that De Jager was subsequently arrested in Johannesburg, South Africa, on July 27 while attempting to traffic 3.2kg of illicit drugs to Hong Kong.

NDLEA Chairman Buba Marwa stated that the conviction demonstrates the judiciary's robust support for anti-narcotics efforts and reinforces the agency's stance that Nigeria will not function as a safe haven for international drug cartels. Marwa credited the operation's success to intelligence-led strategies and international cooperation.

Political Reactions

The outcome prompted reactions from political figures abroad. ActionSA president and Johannesburg mayoral candidate Herman Mashaba praised the Nigerian judiciary for taking firm legal action. Writing on X, Mashaba contrasted the swift conviction with shortcomings in South Africa's domestic legal response to drug trafficking, stating that Nigeria acted decisively against illicit networks affecting communities and youth.
