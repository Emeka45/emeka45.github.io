South African Woman Sentenced to Prison in Nigeria for Heroin Trafficking as ActionSA Praises Verdict

A South African woman has been sentenced to prison in Nigeria for trafficking heroin, drawing praise from ActionSA president Herman Mashaba for the decisive legal action.

A Federal High Court in Abuja has sentenced a 38-year-old South African woman, Will Jessica Ann (also referred to as Jessica Will), to 25 years in prison for importing 5.75 kilogrammes of heroin into Nigeria. The ruling drew sharp praise from South African politician Herman Mashaba, who commended Nigerian authorities for executing decisive legal action against transnational drug trafficking.

Arrest and Trial Details

The defendant was arrested on July 6 at the Nnamdi Azikiwe International Airport in Abuja by operatives of the National Drug Law Enforcement Agency (NDLEA). According to the agency, she arrived on a Qatar Airways flight from Doha, travelling with her three-year-old son, and initially denied having any checked-in luggage.

Operatives subsequently discovered that two suitcases containing 14 large blocks of heroin bore baggage tags matching the claim tags attached to her passport. She then recanted, admitting ownership of the luggage and stating that she had travelled from Cambodia through Doha to Abuja. The NDLEA also linked her to an international drug trafficking network operating along the Cambodian-South African route, noting that her partner, Jan Coenraad De Jager, was later arrested in Johannesburg on July 27 while attempting to traffic illicit drugs to Hong Kong.

During her trial before Justice Obiora Egwuatu, the defendant pleaded guilty to a two-count charge of conspiracy and unlawful importation under the NDLEA Act. While the prosecution noted that heroin carries a street value of approximately N18 million per kilogramme and the judge observed that the law does not operate on emotions, the defendant begged for leniency, expressed remorse, and promised not to reoffend.

Sentencing and Reactions

Justice Egwuatu sentenced her to 15 years’ imprisonment on the first count and 25 years’ imprisonment on the second count, with the terms ordered to run concurrently. The court further ruled that the seized heroin should be destroyed if no appeal is filed within the legally prescribed timeframe.

NDLEA Chairman Buba Marwa welcomed the judgment, stating that it highlights the judiciary’s backing of the agency’s mandate and demonstrates that Nigeria will not provide a safe haven for international cartels. Meanwhile, ActionSA president Herman Mashaba reacted strongly to the outcome on social media, praising Nigerian authorities for taking firm steps against cross-border drug smuggling as reported by The Punch.
