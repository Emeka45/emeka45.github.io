South African Woman Sentenced to Prison in Nigeria for Heroin Trafficking as ActionSA Praises Verdict

A South African woman has been sentenced to prison in Nigeria for trafficking heroin, drawing praise from ActionSA president Herman Mashaba for the decisive legal action.

A 38-year-old South African woman, Jessica Will (also referred to in court documents as Will Jessica Ann), has been sentenced to 25 years in prison by the Federal High Court in Abuja for importing 5.75 kilogrammes of heroin into Nigeria. The ruling has drawn strong reactions and praise from political figures, notably ActionSA president Herman Mashaba.

Arrest and Court Proceedings

The defendant was arrested on 6 July by operatives of the National Drug Law Enforcement Agency (NDLEA) at the Nnamdi Azikiwe International Airport in Abuja. She arrived on a Qatar Airways flight from Doha, travelling through Cambodia, and was accompanied by her three-year-old son. Initially, she denied having checked-in baggage, but officers discovered that bag tags matched the claim tags on her passport. She subsequently admitted the luggage belonged to her.

During her arraignment, she pleaded guilty to two counts brought against her by the NDLEA regarding the unlawful importation and conspiracy. Presiding Justice Obiora Egwuatu noted that the law does not respect emotions and that crime does not pay. The judge sentenced her to 15 years on the first count and 25 years on the second count, with both sentences ordered to run concurrently. The court also ruled that the seized heroin is to be destroyed if no appeal is filed within the legal timeframe.

Transnational Network and NDLEA Response

According to the NDLEA, investigations linked Ms Will to a broader transnational drug trafficking network operating along the Cambodian-South African corridor. Her partner, Jan Coenraad De Jager, was subsequently arrested in Johannesburg, South Africa, on 27 July while allegedly attempting to traffic 3.2kg of illicit drugs to Hong Kong.

NDLEA Chairman Buba Marwa stated that the conviction demonstrates the judiciary's backing of the agency's anti-narcotics campaigns, reinforcing the stance that Nigeria will not serve as a soft landing for international cartels. Further details on the agency's operations can be found via The Punch and Vanguard.

Political Reactions

ActionSA President Herman Mashaba publicly commended Nigerian authorities for the decisive legal outcome. Comparing the swift judicial response to domestic challenges in South Africa, Mashaba praised Nigeria for taking firm action against illicit drug operations.
