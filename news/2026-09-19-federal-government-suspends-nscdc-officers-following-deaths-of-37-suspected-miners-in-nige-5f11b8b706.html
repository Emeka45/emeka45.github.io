Federal Government Suspends NSCDC Officers Following Deaths of 37 Suspected Miners in Niger State Custody

The Federal Government has suspended 20 officers of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps alongside the Niger State Commandant following the tragic deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners held in custody. The Nigerian Bar Association has also demanded thorough probes into the incident and the financiers of the illegal mining activities.

The Federal Government has suspended 20 officers of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC), alongside the Niger State Commandant, following the tragic deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners held in custody. The incident, which occurred in Minna, Niger State, has drawn widespread condemnation and urgent demands for transparency and accountability from legal bodies and civil society.

Government Action and Investigation Panel

Following a directive by President Bola Tinubu for a comprehensive investigation, Minister of Interior Olubunmi Tunji Ojo announced the suspension of the personnel on Saturday. The suspended officials include the Niger State Commandant, Suberu Aniviye, as well as 20 other officers involved in station guard, arrest, investigation, intelligence, legal, and guard duties.

To investigate the incident, the Federal Government constituted a 10-member independent investigative committee chaired by retired Department of State Services Deputy Director General Jonathan Kure, with former Nigerian Law School Director General Prof. Isa Hayatu Chiroma serving as secretary. Other committee members include retired AIG Hosea Hassan Karma, Prof. Olayinka Buhari, representatives of the Minna Emirate Council and Niger State Government, Miners Association of Nigeria National Secretary Liman Sulaiman, human rights activist Deji Adeyanju, journalist Zainab Suleiman Okino, and public affairs analyst George Agbakahi.

The panel has been given two weeks to complete its assignment. Its mandate includes establishing the identities of the deceased, determining the cause and circumstances of the deaths, investigating the conditions of their arrest and detention, and assigning responsibility for any negligence or misconduct. The Minister of Interior warned that any obstruction, intimidation, or attempts to conceal evidence would be treated as a serious offence.

Nigerian Bar Association Demands Broader Probes

Reacting to the tragedy, the Nigerian Bar Association (NBA) called for an independent, transparent, and credible probe into the deaths, which occurred in the early hours of Thursday, September 17, 2026. While Reuters reported that 33 bodies had been deposited at the General Hospital in Minna, Niger State Governor Mohammed Umaru Bago confirmed the death toll at 37.

In a statement issued by its President, Oyinkansola Badejo-Okusanya, the NBA stressed that the deceased were merely suspects rather than convicts, emphasizing that the state must uphold detainees' rights regardless of their social standing. The association urged investigators to examine facility conditions, overcrowding, ventilation, and the availability of medical attention, as well as investigate reports that some detainees cried out for help while struggling to breathe.

Furthermore, the NBA urged authorities to extend the scope of enforcement beyond the labourers to unmask the owners, financiers, and commercial beneficiaries of the illegal mining operations. The association also raised concerns over reports that children may have been among those detained and urged authorities to determine who placed minors at the mining sites. The NBA has deployed an independent team led by Senior Advocate of Nigeria Jonathan Gunu Taidi to engage with victims' families and survivors to ensure all material evidence is preserved.
