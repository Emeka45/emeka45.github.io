South African Woman Sentenced to 25 Years Imprisonment for Trafficking 5.75kg of Heroin into Nigeria

A Federal court in Nigeria has sentenced a South African woman to 25 years in prison after she was convicted of importing 5.75 kilograms of heroin, a ruling that has drawn regional attention and praise.

A Federal High Court in Abuja has sentenced a 38-year-old South African woman, Will Jessica Ann (also referred to as Jessica Will), to 25 years in imprisonment for smuggling 5.75 kilogrammes of heroin into Nigeria. The ruling was delivered on Thursday by Justice Obiora Egwuatu (also noted as Justice Obiora Atuegwu Egwuatu) following a guilty plea entered by the defendant.

Arrest and Investigation at the Airport

The National Drug Law Enforcement Agency (NDLEA) arrested Ann on July 6 during the inward clearance of passengers on a Qatar Airways flight (QR1433) from Doha. She had travelled from Cambodia through Doha to Abuja, accompanied by her three-year-old son.

According to the NDLEA, Ann initially denied travelling with any checked-in luggage. However, operatives established that two suitcases containing 14 large blocks of heroin bore baggage tags matching the claim tags attached to her passport. She subsequently recanted, admitted ownership of the bags, and claimed she had forgotten that she checked them in.

Court Proceedings and Sentencing

In August, the NDLEA arraigned Ann before the Federal High Court on a two-count charge bordering on conspiracy and unlawful importation, punishable under Section 14 of the National Drug Law Enforcement Agency Act, Cap N30, Laws of the Federation of Nigeria, 2004. Before entering her guilty plea, she admitted to the offence in her extrajudicial statement and also admitted to conspiring with a South African man, Jan Coenraad De Jager.

During the sentencing phase, the prosecution noted that one kilogramme of heroin has a street value of about N18 million. Speaking in mitigation, Ann expressed remorse, promised not to engage in the act again, and claimed she was unaware there were illicit drugs in the bags.

Justice Egwuatu held that a conviction legally follows a guilty plea, stating that "crime does not pay" and that the law is not influenced by emotions. The judge sentenced her to 15 years' imprisonment on Count 1 and 25 years' imprisonment on Count 2, with the sentences ordered to run concurrently. She will serve a maximum of 25 years behind bars. The court also ruled that if the convict does not appeal within the prescribed period, the seized heroin is to be destroyed.

Broader Network and Reactions

Investigations linked Ann to a transnational drug trafficking network operating along the Cambodian-South African axis alongside her partner, Mr. De Jager. According to the NDLEA, De Jager was arrested in Johannesburg, South Africa, on July 27 while allegedly attempting to traffic 3.2kg of illicit drugs to Hong Kong.

Reacting to the judgment, NDLEA Chairman Brig. Gen. Mohamed Buba Marwa (retd.) stated that the conviction demonstrates the judiciary's robust support for the agency and reinforces that Nigeria will not serve as a soft landing for international drug cartels. The case also drew international attention, with ActionSA president and Johannesburg mayoral candidate Herman Mashaba praising Nigeria's justice system for the conviction.
