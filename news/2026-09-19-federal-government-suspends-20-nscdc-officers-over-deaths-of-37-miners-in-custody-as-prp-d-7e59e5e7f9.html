Federal Government Suspends 20 NSCDC Officers Over Deaths of 37 Miners in Custody as PRP Demands Justice

The Federal Government has suspended 20 Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC) officers and the Niger State Commandant following the tragic deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody, prompting strong demands for a thorough inquiry from political parties.

The Federal Government has suspended 20 officers of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC) alongside the Niger State Commandant following the tragic deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody, as political groups demand thorough accountability. The incident, which occurred on Thursday, September 17, 2026, has sparked widespread concern and drawn strong reactions from political parties.

Government Action and Investigation Panel

Following a directive from President Bola Tinubu for a comprehensive and unhindered investigation, Minister of Interior Olubunmi Tunji-Ojo announced the suspension of the Niger State NSCDC Commandant, Suberu Aniviye, and 20 other personnel involved in station guard, arrest, investigation, intelligence, legal, and guard duties (The Reporter). Reports indicate that the Commandant and 21 officers were detained in Abuja in connection with the incident (The Punch).

To examine the tragedy, the Federal Government constituted a 10-member independent committee chaired by retired Department of State Services Deputy Director General Jonathan Kure, with former Nigerian Law School Director General Prof. Isa Hayatu Chiroma serving as secretary (The Reporter). Other committee members include retired AIG Hosea Hassan Karma, Prof. Olayinka Buhari, representatives of the Minna Emirate Council and the Niger State Government, Miners Association of Nigeria National Secretary Liman Sulaiman, human rights lawyer Deji Adeyanju, journalist Zainab Suleiman Okino, and public affairs analyst George Agbakahi (The Reporter). The panel has been given two weeks to establish the identities of the deceased, determine the cause of death, investigate the circumstances surrounding their detention, and assign responsibility for any negligence or misconduct (The Reporter).

Disputed Causes and Conflicting Accounts

The precise cause of the 37 deaths remains under intense scrutiny, with multiple conflicting accounts emerging regarding the conditions inside the holding facility (The Punch). While the NSCDC attributed the fatalities to a disease outbreak without specifying a precise illness, survivors fiercely countered this claim, stating that the detainees perished from severe suffocation and dehydration inside an overcrowded cell (The Punch). Furthermore, Niger State Governor Mohammed Bago noted that preliminary forensic findings identified foam mixed with bloodstains in the mouths of several victims alongside peeling skin, which introduces the possibility of lead poisoning (The Punch).

Political Reactions and Broader Security Concerns

The tragedy has drawn sharp political criticism. The Peoples Redemption Party (PRP) issued a statement in Abuja through its National Publicity Secretary, Muhammed Ishaq, demanding justice for the deceased miners and calling for the immediate prosecution of anyone found culpable (The Punch). The PRP asserted that the incident reflects broader governance failures regarding the protection of citizens' lives and property (The Punch).

In the same statement, the party also addressed ongoing security challenges in the region, condemning the continued captivity of residents from the Dekara community in Borgu Local Government Area of Niger State, who were abducted by bandits in August 2026 (The Punch). The PRP urged authorities to intensify rescue operations, hold security personnel accountable for operational failures, and review policies aimed at safeguarding the North-Central and North-West regions from unchecked insecurity (The Punch).
