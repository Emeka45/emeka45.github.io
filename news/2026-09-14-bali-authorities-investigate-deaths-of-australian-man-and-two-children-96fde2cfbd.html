Bali Authorities Investigate Deaths of Australian Man and Two Children

Indonesian police are investigating the deaths of an Australian man and his two children in Bali following a welfare check requested by concerned family members.

Indonesian police in Bali have launched an investigation following the tragic deaths of an Australian man and his two young children inside a bungalow located in a popular tourist destination. The incident, which has drawn profound concern, is being examined by authorities as a suspected murder-suicide, according to Australian media reports.

Discovery of the Victims

The bodies were discovered on Sunday inside a home situated in Kuta. Law enforcement identified the adult victim as 56-year-old Damien John Shaw, originally from Fremantle in Western Australia. He was found alongside his two children: a six-year-old boy and a four-year-old girl.

According to details provided by police, the sequence of events began when the children's mother became alarmed during a phone call with her husband after hearing one of the children crying. The wife, who was not in Bali at the time and had reportedly traveled to the capital city of Jakarta in the preceding days, subsequently found herself unable to reach her husband again.

Welfare Check and Police Response

Prompted by her mounting concerns, the man's wife contacted a neighbor to request a welfare check. Accompanied by other local residents, the neighbor went to the property. After receiving no response to their repeated knocks, the group felt compelled to force their way inside the bungalow, where they ultimately uncovered the three deceased victims.

While local media outlets have pointed toward a suspected murder-suicide framework, law enforcement officials told the BBC that they have not yet officially determined the precise cause or manner of death. Further medical and forensic examinations are scheduled to be carried out to establish the exact circumstances surrounding the tragedy.

Official Reactions

The grim discovery has sent ripples of grief back to Australia. Western Australian Premier Roger Cook addressed the unfolding situation publicly, characterizing it as deeply distressing. "This is a very sad situation; our hearts go out to everyone involved," Cook stated, adding an expression of hope that investigators would be able "to get to the bottom of what's gone on as soon as possible." As the investigation continues, authorities in Bali are expected to release further details once additional forensic evaluations are finalized.
