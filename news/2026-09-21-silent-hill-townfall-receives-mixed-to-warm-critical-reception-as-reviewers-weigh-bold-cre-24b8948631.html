Silent Hill: Townfall Receives Mixed to Warm Critical Reception as Reviewers Weigh Bold Creative Swings

Konami's latest survival horror title, Silent Hill: Townfall, has launched on PlayStation 5 and PC, drawing a range of assessments from major gaming publications that contrast it with recent franchise entries like 2025's Silent Hill f.

Konami's survival horror revival continues with Silent Hill: Townfall, developed by Scottish studio Screen Burn for the PlayStation 5 and PC platforms. Released on September 24, the title has sparked a range of assessments across major gaming publications, earning a positive Metacritic score of 81 and an OpenCritic rating of 82. While critics have widely praised the game's meticulous atmosphere and bold creative directions, many have expressed reservations regarding its stealth mechanics and pacing.

A Distinctly Realised Setting and Tone

Set in 1996, Townfall shifts the franchise's traditional fog-shrouded environment to St. Amelia, a fictional remote island fishing village in the North Sea. Reviewers highlighted the setting's immersive detail, noting elements such as weather-battered sea fronts, local noticeboards, British street signs, and brutalist architecture. The narrative centers on a confused protagonist named Simon Ordell, who wakes up with an IV bag attached to his shoulder and eventually crosses paths with other playable characters, including Zoe and Richard.

Unlike previous franchise entries that rely heavily on ambiguity, Townfall incorporates social realism into its horror. The storyline grapples directly with themes of corporate colonization, worker exploitation, and community tragedy, drawing comparisons by critics like Matt Wales at Eurogamer to ambient titles like Everybody's Gone to the Rapture.

Gameplay Mechanics and Critical Division

As the first mainline-style entry in the series to adopt a first-person perspective, Townfall replaces traditional third-person action with slow-paced, violence-avoidant stealth. Players utilize a handheld CRT device—dubbed a CRTV—to track enemy silhouettes through static signals, echoing mechanics from cult classic horror games like Siren. Combat is limited, relying on clumsy blocks, improvised melee tools like wooden planks, and a late-game revolver with slow reload times.

While IGN's Tristan Ogilvie awarded the game an 8/10 and praised its oppressive atmosphere and challenging puzzles, other reviewers found fault with the moment-to-moment mechanics. Writing for Polygon, Elijah Gonzalez noted that the initial tension of evading fast-moving monsters eventually gives way to repetitive routines and frustrating, unkillable enemy encounters. VGC critic Chris Scullion similarly pointed to instant-death stealth segments as momentum-halting obstacles.

Context Within the Franchise

Townfall arrives following the release of 2025's rural Japan-set Silent Hill f, which earned a higher Metacritic score of 86. While some reviewers felt that Townfall spells out its thematic messaging a bit too plainly compared to earlier classics like Silent Hill 2, the title is nonetheless recognized as a significant, ambitious step forward for Konami's ongoing survival horror resurgence.
