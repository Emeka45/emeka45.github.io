Silent Hill: Townfall Receives Mixed to Warm Critical Reception as Reviewers Weigh Bold Creative Swings

Konami's latest survival horror title, Silent Hill: Townfall, has launched on PlayStation 5 and PC, drawing a range of assessments from major gaming publications that contrast it with recent franchise entries like 2025's Silent Hill f.

Konami's survival horror title Silent Hill: Townfall has launched on PlayStation 5 and PC, drawing a range of assessments from major gaming publications that contrast it with recent franchise entries like 2025's Silent Hill f. Developed by Scottish indie studio Screen Burn—known for Observation and Stories Untold—the game shifts the franchise's setting to 1996 in St. Amelia, a remote island town in the North Sea.

Critical Reception and Ratings

Critical response to the title has been mixed to warm, with reviewers praising its striking atmosphere, detailed setting, and ambitious storytelling while expressing frustration with certain mechanical elements. On Metacritic, Silent Hill: Townfall holds an 81 rating, while OpenCritic places it at 82, falling somewhat short of the 86 rating achieved by 2025's Silent Hill f.

Publications offered varied perspectives on the experience. IGN awarded the game an 8/10, with Tristan Ogilvie highlighting its oppressive atmosphere and clever puzzles involving mid-nineties technology. Eurogamer's Matt Wales described the title as ambitious and deeply atmospheric in a four-star review, while PC Gamer's Elie Gould awarded it an 89 rating, praising the inclusion of multiple playable characters.

However, not all reviews were glowing. VGC's Chris Scullion issued a three-star review criticizing frustrating instant-death stealth sequences, and Polygon's Elijah Gonzalez noted that the horror elements eventually lose their impact due to repetitive creature encounters and overly explicit themes.

Atmosphere, Setting, and Narrative Themes

Reviewers widely lauded Screen Burn's evocation of St. Amelia. The location features distinct British street signs, local noticeboard advertisements, brutalist architecture, and a persistent coastal fog. Rather than relying solely on supernatural threats, the narrative incorporates social realism, touching upon themes of corporate colonization, worker exploitation, and personal trauma following a local tragedy.

The story follows Simon Ordell, a confused protagonist who wakes up in the harbor with an IV drip attached to his arm. Unlike traditional franchise entries focused entirely on a single perspective, players also control other characters such as Zoe and Richard, adding narrative variety to the investigation of the town's dark history.

Gameplay and Mechanical Shifts

Townfall marks the first time a main-line or major Silent Hill title adopts a first-person perspective, creating an intimate and vulnerable viewpoint. Players must rely heavily on stealth, distraction items like glass bottles, and a portable CRT device that detects nearby enemy signals through static hiss. Combat is limited, relying on clumsy blocks, melee swings with deteriorating wooden planks, and a late-game revolver with slow reload times that risks drawing enemy attention.

While early stealth encounters praised for initial tension, several critics felt that predictable monster patrols and unyielding foes eventually drained momentum.
