Silent Hill: Townfall Receives Mixed to Warm Critical Reception as Reviewers Weigh Bold Creative Swings

Konami's latest survival horror title, Silent Hill: Townfall, has launched on PlayStation 5 and PC, drawing a range of assessments from major gaming publications that contrast it with recent franchise entries like 2025's Silent Hill f.

Konami's survival horror franchise has expanded with the release of Silent Hill: Townfall, developed by Scottish studio Screen Burn for the PlayStation 5 and PC. Landing on September 24, the title has drawn a range of assessments from major gaming publications, earning an 81 on Metacritic and an 82 on OpenCritic. While critics widely praised its atmosphere and meticulous world design, the game's reception contrasted with the stellar reviews received by 2025's Silent Hill f, which scored an 86.

A New Setting: St. Amelia

Moving away from traditional North American or rural Japanese settings, Townfall is set in 1996 in St. Amelia, a remote island fishing village in the North Sea. Reviewers were deeply impressed by the detail of the location. Critics noted that the environment features convincing elements such as British street signs, local noticeboard advertisements, and a weather-battered seafront that transitions into stark 1970s brutalism.

Screen Burn infused the narrative with social realism, tackling themes of corporate colonization, worker exploitation, and community tragedy. The story follows a dazed protagonist named Simon Ordell, who climbs out of St. Amelia's harbor with an IV bag attached to his arm. Unlike traditional franchise entries that focus entirely on a single perspective, players also experience sections through other characters such as Zoe and Richard, adding diversity to the narrative and its exploration of interconnected grief and guilt.

First-Person Horror and Mechanics

Townfall marks the first time a main-line style Silent Hill game utilizes a first-person perspective. Reviewers noted that this viewpoint, combined with slow-paced stealth, creates initial tension and vulnerability. Players utilize a handheld CRT device—acting as a modern twist on the series' classic radio static—to detect enemy signals through walls before encountering creatures up close.

However, the gameplay mechanics divided critics. While initial monster encounters were praised as tense and dynamic, several reviewers found that the stealth sections eventually wore out their welcome. Critics pointed out that memorizing creature movement patterns turned frightening moments into repetitive routines, with some late-game stealth segments and instant-death enemies causing significant frustration.

Critical Consensus and Themes

Writing for Polygon, reviewers highlighted the contrast between the game's evocative atmosphere and its mechanical shortcomings, noting that its blunt thematic delivery lacked the ambiguous, spiraling dread of classic entries like Silent Hill 2. Meanwhile, Eurogamer praised the title's ambition, world-building, and the distinct soundscape crafted by composer Anthony Scott Burns. Additional insights and critiques were further detailed in Polygon's review coverage, emphasizing how the title's bold creative swings successfully revitalized aspects of the long-running horror property despite occasional pacing hurdles.
