Silent Hill: Townfall Receives Mixed to Warm Critical Reception as Reviewers Weigh Bold Creative Swings

Konami's latest survival horror title, Silent Hill: Townfall, has launched on PlayStation 5 and PC, drawing a range of assessments from major gaming publications that contrast it with recent franchise entries like 2025's Silent Hill f.

Konami's latest survival horror title, Silent Hill: Townfall, has launched on PlayStation 5 and PC, drawing a range of assessments from major gaming publications that contrast it with recent franchise entries like 2025's Silent Hill f. Developed by Scottish studio Screen Burn—known for Observation and Stories Untold—the game introduces a fresh setting and perspective to the long-running series, earning critical praise for its rich atmosphere while drawing mixed reactions regarding its gameplay mechanics.

Critical Reception and Ratings

Upon its September 24 release, Silent Hill: Townfall secured generally favorable review aggregate scores, landing an 81 on Metacritic and an 82 on OpenCritic. While reviewers widely commended the title's environmental storytelling, soundscape, and bold creative swings, critics noted that it could not quite match the stellar 86 Metacritic score achieved by 2025's Silent Hill f.

A Distinctly British Setting and Atmospheric World-Building

Set in 1996, Townfall shifts the franchise's geographic focus to St. Amelia, a remote island fishing village in the North Sea. Reviewers highlighted the meticulous detail of the setting, noting that elements such as British street signs, local noticeboard advertisements, and weather-battered coastal vistas create an intensely convincing and immersive space.

Rather than relying solely on traditional psychological tropes, developer Screen Burn infuses the narrative with social realism, touching upon themes of corporate colonization, worker exploitation, and the heavy toll of tragedy on local key workers. IGN's Tristan Ogilvie awarded the game an 8/10, praising its oppressively ominous atmosphere and inventive retro-tech puzzles, while Eurogamer's Matt Wales awarded it four stars for its ambient fidelity and narrative ambition.

First-Person Perspective and Stealth Mechanics

Townfall marks a major structural shift for the franchise by adopting a first-person perspective. Players step into the shoes of Simon Ordell, a dazed protagonist found with an IV drip in his arm. Due to Simon's physical frailty, the title places a heavy emphasis on slow-paced, violence-avoidant stealth rather than direct combat.

To navigate enemy-infested areas, players utilize a handheld CRT device that functions similarly to the series' classic radio static, tracking enemy signals as roiling red silhouettes. However, these mechanics drew criticism from reviewers such as VGC's Chris Scullion and Polygon's Elijah Gonzalez. Critics argued that repeated encounters with patrolling creatures and mandatory stealth sections eventually exposed repetitive patterns, transforming initial tension into frustration.

Multiple Perspectives and Narrative Structure

Expanding beyond a single viewpoint, the game features playable sections for additional characters alongside Simon, including Zoe and Richard. PC Gamer's Elie Gould, who awarded the game an 89 rating, noted that examining the curse through multiple characters adds valuable diversity to the story. However, some critics felt that the narrative's themes were laid out too plainly, lacking the ambiguous, spiraling dread characteristic of earlier entries like Silent Hill 2.
