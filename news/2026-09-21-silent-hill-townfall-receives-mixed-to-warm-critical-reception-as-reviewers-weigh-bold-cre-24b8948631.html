Silent Hill: Townfall Receives Mixed to Warm Critical Reception as Reviewers Weigh Bold Creative Swings

Konami's latest survival horror title, Silent Hill: Townfall, has launched on PlayStation 5 and PC, drawing a range of assessments from major gaming publications that contrast it with recent franchise entries like 2025's Silent Hill f.

Konami's latest survival horror title, Silent Hill: Townfall, has launched on PlayStation 5 and PC, drawing a range of assessments from major gaming publications that contrast it with recent franchise entries like 2025's Silent Hill f. Developed by Scottish studio Screen Burn—known for Observation and Stories Untold—the game shifts the iconic franchise's setting to a fictional island town in the North Sea named St. Amelia, set in 1996.

Critical Reception and Ratings

Initial reviews have been mixed to warm, with critics generally praising the title's bold creative direction and atmospheric world-building while expressing frustration with certain mechanical elements. On Metacritic, Townfall holds a positive score of 81, coming in slightly behind last year's Silent Hill f, which scored an 86. OpenCritic lists the game with a matching rating of 82.

Reviewers praised the title for its immersive atmosphere and distinct cultural lens. IGN's Tristan Ogilvie, who awarded the game an 8/10, highlighted the title's oppressive ambiance and inventive puzzles that incorporate mid-nineties technology, noting it feels like a return to form for Konami's franchise following more action-oriented entries. Eurogamer's Matt Wales awarded the game a four-star rating (88), praising the intricate detail of St. Amelia and its exploration of real historical themes, including corporate colonization and worker exploitation. PC Gamer's Elie Gould awarded an 89, commending the narrative structure for dividing playable sections across three distinct characters—Simon, Zoe, and Richard—to offer a rewarding analysis of the game's curse.

Gameplay and Mechanical Shifts

Townfall marks a major structural shift for the franchise by adopting a first-person perspective. Players control Simon Ordell, a frail protagonist who wakes up in the harbor with an IV bag attached to his arm and must rely heavily on stealth and avoidance rather than direct combat. To navigate enemy-infested territory, players utilize a handheld CRTV device that picks up enemy signals as static blips on a screen, drawing comparisons by critics to cult horror classics like Siren.

However, several reviewers found that the game's mechanics struggled to maintain momentum. VGC critic Chris Scullion awarded the game three stars, criticizing instant-death stealth sequences and momentum-halting encounters with creatures like The Weight. Polygon's Elijah Gonzalez similarly noted that initial tension during monster encounters eventually gave way to repetitive, predictable patterns and frustrating unkillable enemy sequences.

Themes and Narrative Approach

Unlike traditional entries that rely heavily on ambiguous symbolism and psychological obfuscation, critics observed that Townfall addresses its themes with unusual directness. The narrative incorporates elements of social realism, touching upon pandemic-era NHS pressures, worker exploitation, and corporate greed. While some reviewers appreciated the clarity and emotional weight of these social themes, others felt that spelling out its meanings plainly reduced the spiraling dread and interpretive mystery historically associated with classics like Silent Hill 2.
