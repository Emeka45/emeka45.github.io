Valve Opens Reservations for Steam Frame Standalone VR Headset

Valve has officially opened reservations for its new Steam Frame virtual reality headset, revealing a starting price above $1,000 for the standalone and PC-compatible device.

Valve has officially opened reservations for its new Steam Frame virtual reality headset, revealing a starting price above $1,000 for the standalone and PC-compatible device. Following initial reveals of Valve's hardware lineup last fall, the pricing and release particulars for the standalone headset have arrived, placing it near the cost of the Steam Machine gaming PC.

Pricing, Specifications, and Models

The Steam Frame comes in two primary storage configurations. The 256GB model is priced at $1,059 USD (£889 / €1,049), while the 1TB model is available for $1,299 USD (£1,089 / €1,279). Both options include two Steam Frame controllers, 16GB of Unified LPDDR5X RAM, a 4 nm Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 - ARM64 processor, and a complimentary copy of Half-Life: Alyx added to the user's library.

In terms of hardware improvements over Valve's 2019 Index headset, the Steam Frame drops external base stations in favor of on-device tracking cameras, shifts to pancake lenses, and nearly halves the weight down to 440g. Its per-eye display resolution reaches 2160x2160 with a refresh rate ranging between 72Hz and 144Hz.

Reservation Process and Availability

Valve is handling sales through a reservation system similar to the one used for the Steam Machine. Customers must sign up for a single model on the Steam store before Thursday, Sept. 17 at 1:00 p.m. EDT (6:00 p.m. BST / 10:00 a.m. Pacific Time). Following the deadline, Valve will randomize the sign-ups into a reservation queue or a waitlist. Purchase emails are scheduled to start going out on Sept. 18 to those in the reservation bucket as units become ready to ship.

To participate, users must have a Steam account in good standing with a purchase history prior to April 27, 2026, with a limit of one signup per household. Direct shipping is available in the US, Canada, United Kingdom, European Union, and Australia, while Komodo will handle distribution in Hong Kong, Japan, and Taiwan.

Standalone Play and Software Integration

Functioning as a PC running SteamOS, the Steam Frame supports both dedicated VR titles and traditional flatscreen games via Valve's Proton compatibility layer. Valve implements a verification rating system similar to the Steam Deck, featuring a curated "Great on Frame" store page to highlight titles optimized for standalone play. Additionally, the device supports direct streaming from a user's PC library.

Accessories and Optional Upgrades

Neither model includes a dedicated power supply in the box, though a Steam Deck charger or equivalent works, and a 45W power supply is sold separately for $29. Additional first-party add-ons include the Ergonomic Accessories Kit for $59 and an Accessory Replacement Kit for $49. Third-party partners are also offering hardware enhancements, such as Zenni Optical prescription lenses and Arcturus's full-colour passthrough camera upgrade.
