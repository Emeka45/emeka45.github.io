Valve Opens Reservations for Steam Frame Standalone VR Headset

Valve has officially opened reservations for its new Steam Frame virtual reality headset, revealing a starting price above $1,000 for the standalone and PC-compatible device.

Valve has officially opened reservations for its new Steam Frame standalone virtual reality headset, revealing a starting price above $1,000 for the standalone and PC-compatible device. The launch marks Valve's first VR headset since the release of the Index in 2019.

Pricing and Hardware Configurations

The Steam Frame is available in two primary storage configurations. The 256GB model is priced at $1,059 USD (£889 / €1,049), while the 1TB model costs $1,299 USD (£1,089 / €1,279). Both versions come equipped with two Steam Frame controllers, 16GB of Unified LPDDR5X RAM, a 4 nm Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 ARM64 processor, and a bundled copy of Half-Life: Alyx. Neither model includes a dedicated power supply in the box, though a 45W power supply is available separately for $29 (£39 / €39).

First-party optional accessories include an Ergonomic Accessories Kit priced at $59 (£59 / €69) which features controller hand straps, battery doors, a headset top strap, and an extended light blocker. An Accessory Replacement Kit containing a face gasket, head cushion, and standard light blocker is available for $49 (£49 / €59). Additionally, third-party partners are supporting the launch, with Zenni Optical offering snap-in prescription lenses for around $67.95 a pair and Arcturus offering a full-colour camera upgrade for $149 (£129 / €157).

Reservation Process and Availability

Valve is handling sales of the Steam Frame via a reservation system similar to the Steam Machine. Customers must sign up for a single model by Thursday, Sept. 17 at 1:00 p.m. EDT (6 p.m. BST / 10 a.m. Pacific Time). Following the deadline, Valve will conduct a one-time randomisation to sort applicants into a reservation queue or a waitlist. Customers will receive notification emails starting Sept. 18. Shipping is direct from Steam in the US, Canada, United Kingdom, European Union, and Australia, while Komodo handles distribution in Hong Kong, Japan, and Taiwan.

Standalone Capabilities and Ecosystem

Designed to expand the VR market, the Steam Frame emphasizes a seamless transition between virtual reality titles and traditional flat-screen games, movies, and web browsing. Operating on SteamOS, the headset allows users to access their entire Steam library and install non-Steam applications. Valve has implemented a verification system similar to the Steam Deck, featuring a curated "Great on Frame" page to designate verified standalone titles.
