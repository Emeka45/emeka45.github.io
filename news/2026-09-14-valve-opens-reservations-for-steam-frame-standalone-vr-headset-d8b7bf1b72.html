Valve Opens Reservations for Steam Frame Standalone VR Headset

Valve has officially opened reservations for its new Steam Frame virtual reality headset, revealing a starting price above $1,000 for the standalone and PC-compatible device.

Valve Opens Steam Frame Reservations

Valve has officially opened reservations for the Steam Frame, its long-awaited standalone and PC-compatible virtual reality headset. Marking Valve's first VR hardware release since the 2019 Index, the device carries a starting price above $1,000.

The Steam Frame is available in two main storage configurations. The 256GB model is priced at $1,059 (or £889 / €1,049), while the 1TB version costs $1,299 (or £1,089 / €1,279). Both packages include two Steam Frame controllers, 16GB of Unified LPDDR5X RAM, and a 4 nm Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 ARM64 processor. Buyers also receive a copy of Half-Life: Alyx, an attached rear battery pack, a spacer, a charging cable, and a USB streaming dongle.

Reservation Process and Availability

Valve is managing sales through a reservation system similar to the approach used for its Steam Machine. Interested customers must sign up for a single model by Thursday, Sept. 17 at 1:00 p.m. EDT (6 p.m. BST / 10 a.m. Pacific Time). Following the deadline, Valve will randomize the submissions into reservation and waitlist queues. Notification emails are expected to begin rolling out on Sept. 18 for those assigned to the reservation bucket.

To participate, users must hold a Steam account in good standing with a purchase history prior to April 27, 2026. Signups are limited to one per household. Direct shipping from Steam is available in the US, Canada, United Kingdom, European Union, and Australia, while Komodo handles distribution in Hong Kong, Japan, and Taiwan.

Hardware Design and Standalone Capabilities

Weighing in at 440g, the Steam Frame achieves a substantial weight reduction compared to older hardware and utilizes pancake lenses for enhanced visual clarity. Position tracking is managed via on-device cameras rather than external base stations. Beyond native VR software, the device supports streaming from a personal PC library and runs flat-screen games through Valve's Proton compatibility layer. Valve implements a verification system similar to the Steam Deck, curating "Great on Frame" titles to designate compatible standalone software.

Optional first-party accessories include a 45W power supply for $29 (£29 / €39), an Ergonomic Accessories Kit for $59 (£59 / €69) featuring a top headstrap, controller hand straps, battery doors, and an extended light blocker, and an Accessory Replacement Kit for $49 (£49 / €59) containing a replacement face gasket, head cushion, and standard light blocker. Third-party partners are also offering add-ons, such as Zenni Optical snap-in prescription lenses and an optional full-colour passthrough camera upgrade from Arcturus.
