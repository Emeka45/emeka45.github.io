Valve Opens Reservations for Steam Frame Standalone VR Headset

Valve has officially opened reservations for its new Steam Frame virtual reality headset, revealing a starting price above $1,000 for the standalone and PC-compatible device.

Introduction and Pricing Details

Valve has officially opened reservations for its new Steam Frame virtual reality headset, revealing a starting price above $1,000 for the standalone and PC-compatible device. Following years of rumors and months since its initial unveiling, Valve's first VR headset since the 2019 Index is priced at $1,059 (£889 / €1,049) for the 256GB storage model, while the 1TB model costs $1,299 (£1,089 / €1,279). Both configurations include two Steam Frame controllers, 16GB of unified LPDDR5X RAM, and a 4 nm Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 ARM64 processor.

Neither version includes a power supply in the box, though Valve notes that a Steam Deck charger or equivalent will work. Alternatively, users can purchase a separate 45W power supply for $29 (£29 / €39). Additional first-party accessories include an Ergonomic Accessories Kit for $59 (£59 / €69) featuring two controller hand straps, battery doors, a headset top strap, and an extended light blocker, alongside an Accessory Replacement Kit for $49 (£49 / €59) containing a replacement face gasket, head cushion, and standard light blocker.

Reservation Process and Availability

Valve is managing sales through a specific reservation and waitlist system similar to the rollout used for the Steam Machine. Interested customers must sign up for one model by Thursday, Sept. 17, at 1:00 p.m. EDT (6 p.m. BST). Following the deadline, Valve will conduct a one-time randomization process to place participants into either a reservation bucket or a waitlist. Customers in the reservation queue will begin receiving purchase emails starting Sept. 18.

The Steam Frame will ship directly from Steam in the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, the European Union, and Australia, while shipments to Hong Kong, Japan, and Taiwan will be handled via Komodo. To participate, users must have a Steam account in good standing with a purchase history prior to April 27, 2026, limited to one signup per household.

Hardware Improvements and Standalone Integration

The Steam Frame introduces several substantial hardware improvements over the older Valve Index, reducing weight down to 440g from 800g and replacing fresnel lenses with pancake lenses. The device features per-eye resolutions of 2160x2160 with a refresh rate ranging between 72Hz and 144Hz. Position tracking is managed entirely by on-device cameras rather than external base stations.

Designed to expand the market, Valve software engineer Jeremy Selan and hardware engineer Joy Lyons emphasized that the headset aims to remove the friction between playing virtual reality and traditional flatscreen games. Users can seamlessly access the entire Steam library, stream games from a PC via an included USB dongle, or run titles natively in standalone mode using Valve's Proton compatibility layer. Valve implements a verification system similar to the Steam Deck, with an initial lineup of around 130 verified standalone and flatscreen games available at launch.
