Federal Government Probes 37 Deaths in NSCDC Custody, Suspends Niger Commandant and 20 Officers

The Federal Government has launched an independent investigation and suspended 20 Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps officers, including the Niger State Commandant, following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody.

The Federal Government has taken decisive action following the tragic death of 37 suspected illegal miners who were held in the custody of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC) in Niger State. As part of the initial response to the incident, authorities have established an independent investigative committee to thoroughly examine the circumstances surrounding the fatalities, promising that there will be no cover-up in the course of the probe, as reported by The Punch.

In tandem with the inauguration of the probe panel, the government has announced the immediate suspension of 20 NSCDC personnel connected to the matter. This disciplinary measure also affects the Niger State Commandant of the corps, ensuring that a comprehensive and unbiased review can take place without interference from local command structures, according to details shared by The Reporter.

The incident has drawn significant public attention due to the high death toll of individuals apprehended over suspected illegal mining activities. The newly instituted panel is expected to uncover how the 37 detainees lost their lives while in state custody, ascertain accountability, and recommend appropriate actions to prevent future occurrences.

Further developments and outcomes from the independent panel are expected as the investigation progresses. Accountability measures for the suspended officers and the final verdict on the causes of the deaths remain pending as the authorities continue their inquiry.
