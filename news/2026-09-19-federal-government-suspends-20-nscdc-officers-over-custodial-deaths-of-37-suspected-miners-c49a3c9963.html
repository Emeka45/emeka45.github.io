Federal Government Suspends 20 NSCDC Officers Over Custodial Deaths of 37 Suspected Miners in Niger State

The Federal Government has taken swift administrative action by suspending 20 Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps officers, including the Niger State Commandant, following the tragic death of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody. Political parties have also stepped forward to demand a full, transparent inquiry.

The Federal Government has taken swift administrative action following the tragic deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners while in detention. As reported by The Reporter, 20 officers of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC), including the Niger State Commandant, have been suspended.

Background of the Incident

The detainees were arrested during separate security operations conducted on September 15 and 16 in M.I. Wushishi and Lukoto villages in Niger State. According to initial disclosures by the Niger State NSCDC Commandant, Suberu Aniviye, the individuals were held as suspected illegal miners. The deaths occurred in custody on Thursday, September 17, 2026. Reports surrounding the conditions of the facility noted that detainees were confined in a single cell, prompting widespread public concern and scrutiny over detention practices.

Government Response and Investigation Panel

Following a directive from President Bola Tinubu for a comprehensive investigation, Minister of Interior Olubunmi Tunji Ojo announced the immediate suspensions on Saturday. The suspended personnel include officers involved in station guard, arrest, investigation, intelligence, legal, and guard duties, alongside the separately suspended state commandant.

To thoroughly examine the incident, the Federal Government constituted a 10-member independent investigative committee chaired by retired Department of State Services Deputy Director General Jonathan Kure, with former Nigerian Law School Director General Prof. Isa Hayatu Chiroma serving as secretary. Other members include retired AIG Hosea Hassan Karma, Prof. Olayinka Buhari, representatives from the Minna Emirate Council and the Niger State Government, Miners Association of Nigeria National Secretary Liman Sulaiman, human rights lawyer Deji Adeyanju, journalist Zainab Suleiman Okino, and public affairs analyst George Agbakahi.

The committee has been mandated to:

  • Establish the identities of the deceased.
  • Investigate the circumstances surrounding the arrests and detention.
  • Determine the root cause and circumstances of the fatalities.
  • Assess responsibility, complicity, negligence, and misconduct.
  • Recommend appropriate disciplinary actions, compensation where applicable, and preventive measures.

Minister Tunji Ojo directed all relevant officers to cooperate fully with the panel, preserve all records and material evidence, and warned that any obstruction, witness intimidation, or evidence tampering would be treated as a serious offense. The committee is empowered to co-opt experts, visit relevant locations, receive public memoranda, and must submit its final report within two weeks.

Public Reactions and Broader Context

The incident has drawn strong condemnation from political bodies. As detailed by The Punch, the Allied Peoples Movement (APM), through its National Publicity Secretary Abubakar Yusuf, condemned the avoidable deaths and criticized the conditions under which detainees were held. The party also voiced concern over subsequent security responses, condemning the reported use of ammunition and tear gas against protesters who took to the streets following the tragedy.

Political parties and civil society stakeholders continue to push for an open, transparent, and unrestricted inquiry to ensure accountability and uphold the constitutional dignity of citizens.
