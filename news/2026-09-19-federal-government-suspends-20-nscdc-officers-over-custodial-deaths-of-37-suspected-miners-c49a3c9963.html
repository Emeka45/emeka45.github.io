Federal Government Suspends 20 NSCDC Officers Over Custodial Deaths of 37 Suspected Miners in Niger State

The Federal Government has taken swift administrative action by suspending 20 Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps officers, including the Niger State Commandant, following the tragic death of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody. Political parties have also stepped forward to demand a full, transparent inquiry.

The Federal Government has ordered the immediate suspension of 20 personnel from the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (The Reporter), including the Niger State Commandant. This drastic disciplinary measure comes in the wake of a shocking incident that resulted in the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners while they were held in official custody within Niger State (Punch Newspapers).

Alongside the suspensions, authorities have established an investigative panel to uncover the exact circumstances surrounding the fatalities. The tragedy has triggered widespread concern and condemnation from various quarters across the nation, highlighting ongoing scrutiny regarding custodial safety and human rights compliance within security agencies.

Political Condemnation and Calls for Transparency

Reacting to the development, the Allied Peoples Movement (Punch Newspapers) strongly condemned the loss of 37 lives under the watch of state security operatives. The political party has demanded a comprehensive and transparent probe to ensure accountability and prevent any recurrence of such an incident in future law enforcement operations.

As the newly formed panel begins its investigation, the public awaits further findings regarding the suspended officers' level of culpability and the systematic issues that led to the heavy casualties in detention.
