Federal Government Suspends 20 NSCDC Officers Over Custodial Deaths of 37 Suspected Miners in Niger State

The Federal Government has taken swift administrative action by suspending 20 Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps officers, including the Niger State Commandant, following the tragic death of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody. Political parties have also stepped forward to demand a full, transparent inquiry.

The Federal Government has taken swift administrative action following the tragic deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in the custody of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC) in Niger State. Minister of Interior Olubunmi Tunji Ojo announced the suspension of 20 NSCDC officers, including the Niger State Commandant, Suberu Aniviye, in connection with the incident The Reporter Media.

Background and Custodial Circumstances

The 37 victims were among 66 suspected illegal miners arrested during separate operations conducted on September 15 and 16 in M.I. Wushishi and Lukoto villages in Niger State APM. The detainees were subsequently held in an NSCDC facility, where the fatalities occurred on Thursday, September 17, 2026 The Reporter Media. While authorities initially pointed to a suspected disease outbreak within the cell, reports and critics indicated that 66 individuals were cramped into a single cell under severe conditions, allegedly suffering from excessive heat and exposure to an unidentified sprayed substance APM.

Government Investigation and Suspended Personnel

Acting on a directive from President Bola Tinubu, Interior Minister Tunji Ojo constituted a 10-member independent investigative committee on Saturday, September 19, 2026 The Reporter Media. The panel is chaired by retired Department of State Services Deputy Director General Jonathan Kure, with former Nigerian Law School Director General Prof. Isa Hayatu Chiroma serving as secretary The Reporter Media. Other committee members include retired AIG Hosea Hassan Karma, Prof. Olayinka Buhari, representatives of the Minna Emirate Council and the Niger State Government, Miners Association of Nigeria National Secretary Liman Sulaiman, human rights lawyer Deji Adeyanju, journalist Zainab Suleiman Okino, and public affairs analyst George Agbakahi The Reporter Media.

The probe panel has been given two weeks to complete its assignment, establish the identities of the deceased, determine the exact causes of death, investigate arrest and detention procedures, and assign responsibility for any negligence or misconduct The Reporter Media. Alongside State Commandant Suberu Aniviye, the 20 suspended personnel include officers assigned to station guard, arrest, investigation, intelligence, legal, and general guard duties The Reporter Media.

Political Reactions and Public Outcry

The tragedy sparked wider public reactions, including street protests in Niger State where security agencies reportedly deployed ammunition and tear gas APM. Political organizations have also weighed in on the event. The Allied Peoples Movement (APM), through its National Publicity Secretary Abubakar Yusuf, strongly condemned the deaths and the reported use of force against protesters, calling for an unrestricted and transparent inquiry into the matter APM.
