Federal Government Suspends 20 NSCDC Officers Over Custodial Deaths of 37 Suspected Miners in Niger State

The Federal Government has taken swift administrative action by suspending 20 Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps officers, including the Niger State Commandant, following the tragic death of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody. Political parties have also stepped forward to demand a full, transparent inquiry.

The Federal Government has taken swift administrative action following the tragic deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in the custody of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC) in Minna, Niger State. Minister of Interior Olubunmi Tunji Ojo announced the immediate suspension of 20 NSCDC officers, including the Niger State Commandant, Suberu Aniviye, following a directive from President Bola Tinubu for a comprehensive investigation into the incident.

Background and Arrest Details

The victims were among individuals arrested during separate operations conducted on September 15 and 16, 2026, in M.I. Wushishi and Lukoto villages in Niger State by the NSCDC. State Commandant Suberu Aniviye initially disclosed the fatalities in a statement, noting that the suspects died while in detention. Reports surrounding the facility indicated that 66 detainees had been confined in a single cell, raising widespread concerns over detention conditions.

The Allied Peoples Movement (APM), through its National Publicity Secretary Abubakar Yusuf, strongly condemned the deaths, describing them as avoidable and characterizing the conditions as inhumane. The party also criticized the reported use of ammunition and tear gas by security agencies against protesters who took to the streets following the deaths, warning that such actions risked further casualties.

Independent Investigation Panel Constituted

In response to the crisis, the Federal Government established a 10-member independent investigative committee. The panel is chaired by retired Department of State Services Deputy Director General Jonathan Kure, with former Nigerian Law School Director General Prof. Isa Hayatu Chiroma serving as secretary.

Other notable members of the investigative body include retired AIG Hosea Hassan Karma, Prof. Olayinka Buhari, representatives of the Minna Emirate Council and the Niger State Government, Miners Association of Nigeria National Secretary Liman Sulaiman, human rights lawyer Deji Adeyanju, journalist Zainab Suleiman Okino, and public affairs analyst George Agbakahi.

The committee's mandate includes:

  • Establishing the identities of the deceased.
  • Investigating the circumstances surrounding their arrest, detention, and eventual deaths.
  • Determining responsibility, complicity, negligence, and misconduct.
  • Recommending appropriate disciplinary actions, compensation where applicable, and preventive measures.

Minister Tunji Ojo explicitly warned that any attempts to obstruct the probe, intimidate witnesses, or conceal evidence would be treated as a severe offense. The panel has been granted two weeks to complete its assignment and submit its official report to the Ministry of Interior.

Suspended Personnel and Next Steps

The 20 suspended personnel comprise officers involved in station guard, arrest, investigation, intelligence, legal, and general guard duties. Further details can be found in reporting by The Punch and The Reporter. As public scrutiny intensifies, political bodies and civil society groups continue to demand total transparency as the independent panel commences its work.
