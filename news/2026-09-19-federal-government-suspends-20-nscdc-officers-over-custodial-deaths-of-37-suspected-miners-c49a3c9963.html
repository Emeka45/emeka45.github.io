Federal Government Suspends 20 NSCDC Officers Over Custodial Deaths of 37 Suspected Miners in Niger State

The Federal Government has taken swift administrative action by suspending 20 Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps officers, including the Niger State Commandant, following the tragic death of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody. Political parties have also stepped forward to demand a full, transparent inquiry.

The Federal Government has suspended 20 officers of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC), including the Niger State Commandant, following the tragic deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners held in custody. The swift administrative measures follow a directive from President Bola Tinubu calling for a comprehensive inquiry into the incident, which occurred on Thursday, September 17, 2026.

Government Action and Independent Investigation Panel

Minister of Interior Olubunmi Tunji Ojo announced the suspensions on Saturday, September 19, 2026, while establishing a 10-member independent investigative committee. The panel is tasked with identifying the deceased, reviewing the circumstances of their arrest and detention, and determining the direct cause of the fatalities. Furthermore, the committee will evaluate responsibility, potential misconduct, negligence, and complicity, while recommending appropriate disciplinary actions, compensation where applicable, and safeguards to prevent similar occurrences.

The Interior Minister instructed NSCDC leadership and related personnel to fully cooperate with the probe and strictly preserve all records and material evidence linked to the event. Authorities cautioned that any interference, witness intimidation, or attempts to conceal evidence would be treated as a grave offense.

The investigative committee is chaired by Jonathan Kure, a retired Deputy Director General of the Department of State Services, with Professor Isa Hayatu Chiroma, former Director General of the Nigerian Law School, serving as secretary. Other notable members include retired AIG Hosea Hassan Karma, Professor Olayinka Buhari, representatives from the Niger State Government and Minna Emirate Council, Liman Sulaiman representing the Miners Association of Nigeria, lawyer and human rights activist Deji Adeyanju, journalist Zainab Suleiman Okino, and public affairs analyst George Agbakahi. The panel has a mandate to complete its assignment and submit a report within two weeks.

Background and Context of the Arrests

The 37 victims were among a group of suspected illegal miners detained following separate operations conducted on September 15 and 16 in M.I. Wushishi and Lukoto villages in Niger State. According to initial disclosures by Niger State Commandant Suberu Aniviye, the detainees were held before the fatalities unfolded. The Allied Peoples Movement (APM), through its National Publicity Secretary Abubakar Yusuf, condemned the incident, raising concerns over reports that 66 detainees were confined to a single cell under harsh conditions.

The suspended personnel comprise individuals assigned to station guard, arrest, investigation, intelligence, legal, and guard duties, alongside the separately suspended state commandant, Suberu Aniviye. The independent panel remains responsible for clarifying the definitive cause of the deaths as investigations continue.
