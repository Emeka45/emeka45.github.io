Former Sydney Police Officer Goes on Trial for Alleged Double Murder

The trial of Beaumont Lamarre-Condon, a former New South Wales police officer accused of murdering his ex-partner and his new boyfriend, has begun in Sydney. The defendant has pleaded not guilty.

The trial of Beaumont Lamarre-Condon, a former New South Wales police officer accused of murdering his ex-partner and his new boyfriend, has begun in Sydney. The 31-year-old defendant has pleaded not guilty to two charges of domestic violence-related murder and one of aggravated break and enter, as reported by the BBC.

Opening Address and Prosecution Allegations

During the opening address on Monday, prosecutor Brett Hatfield SC told the court that Lamarre-Condon was driven by a "jealous infatuation" with his former partner, 26-year-old TV presenter Jesse Baird. Following a brief romantic relationship, the accused allegedly engaged in compulsive behavior that escalated after Baird ended all contact in November 2023. Prosecutors stated that Lamarre-Condon used a friend's phone to falsely claim he had cancer, stalked Baird, and tampered with his car.

The prosecution alleges that Lamarre-Condon planned Baird's murder, setting his alarm for 04:30 local time on the day of the killings. He allegedly switched to a burner phone, picked up a rental car, and waited outside Baird's home in the Sydney suburb of Paddington for three hours while disguised as an Uber Eats rider, a detail also noted by The Guardian. Using a key he had previously obtained, Lamarre-Condon allegedly entered the property and shot Baird in the kitchen. He then killed Baird's new partner, 29-year-old Qantas flight attendant Luke Davies, in the bedroom as Davies attempted to call emergency services. Hatfield told the court that Davies was killed to eliminate him as a witness.

Cover-Up and Investigation

Following the alleged killings, Lamarre-Condon attempted to cover up the crimes by sending messages from Baird's mobile phone. The victims' bodies were later found hidden in surfboard bags in a rural area 200 kilometers away. Police were first alerted after bloodied items belonging to Baird and Davies were discovered in a bin in Sydney's south. Eight days after the incident, authorities located the couple's bodies, and Lamarre-Condon subsequently handed himself in at a police station.

The aggravated break and enter charge stems from an earlier incident in August 2023, where Lamarre-Condon is accused of breaking into Baird's home to steal his phone and wallet. Additionally, the court heard that the accused had previously offered to plead guilty to a single charge of manslaughter regarding Baird's death, but the offer was rejected.

Trial Details and Next Steps

The trial, overseen by Justice Natalie Adams, is expected to run for three months. Prior to the opening statements, a 15-member jury was selected. Justice Adams explained that three extra jurors were chosen beyond the standard 12 as a contingency for sickness or other unforeseen circumstances.

Lamarre-Condon was sacked by the New South Wales Police in the weeks following his arrest and charges.
