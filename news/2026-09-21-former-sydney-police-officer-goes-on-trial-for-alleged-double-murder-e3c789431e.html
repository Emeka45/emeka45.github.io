Former Sydney Police Officer Goes on Trial for Alleged Double Murder

The trial of Beaumont Lamarre-Condon, a former New South Wales police officer accused of murdering his ex-partner and his new boyfriend, has begun in Sydney. The defendant has pleaded not guilty.

The trial of Beaumont Lamarre-Condon, a former New South Wales police officer accused of murdering his ex-partner and his new boyfriend, has begun in Sydney, according to BBC News. The defendant has pleaded not guilty to two charges of domestic violence-related murder and one of aggravated break and enter.

The Prosecution's Opening Address

In his opening address, prosecutor Brett Hatfield SC described how Lamarre-Condon developed an "obsessive and jealous infatuation" with 26-year-old TV presenter Jesse Baird after a brief romantic relationship. The barrister told the court that this infatuation manifested in a "range of compulsive behaviour" that escalated after Baird ended all contact in November 2023. Prosecutors allege that Lamarre-Condon used a friend's phone to message Baird claiming he had cancer, stalked his former partner, and tampered with his car.

The trial is expected to run for three months before Justice Natalie Adams. A 15-member jury was selected for the proceedings, including three extra contingency jurors chosen to account for sickness or other unforeseen circumstances.

Details of the Alleged Killings

According to the prosecution, Lamarre-Condon set his alarm for 04:30 local time on the day of the alleged murders, sent a text message to a friend indicating he would update them later, and switched to a burner phone. He then allegedly picked up a rental car and waited outside Baird's home in the Sydney suburb of Paddington for three hours while disguised as an Uber driver. Prosecutors stated he had previously purchased a surfboard bag to dispose of the body and had obtained a key to the property in advance.

After entering the home, Lamarre-Condon allegedly shot Baird in the kitchen. He then killed Baird's new partner, 29-year-old Qantas flight attendant Luke Davies, in the bedroom as Davies attempted to call emergency services. Hatfield told the court that Baird's murder was planned and that Davies was killed to "eliminate him as a witness."

Cover-Up Attempts and Investigation

Following the alleged killings, Lamarre-Condon attempted to cover up the crimes by sending messages from Baird's mobile phone, according to the prosecution. The couple's bodies were eventually found hidden in surfboard bags in a rural area 200 kilometers away eight days after the incident.

Police were first alerted to the case after bloodied items belonging to Baird and Davies were discovered in a bin in Sydney's south. Lamarre-Condon subsequently handed himself in at a police station and was charged. The aggravated break and enter charge stems from a separate alleged incident in August 2023, where the accused is accused of breaking into Baird's home to steal his phone and wallet. Hatfield also revealed that Lamarre-Condon had earlier offered to plead guilty to a single charge of manslaughter regarding Baird's death, but the offer was rejected.

Following his arrest and charges, Lamarre-Condon was dismissed from the New South Wales Police Force.
