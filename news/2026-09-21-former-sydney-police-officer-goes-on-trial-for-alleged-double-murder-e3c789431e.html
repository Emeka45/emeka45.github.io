Former Sydney Police Officer Goes on Trial for Alleged Double Murder

The trial of Beaumont Lamarre-Condon, a former New South Wales police officer accused of murdering his ex-partner and his new boyfriend, has begun in Sydney. The defendant has pleaded not guilty.

The trial of Beaumont Lamarre-Condon, a former New South Wales police officer accused of murdering his ex-partner and his new boyfriend, has begun in Sydney. The defendant has pleaded not guilty to two charges of domestic violence-related murder and one charge of aggravated break and enter, as reported by BBC News.

Prosecution Opening Address and Allegations

During the opening address on Monday, prosecutor Brett Hatfield SC told the court that Lamarre-Condon was driven by an "obsessive and jealous infatuation" with 26-year-old TV presenter Jesse Baird, his former partner. According to the prosecution, this infatuation manifested in compulsive behavior that escalated after Baird ended all contact in November 2023. The barrister stated that Lamarre-Condon used a friend's phone to falsely claim he had cancer, stalked Baird, and tampered with his car following the breakup.

Further details regarding the day of the alleged killings in 2024 indicate that Lamarre-Condon set his alarm for 04:30 local time and messaged a friend before switching to a burner phone. Prosecutors allege he picked up a rental car, dressed as an Uber driver, and waited outside Baird's home in the Sydney suburb of Paddington for three hours. He had also allegedly purchased a surfboard bag ahead of time to dispose of the body.

The court heard that Lamarre-Condon used a previously obtained key to enter Baird's home, where he shot Baird in the kitchen. He then allegedly killed Baird's new partner, 29-year-old Qantas flight attendant Luke Davies, in the bedroom as Davies attempted to call emergency services. Hatfield stated that Lamarre-Condon planned Baird's murder and targeted Davies to eliminate him as a witness.

Cover-Up Attempts and Prior Incidents

Following the alleged killings, Lamarre-Condon attempted to cover up the crimes by sending messages from Baird's mobile phone. The aggravated break and enter charge stems from a separate incident in August 2023, where the accused allegedly broke into Baird's home to steal his phone and wallet. Additionally, prosecutors revealed that Lamarre-Condon had previously offered to plead guilty to a single charge of manslaughter regarding Baird's death, but the offer was rejected.

Discovery of Bodies and Police Investigation

The investigation began after bloodied items belonging to Baird and Davies were discovered in a waste bin in southern Sydney. Lamarre-Condon subsequently handed himself in at a police station and was charged, while authorities located the couple's bodies hidden inside surfboard bags in a rural area 200 kilometers away eight days after the incident. Lamarre-Condon was dismissed from the New South Wales Police force in the weeks following his arrest.

Trial Structure

The trial is expected to last for three months. Before opening arguments, a 15-member jury was selected under Justice Natalie Adams, who noted that three extra jurors were added beyond the standard 12 as a contingency for sickness or other unforeseen circumstances.
