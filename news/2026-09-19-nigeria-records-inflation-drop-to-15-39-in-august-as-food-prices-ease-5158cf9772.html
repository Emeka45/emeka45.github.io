Nigeria Records Inflation Drop to 15.39% in August as Food Prices Ease

Nigeria's inflation rate fell to 15.39% in August driven by easing food prices, while fuel pricing and energy costs continue to impact the broader business environment.

Nigeria's economic landscape has experienced a notable shift as inflation figures for the month of August dropped to 15.39 percent. According to reports from Techeconomy and TechNext.ng, the reduction was largely propelled by a decrease in food prices across the country, offering a temporary reprieve to consumers grappling with high living costs.

Consumer Pressures and Energy Costs

Despite the dip in headline inflation, the domestic economic environment remains challenging for enterprises and motorists alike. Concurrently, businesses are navigating a fresh operational squeeze as petroleum pump prices edge closer to ₦1,500 per litre, as highlighted by Business News Nigeria. Energy expenses remain a critical factor determining transportation overheads and general production expenditures.

Market Responses and Ex-Depot Adjustments

In an effort to mitigate these persistent fuel pressures within the domestic market, industrial adjustments are underway. Notably, the Dangote Refinery has adjusted its ex-depot prices downward, setting petrol at ₦1,250 per litre and diesel at ₦1,700 per litre to help stabilize supply costs across the federation.

Market watchers and financial analysts continue to monitor how these concurrent trends—softening consumer inflation metrics alongside high retail energy realities—will shape upcoming fiscal and monetary strategies in the months ahead.
