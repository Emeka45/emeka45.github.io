Atiku Urges Tinubu to Adopt Petrol Price Reduction Proposal, Cites Survey Data

Former Vice President Atiku Abubakar has called on President Bola Tinubu to embrace his proposal for intervention to lower petrol prices and alleviate the high cost of living, pointing to a recent SBM Intelligence survey indicating widespread public support for restoring subsidies.

Former Vice President Atiku Abubakar has called on President Bola Tinubu to adopt his policy proposal for market intervention to lower petrol prices and alleviate the heavy cost of living currently facing citizens. Atiku pointed to recent empirical data showing widespread public backing for government intervention to ease economic hardships.

SBM Intelligence Survey Findings

The call follows the release of a survey by SBM Intelligence, which indicated that 67.4 per cent of respondents support the restoration of petrol subsidies. Published as part of the second wave of the Nigeria 2027 Voter Sentiment Tracker titled Nigeria 2027: A Narrower Race, the research gathered data from 1,103 eligible voters across 12 states, the Federal Capital Territory, and all six geopolitical zones through face-to-face interviews in both urban and rural areas.

According to the survey metrics highlighted by Atiku's camp, petrol was identified as the second-most cited national issue and the administration's worst-rated policy, securing a score of 1.69 out of four. The data showed that 32.4 per cent of rural respondents flagged petrol as their primary concern, compared to 11.7 per cent in urban locations. Regionally, petrol emerged as the foremost concern in the Southwest at 30.5 per cent and the South-South at 29.1 per cent.

Atiku's Critique and Proposed Alternative

In a statement issued by his Senior Special Assistant on Public Communication, Phrank Shaibu, Atiku argued that the survey numbers reflect a democratic majority rejecting the administration's economic philosophy. He contended that the removal of the petrol subsidy without adequate strategies for domestic refining, wage protection, or mass transit had caused widespread hardship, triggering soaring food inflation and skyrocketing transportation costs.

Atiku urged President Tinubu to implement his proposed intervention framework regardless of who receives the political credit, suggesting that the administration could even rename the policy to take ownership of it. Detail of his alternative plan includes:

  • Implementing a transparent production subsidy dedicated exclusively to petroleum products refined within Nigeria and sold locally.
  • Excluding imported petroleum products from qualifying for the subsidy scheme.
  • Enforcing a fixed spending limit requiring National Assembly approval alongside mandatory independent audits.

Furthermore, Atiku cautioned the Federal Government against replicating the petrol subsidy removal experience with electricity subsidies, noting that households and manufacturers are already struggling with steep energy costs. He reiterated that his broader vision aims to expand domestic production, create jobs, and restore the purchasing power of ordinary citizens.
