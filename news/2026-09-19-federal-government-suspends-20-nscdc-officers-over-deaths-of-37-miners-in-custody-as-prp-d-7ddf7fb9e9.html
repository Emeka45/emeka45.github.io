Federal Government Suspends 20 NSCDC Officers Over Deaths of 37 Miners in Custody as PRP Demands Justice

The Federal Government has suspended 20 Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps officers and the Niger State Commandant following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody, prompting widespread calls for an independent inquiry.

The Federal Government has taken decisive administrative action by suspending 20 officers of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC), including the Niger State Commandant, following the tragic deaths of 37 individuals held in custody. The victims were reportedly detained as suspected illegal miners before their deaths occurred under circumstances that have triggered national concern and regulatory scrutiny.

As part of the government's response to the incident, an investigative panel has been established to examine the root causes of the fatalities and determine the level of accountability among the personnel involved. The suspension of the commanding officer and the rank-and-file personnel is intended to ensure an unhindered inquiry into how dozens of detainees lost their lives while in state custody.

Political Reactions and Calls for Accountability

The incident has drawn sharp reactions from political bodies, most notably the Peoples Redemption Party (PRP). As detailed in reports from The Punch, the party has formally demanded absolute justice for the 37 deceased miners. The PRP is pushing for a fully independent judicial or administrative inquiry that goes beyond internal disciplinary measures, insisting that anyone found culpable in the chain of command must face prosecution.

Public safety advocates and legal observers have similarly stressed the necessity of transparency as the newly formed Federal panel begins its work. The handling of suspects in custody remains a critical benchmark for institutional accountability within Nigeria's security architecture.

Next Steps in the Investigation

With the panel now instituted and the affected personnel currently on suspension, attention shifts to the findings and recommendations the investigative committee will produce. Further updates are expected as federal authorities review the operational conduct of the NSCDC personnel stationed in Niger State and determine the formal next steps regarding potential criminal prosecutions or systemic reforms.
