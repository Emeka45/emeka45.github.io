Federal Government Suspends 20 NSCDC Officers Over Deaths of 37 Miners in Custody as PRP Demands Justice

The Federal Government has suspended 20 Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps officers and the Niger State Commandant following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody, prompting widespread calls for an independent inquiry.

The Federal Government has suspended 20 officers of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC) and the Niger State Commandant following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in the Corps’ custody in Minna. The development has triggered strong reactions, including a demand for justice and an independent inquiry from the Peoples Redemption Party (PRP).

Government Action and Investigation Panel

Minister of Interior Olubunmi Tunji-Ojo announced the suspensions on Saturday following a directive from President Bola Tinubu for a comprehensive, transparent, and unhindered investigation into the incident, which occurred on Thursday, September 17, 2026. Alongside the Niger State Commandant, Suberu Aniviye, 20 other personnel involved in station guard, arrest, investigation, intelligence, legal, and guard duties were suspended. Reports also indicated that the Commandant and 21 officers were detained in Abuja in connection with the matter.

To probe the tragedy, the Federal Government constituted a 10-member independent investigative committee chaired by retired Department of State Services Deputy Director General Jonathan Kure, with former Nigerian Law School Director General Prof. Isa Hayatu Chiroma serving as secretary. Other members include retired AIG Hosea Hassan Karma, Prof. Olayinka Buhari, representatives of the Minna Emirate Council and Niger State Government, Miners Association of Nigeria National Secretary Liman Sulaiman, human rights lawyer Deji Adeyanju, journalist Zainab Suleiman Okino, and public affairs analyst George Agbakahi. The panel has been given two weeks to complete its assignment, establish the identities of the deceased, determine the causes and circumstances of their arrest and detention, and assess responsibility, negligence, or misconduct.

Disputed Circumstances and Conflicting Accounts

The circumstances surrounding the 37 deaths remain a subject of dispute. While the NSCDC attributed the fatalities to an outbreak of disease without specifying the exact illness, survivors alleged that the detainees suffered from suffocation and dehydration inside an overcrowded cell. Meanwhile, Niger State Governor Mohammed Bago stated that preliminary forensic findings revealed foam mixed with bloodstains in the mouths of some victims, alongside peeling skin, pointing to the possibility of lead poisoning.

Political Reactions and Broader Security Concerns

The incident has drawn sharp criticism from political organizations. In a statement issued in Abuja, PRP National Publicity Secretary Muhammed Ishaq condemned the tragedy and called for the immediate establishment of an Independent Commission of Inquiry alongside the prosecution of anyone found culpable. The party stated that the incident highlighted broader failures in protecting the lives and property of Nigerians.

Additionally, the PRP raised concerns regarding separate security challenges in the region, drawing attention to the continued captivity of Dekara community residents in Borgu Local Government Area of Niger State who had been abducted by bandits since August 2026. The party urged authorities to intensify efforts to secure the release of kidnapped residents, hold security personnel accountable, and review policies to restore peace across the North-Central and North-West regions.
