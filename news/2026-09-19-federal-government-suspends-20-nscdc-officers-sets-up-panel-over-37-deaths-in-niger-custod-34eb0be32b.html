Federal Government Suspends 20 NSCDC Officers, Sets Up Panel Over 37 Deaths in Niger Custody

The Federal Government has suspended 20 Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps officers and the Niger State Commandant following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody, as the Nigerian Bar Association also launches an independent investigation.

The Federal Government has taken decisive action following the tragic deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners who died while in the custody of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC) in Niger State. According to reports from The Reporter, authorities have suspended 20 NSCDC officers alongside the Niger State Commandant in connection with the incident.

In response to the gravity of the custodial deaths, an administrative panel has been established to investigate the circumstances surrounding the fatalities. The incident has drawn widespread attention regarding custodial safety, human rights compliance, and the handling of suspects detained during operations against illegal mining activities in the region.

Parallel to the government's investigative panel, legal bodies are also stepping up oversight. The Punch reports that the Nigerian Bar Association (NBA) has formed a dedicated team to independently probe the deaths of the 37 individuals, ensuring that accountability is maintained and that full transparency is brought to light regarding what transpired within the security agency's facility.

As both the federal investigative panel and the NBA probe begin their work, further developments are expected regarding potential administrative or legal consequences for the suspended personnel, as well as broader institutional reforms within the security framework.
