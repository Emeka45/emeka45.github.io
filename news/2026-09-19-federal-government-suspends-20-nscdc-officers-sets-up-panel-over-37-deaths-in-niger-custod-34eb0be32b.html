Federal Government Suspends 20 NSCDC Officers, Sets Up Panel Over 37 Deaths in Niger Custody

The Federal Government has suspended 20 Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps officers and the Niger State Commandant following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody, as the Nigerian Bar Association also launches an independent investigation.

Federal Government and NBA Launch Investigations Into Custodial Deaths

The Federal Government has suspended 20 officers of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC), including the Niger State Commandant, following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners held in the Corps' custody in Minna, Niger State. The incident occurred on Thursday, September 17, 2026. Minister of Interior Olubunmi Tunji Ojo announced the suspensions alongside the formation of a 10-member independent investigative committee following a directive from President Bola Tinubu for a comprehensive inquiry into the tragedy.

Simultaneously, the Nigerian Bar Association (NBA) announced it will independently probe the deaths, constituting a separate team led by Senior Advocate of Nigeria and former NBA General Secretary Jonathan Gunu Taidi, SAN. NBA President Oyinkansola Badejo-Okusanya, SAN, emphasized that the association expects complete transparency, proper forensic and medical examinations, and the full preservation of evidence.

Mandate of the Federal Investigative Panel

The federal investigative committee is chaired by Jonathan Kure, a retired Deputy Director General of the Department of State Services, with former Nigerian Law School Director General Prof. Isa Hayatu Chiroma serving as secretary. Other members include retired AIG Hosea Hassan Karma, Prof. Olayinka Buhari, representatives of the Minna Emirate Council and the Niger State Government, Miners Association of Nigeria National Secretary Liman Sulaiman, human rights lawyer Deji Adeyanju, journalist Zainab Suleiman Okino, and public affairs analyst George Agbakahi.

The panel is tasked with establishing the identities of the deceased, determining the circumstances surrounding their arrest and detention, and finding the scientific cause of death. Furthermore, the committee will examine issues of responsibility, complicity, negligence, and misconduct, with powers to recommend appropriate actions, compensation, and preventive measures. The panel has been granted a two-week timeframe to complete its work and submit its report to the Minister of Interior.

The 20 suspended NSCDC personnel include officers connected to station guard, arrest, investigation, intelligence, legal, and guard duties, alongside the separately suspended Niger State Commandant, Suberu Aniviye. Minister Tunji Ojo warned that any attempts to obstruct the probe, intimidate witnesses, or destroy evidence would be treated as severe offences.

Wider Calls for Accountability and Scrutiny of Mining Operations

The NBA has raised serious concerns regarding conditions at the detention facility, including reports of overcrowding, poor ventilation, and the potential detention of minors. The association stressed that suspects retain the presumption of innocence and that the tragedy demands rigorous answers since 37 individuals entered state custody alive but failed to return.

In addition to probing the immediate causes of the fatalities, the NBA urged authorities to extend investigations to the ownership and financial backing of the illegal mining sites where the suspects were originally arrested. The association stressed that enforcement must target organizers, financiers, and commercial exploiters rather than focusing solely on laborers performing physical work at the sites.

Authorities have urged the public to remain calm and allow the various investigative bodies—including those initiated by the Federal Government, the NSCDC, the Niger State Government, and the police—to conclude their work transparently.
