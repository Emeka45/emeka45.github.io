Lagos State Orders Immediate Relocation of Residents in Flood-Prone Areas Following NiHSA Alert

The Lagos State Government has directed residents living in low-lying and flood-prone communities to relocate immediately, acting on a fresh seven-day flood warning issued by the Nigeria Hydrological Services Agency.

The Lagos State Government has directed residents living in low-lying and flood-prone communities to relocate immediately to higher ground. This directive follows a fresh seven-day flood warning issued by the Nigeria Hydrological Services Agency, aimed at preventing any loss of lives and property.

Details of the NiHSA Flood Advisory

The Commissioner for the Environment and Water Resources, Tokunbo Wahab, disclosed the directive in a statement issued by the ministry’s Director of Public Affairs, Kunle Adeshina. According to the advisory, the alert affects Lagos and 14 other states across the country. In total, the national flood advisory warns that more than 8,000 communities and 4,500 schools across 15 states face potential flood risks between September 19 and 25.

Vulnerable areas specifically highlighted within Lagos include Eti-Osa, Lagos Island, Oke-Ira Nla in Ajah, Kirikiri, Ilaje Waterfront Jetty, and Jetty Terminal B. Furthermore, the state government urged heightened caution for communities situated along the Ogun River and other major waterways due to rising water levels and rainfall intensity that could impact downstream areas.

High-Risk Zones and Safety Measures

Between September 19 and 25, settlements downstream of Isheri Olofin—including Isheri North, Kara, Itowolo, Ajegunle, Majidun, Ogolonto, and parts of Agboyi—have been placed on high alert. The NiHSA report cautioned that rising river levels could inundate vulnerable communities, potentially disrupting critical facilities such as schools, healthcare centres, markets, farms, and places of worship.

Mr Wahab urged residents not to wait until floodwaters rise before taking action, emphasizing that early preparation and temporary relocation are vital. Local governments, emergency management agencies, community leaders, and residents have been called upon to activate early-warning systems and move people, livestock, and valuables to safer locations. Additional information regarding the state's announcements can be found via Punch Nigeria.

Government Monitoring and Public Appeals

The Lagos State Government has committed to continuously monitoring river levels, rainfall patterns, and hydrological developments in partnership with relevant federal agencies, alongside routine maintenance and cleaning of drainage infrastructure. Residents have been strongly advised against dumping refuse into drains and waterways, as blocked channels can worsen flooding. Authorities also cautioned the public against walking or driving through flooded roads, bridges, and fast-moving water, reiterating that the protection of lives and property remains the government's foremost priority.
