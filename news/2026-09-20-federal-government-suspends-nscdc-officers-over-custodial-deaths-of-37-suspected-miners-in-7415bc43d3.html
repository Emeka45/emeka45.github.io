Federal Government Suspends NSCDC Officers Over Custodial Deaths of 37 Suspected Miners in Niger State

The Federal Government has suspended 20 Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps officers and the Niger State Commandant following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody, prompting official investigations and public warnings about detention conditions.

The Federal Government has suspended 20 officers of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC), alongside the Niger State Commandant, following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody. The directive stems from an incident at an NSCDC detention facility in Minna, where detainees arrested during operations against illegal mining on September 15 and 16, 2026, lost their lives.

Government Action and Investigation Panel

Minister of Interior Olubunmi Tunji Ojo announced the suspensions and constituted a 10-member independent investigative committee following a directive from President Bola Tinubu. The committee is tasked with establishing the identities of the deceased, investigating the circumstances of their arrest and detention, and determining the direct cause of death. Furthermore, the panel will examine issues of responsibility, complicity, negligence, and misconduct, while recommending appropriate actions, compensation where applicable, and preventative measures.

The investigative panel is chaired by retired Department of State Services Deputy Director General Jonathan Kure, with former Nigerian Law School Director General Prof. Isa Hayatu Chiroma serving as secretary. Other members include retired AIG Hosea Hassan Karma, Prof. Olayinka Buhari, representatives from the Minna Emirate Council and the Niger State Government, Miners Association of Nigeria National Secretary Liman Sulaiman, human rights lawyer Deji Adeyanju, journalist Zainab Suleiman Okino, and public affairs analyst George Agbakahi. The committee has been granted a two-week window to complete its assignment and submit its report.

Minister Tunji Ojo directed all relevant officers and the NSCDC leadership to fully cooperate with the probe, warning that any interference, witness intimidation, or destruction of material evidence will be treated as a serious offense. The suspended personnel include individuals who handled station guard, arrest, investigation, intelligence, legal, and guard duties, while Niger State Commandant Suberu Siyaka Aniviye faced separate suspension. Additional personnel involved have also been detained in Abuja, as reported by Punch Newspapers.

Conflicting Accounts and Detention Conditions

While official investigations are ongoing to determine the definitive cause of death, conflicting accounts have emerged regarding the conditions inside the facility. The NSCDC initially attributed the fatalities to a suspected disease outbreak. However, survivors recounted to Punch Newspapers that approximately 65 detainees were held in an overcrowded, poorly ventilated cell where several individuals struggled for air before passing away. Survivors pointed to extreme heat, poor ventilation, and severe dehydration as major factors contributing to the tragedy.

The situation has drawn widespread public concern regarding detention facility conditions in Nigeria. Former Kaduna Central lawmaker Senator Shehu Sani publicly drew parallels to his own prolonged detention experiences under military rule, warning about the severe dangers and physical toll associated with overcrowded cells and restricted airflow. Meanwhile, the Niger State Government has ordered forensic examinations and autopsies as federal agencies, the NSCDC, and the police pursue parallel inquiries into the incident.
