Federal Government Suspends NSCDC Officers Over Custodial Deaths of 37 Suspected Miners in Niger State

The Federal Government has suspended 20 Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps officers and the Niger State Commandant following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody, prompting official investigations and public warnings about detention conditions.

The Federal Government has suspended 20 officers of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC) and the Niger State Commandant following the tragic deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody. The directive follows a presidential order by President Bola Tinubu for a comprehensive inquiry into the incident, which has drawn widespread public attention and scrutiny regarding detention conditions.

Background and Arrests

The 37 victims were among individuals arrested during security operations against illegal mining activities in Niger State. According to reports, the arrests took place on September 15 and September 16. Following their apprehension, the suspects were detained at an NSCDC facility in Minna. Survivors of the ordeal recounted that roughly 65 detainees were packed into a heavily congested and poorly ventilated cell. Accounts detailed severe distress, with detainees struggling for air before several individuals succumbed.

Government Action and Independent Panel

In response to the incident, Minister of Interior Olubunmi Tunji Ojo announced sweeping suspensions on Saturday, targeting the Niger State NSCDC Commandant, Suberu Siyaka Aniviye, alongside 20 other personnel involved in station guard, arrest, investigation, intelligence, legal, and guard duties. The suspended officers were subsequently detained in Abuja.

To ensure a thorough inquiry, the Federal Government established a 10-member independent investigative committee chaired by retired Department of State Services Deputy Director General Jonathan Kure, with former Nigerian Law School Director General Prof. Isa Hayatu Chiroma serving as secretary. Additional members include retired AIG Hosea Hassan Karma, Prof. Olayinka Buhari, representatives of the Minna Emirate Council and the Niger State Government, Liman Sulaiman of the Miners Association of Nigeria, human rights lawyer Deji Adeyanju, journalist Zainab Suleiman Okino, and public affairs analyst George Agbakahi.

The panel has been mandated to:

  • Establish the identities of the deceased.
  • Investigate the circumstances surrounding the arrests and detention.
  • Determine the cause and exact circumstances of the deaths.
  • Assess responsibility, complicity, negligence, and misconduct.
  • Recommend appropriate disciplinary actions, compensation where applicable, and preventive measures.

The committee, which has a two-week window to submit its final report, is authorized to co-opt experts, visit relevant locations, review official documents, and accept public memoranda. Minister Tunji Ojo strongly warned that any attempts to obstruct the probe, intimidate witnesses, or conceal evidence would be treated as grave offenses.

Public Reactions and Detention Concerns

While official investigations continue—including separate forensic examinations and autopsies ordered by the Niger State Government—the incident has reignited discussions on the dangers of overcrowded holding facilities. Sharing his perspective via his X handle, former Kaduna Central lawmaker Senator Shehu Sani drew from his historical detention experience under military rule to highlight the severe hazards of congested cells as reported by Punch Newspapers. Sani emphasized the physical toll of poor ventilation and overcrowding, asserting that all parties found culpable should face the full weight of the law.

As state and federal authorities await the findings of the 10-member panel detailed by The Reporter, questions surrounding accountability and custodial standards remain central to the ongoing investigations.
