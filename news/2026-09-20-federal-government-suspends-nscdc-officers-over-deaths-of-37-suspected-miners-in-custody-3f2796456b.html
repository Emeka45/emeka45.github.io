Federal Government Suspends NSCDC Officers Over Deaths of 37 Suspected Miners in Custody

The Federal Government has taken swift administrative action by suspending 20 Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps officers, including the Niger State Commandant, following the tragic deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody. Meanwhile, civil society organizations are pressing for further accountability by demanding the identification, arrest, and prosecution of the mining site owner.

The Federal Government has taken swift administrative action by suspending 20 officers of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC), including the Niger State Commandant, following the tragic deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody. Minister of Interior Olubunmi Tunji Ojo announced the suspensions on Saturday alongside the formation of a 10-member independent investigative panel.

Government Response and Investigation Panel

The administrative measures follow a directive from President Bola Tinubu for a comprehensive inquiry into the incident, which occurred on Thursday, September 17, 2026. The newly constituted independent committee is tasked with establishing the identities of the deceased individuals, probing the circumstances of their arrest and detention, and determining the direct cause of their deaths. Furthermore, the panel will examine issues of responsibility, complicity, negligence, and misconduct, while offering recommendations for preventive measures and compensation where applicable.

The 20 suspended personnel comprise officers involved in station guard, arrest, investigation, intelligence, legal, and guard duties. Additionally, Niger State Commandant Suberu Aniviye was suspended separately. The Minister instructed all relevant officers and NSCDC leadership to cooperate fully with the probe, warning that any obstruction, witness intimidation, or destruction of material evidence would constitute a serious offense. Chaired by retired Department of State Services Deputy Director General Jonathan Kure, with former Nigerian Law School Director General Prof. Isa Hayatu Chiroma serving as secretary, the committee has been given a two-week window to submit its report.

Civil Society Pressure and Accountability Demands

Meanwhile, civil society organizations are pressing for broader accountability by demanding the identification, arrest, and prosecution of the owner of the mining site where the victims were initially detained (Niger tragedy: CSOs demand site owner’s identity). The calls intensified after a survivor alleged that the gold-mining site belonged to former military Head of State General Ibrahim Babangida (retd.). An aide to the former leader, Dr. Danladi Abdulhameed, rejected the claim, describing the reports linking him to the operation as false and baseless.

Despite the denial, civic groups such as the Centre for Anti-Corruption and Open Leadership and Urban Alert have insisted that the probe should extend beyond detention conditions to uncover the individuals behind the unregistered mining activities. Activists argue that illegal mining fuels criminality and economic sabotage, calling for stringent enforcement of the Mining Act and accountability for any licensed entities found in violation of regulatory standards.
