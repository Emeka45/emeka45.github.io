Federal Government Suspends NSCDC Officers Over Deaths of 37 Suspected Miners in Custody

The Federal Government has taken swift administrative action by suspending 20 Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps officers, including the Niger State Commandant, following the tragic deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody. Meanwhile, civil society organizations are pressing for further accountability by demanding the identification, arrest, and prosecution of the mining site owner.

The Federal Government has taken swift administrative action by suspending 20 officers of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC), including the Niger State Commandant, following the tragic deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody. The incident, which occurred on Thursday, September 17, 2026, prompted a directive from President Bola Tinubu for a comprehensive investigation.

Suspensions and Investigation Panel

Minister of Interior Olubunmi Tunji Ojo announced the suspensions and constituted a 10-member independent investigative committee. The suspended personnel include the Niger State Commandant, Suberu Aniviye, as well as 20 other officers involved in station guard, arrest, investigation, intelligence, legal, and guard duties. The panel is chaired by retired Department of State Services Deputy Director General Jonathan Kure, with former Nigerian Law School Director General Prof. Isa Hayatu Chiroma serving as secretary.

Other committee members include retired AIG Hosea Hassan Karma, Prof. Olayinka Buhari, representatives of the Minna Emirate Council and the Niger State Government, Miners Association of Nigeria National Secretary Liman Sulaiman, human rights lawyer Deji Adeyanju, Blueprint journalist Zainab Suleiman Okino, and public affairs analyst George Agbakahi. The panel has been given a mandate of two weeks to establish the identities of the deceased, determine the circumstances of their arrest and detention, ascertain the cause of death, evaluate responsibility and negligence, and recommend appropriate actions, compensation, and preventive measures.

Tunji Ojo directed all relevant officers to cooperate fully with the probe and preserve records and material evidence, warning that any obstruction or concealment would be treated as a serious offense.

Civil Society Demands and Site Ownership Controversy

Meanwhile, civil society organizations are pressing for broader accountability. Groups such as the Centre for Anti-Corruption and Open Leadership, represented by Executive Director Debo Adeniran, and Urban Alert, led by Anthony Adejuwon, have demanded that the investigation go beyond the detention circumstances to identify, arrest, and prosecute the owner and operators of the illegal mining site.

The calls for accountability intensified following reports that a survivor, Malam Babawo Tudun Nasera, alleged the gold-mining site belonged to former military Head of State General Ibrahim Babangida (retd.). However, Babangida's aide, Dr. Danladi Abdulhameed, strongly denied the claim, describing the report as false, baseless, and completely without foundation. The NSCDC stated that it does not know the actual owner of the land, while security sources noted that illegal mining sites in Niger State are frequently linked to powerful local figures.

International Appeals and Next Steps

In addition to local civil society pressure, the Socio-Economic Rights and Accountability Project (SERAP), through Deputy Director Kolawole Oluwadare, urged United Nations Special Rapporteurs to intervene and demand answers regarding the deaths in custody and reported use of force against protesters in Minna. As the independent panel commences its two-week inquiry, stakeholders continue to push for thorough transparency, adherence to international human rights standards, and stronger enforcement of mining regulations across the country.
