The Federal Government has taken swift administrative action by suspending 20 officers of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC), alongside the Niger State Commandant, following the tragic deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody. The incident occurred on Thursday, September 17, 2026, prompting widespread concern and demands for accountability across the country (FG Suspends 20 NSCDC Officers, Sets Up Panel Over 37 Deaths In Custody).

Government Response and Investigation Panel

In response to the tragedy, Minister of Interior Olubunmi Tunji Ojo announced the suspensions and constituted a 10-member independent investigative committee following a directive from President Bola Tinubu (FG Suspends 20 NSCDC Officers, Sets Up Panel Over 37 Deaths In Custody). The committee is chaired by retired Department of State Services Deputy Director General Jonathan Kure, with former Nigerian Law School Director General Prof. Isa Hayatu Chiroma serving as secretary (FG Suspends 20 NSCDC Officers, Sets Up Panel Over 37 Deaths In Custody).

Other members of the panel include retired AIG Hosea Hassan Karma, Prof. Olayinka Buhari, representatives from the Minna Emirate Council and the Niger State Government, Miners Association of Nigeria National Secretary Liman Sulaiman, human rights lawyer Deji Adeyanju, journalist Zainab Suleiman Okino, and public affairs analyst George Agbakahi (FG Suspends 20 NSCDC Officers, Sets Up Panel Over 37 Deaths In Custody). The panel has been given a two-week window to ascertain the identities of the deceased, establish the circumstances surrounding their arrest and detention, determine the cause of death, identify any negligence or misconduct, and recommend appropriate measures (FG Suspends 20 NSCDC Officers, Sets Up Panel Over 37 Deaths In Custody).

Civil Society and Public Demands

Meanwhile, civil society organizations are pressing for further accountability by demanding the identification, arrest, and prosecution of the mining site owner (Niger tragedy: CSOs demand site owner’s identity). Groups such as the Centre for Anti-Corruption and Open Leadership and Urban Alert have insisted that the probe should look beyond the detention facility to uncover those operating the illegal mining site (Niger tragedy: CSOs demand site owner’s identity).
