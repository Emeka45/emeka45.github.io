Federal Government Suspends NSCDC Officers Over 37 Miners' Deaths as Oyo Governor Calls for Mining Devolution

The Federal Government has suspended 20 Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps officers and the Niger State Commandant following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody. Meanwhile, Oyo State Governor Seyi Makinde has used the tragedy to advocate for devolving mining responsibilities to state governments.

The Federal Government has suspended 20 officers of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC), alongside the Niger State Commandant, following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners held in the corps' custody. The tragedy, which occurred on Thursday, September 17, 2026, prompted a directive from President Bola Tinubu for a comprehensive investigation into the incident.

Government Response and Investigation Panel

Announcing the suspensions on Saturday, Minister of Interior Olubunmi Tunji Ojo constituted a 10-member independent investigative committee. The panel is tasked with establishing the identities of the deceased, uncovering the circumstances surrounding their arrest and detention, and determining the direct cause of death. Furthermore, the committee will examine issues of responsibility, potential complicity, negligence, and misconduct, while providing recommendations for preventive measures and compensation where applicable.

The investigative body is chaired by Jonathan Kure, a retired Deputy Director General of the Department of State Services, with Prof. Isa Hayatu Chiroma, former Director General of the Nigerian Law School, serving as secretary. Other prominent members include retired AIG Hosea Hassan Karma, Prof. Olayinka Buhari, and representatives from the Minna Emirate Council and the Niger State Government. Additional members comprise Liman Sulaiman, National Secretary of the Miners Association of Nigeria; human rights lawyer Deji Adeyanju; journalist Zainab Suleiman Okino; and public affairs analyst George Agbakahi. The panel has been given a two-week window to complete its assignment and submit its report.

The suspended personnel include the Niger State Commandant, Suberu Aniviye, as well as 20 other officers involved in station guard, arrest, investigation, intelligence, legal, and guard duties. Minister Tunji Ojo warned that any attempts to obstruct the investigation, intimidate witnesses, or conceal evidence would be treated as severe offences.

Calls for Mining Devolution

In the wake of the incident, Oyo State Governor and Allied Peoples Movement presidential candidate Seyi Makinde used the tragedy to advocate for systemic reforms during a town hall meeting in Katsina, as reported by Punch Nigeria. Makinde argued that state governments would perform significantly better if given greater administrative control over the solid minerals sector.

Pointing out that mining currently sits on the Exclusive Legislative List of the Nigerian Constitution, Makinde suggested devolving operational responsibilities to the states while maintaining federal oversight. He emphasized that the focus should extend beyond the supply side of illegal mining to address the underlying economic pressures driving individuals toward such risks.
