Federal Government Suspends NSCDC Officers Over 37 Miners' Deaths as Oyo Governor Calls for Mining Devolution

The Federal Government has suspended 20 Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps officers and the Niger State Commandant following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody. Meanwhile, Oyo State Governor Seyi Makinde has used the tragedy to advocate for devolving mining responsibilities to state governments.

The Federal Government has taken decisive administrative action by suspending 20 officers of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC), alongside the Niger State Commandant. The suspensions follow the tragic deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners who were held in custody, an incident that has drawn widespread national attention and sparked formal investigations (The Reporter).

In response to the fatalities, authorities have established an investigative panel to probe the circumstances surrounding the custody deaths. The incident has intensified ongoing debates regarding operational oversight, institutional accountability, and the management of solid minerals and mining security across the country.

Governor Makinde Calls for Devolution

Weighing in on the wider implications of the tragedy, Oyo State Governor Seyi Makinde has called for a major shift in the governance of the mining sector. According to Makinde, individual states would achieve better regulatory outcomes and prevent similar catastrophes if control over mining responsibilities were effectively devolved to sub-national governments (Punch Nigeria).

The disaster highlights the complex challenges facing the regulation of solid minerals in Nigeria, particularly the policing of informal mining sites and the protocols governing the detention of suspects. As the newly formed investigative panel begins its work, further details regarding accountability, institutional reforms, and potential policy changes regarding state-level mineral control remain anticipated.
