Federal Government Suspends NSCDC Officers Over 37 Miners' Deaths as Oyo Governor Calls for Mining Devolution

The Federal Government has suspended 20 Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps officers and the Niger State Commandant following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody. Meanwhile, Oyo State Governor Seyi Makinde has used the tragedy to advocate for devolving mining responsibilities to state governments.

The Federal Government has taken disciplinary action against 20 personnel of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC), including the Niger State Commandant, following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners held in custody. The incident, which took place on Thursday, September 17, 2026, prompted a directive from President Bola Tinubu for a comprehensive investigation.

Government Action and Independent Probe Panel

Minister of Interior Olubunmi Tunji Ojo announced the suspension of the 20 officers—covering station guard, arrest, investigation, intelligence, legal, and guard duties—alongside the separate suspension of Niger State Commandant Suberu Aniviye. To investigate the tragedy, the Federal Government constituted a 10-member independent committee.

The panel is chaired by retired Department of State Services Deputy Director General Jonathan Kure, with former Director General of the Nigerian Law School Prof. Isa Hayatu Chiroma serving as secretary. Other members include retired AIG Hosea Hassan Karma, Prof. Olayinka Buhari, representatives of the Minna Emirate Council and Niger State Government, Miners Association of Nigeria National Secretary Liman Sulaiman, human rights lawyer Deji Adeyanju, journalist Zainab Suleiman Okino, and public affairs analyst George Agbakahi.

The committee is tasked with identifying the deceased, examining the circumstances of their arrest and detention, determining the cause of death, assessing responsibility and potential negligence, and recommending preventative measures or compensation where applicable. The panel has been given two weeks to submit its report and was directed to ensure all material evidence and records are preserved.

Calls for Devolution of Mining Responsibilities

The custody deaths have spurred wider political discourse regarding the management of natural resources in Nigeria. Speaking in Katsina at a presidential town hall meeting, Oyo State Governor and Allied Peoples Movement presidential candidate Seyi Makinde used the tragedy to advocate for transferring mining control from the federal level to state governments (Punch Nigeria).

Makinde argued that state governments would perform better if granted greater oversight of the mining sector, proposing that the subject be moved off the Exclusive Legislative List while retaining federal-level oversight. Highlighting the plight of the young miners who lost their lives, Makinde emphasized the need to address both the supply side and demand side of unauthorized mining operations across the country.
