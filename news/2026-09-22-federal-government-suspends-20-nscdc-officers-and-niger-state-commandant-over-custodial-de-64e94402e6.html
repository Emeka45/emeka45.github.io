Federal Government Suspends 20 NSCDC Officers and Niger State Commandant Over Custodial Deaths of 37 Suspected Miners

The Federal Government has suspended 20 personnel of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps, including the Niger State Commandant, following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners held in custody in Minna.

The Federal Government has suspended 20 personnel of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC), including the Niger State Commandant, following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners who were held in the Corps' custody in Minna. Detailed reports on the incident and background can be found via The Reporter Media.

Background of the Arrests and Custodial Deaths

The victims were among scores of suspects arrested during operations conducted on September 15 and 16, 2026, around the Lt Gen Mohammed Inuwa Wushishi Estate in Minna, the Niger State capital. The former state commandant, Suberu Aniviye, previously stated that the detained suspects were found dead in the early hours of Thursday, September 17, following a suspected disease outbreak. Further reporting and compiled details regarding the victims are detailed in coverage by Punch Nigeria.

Government Action and Investigation Panel

Following a directive from President Bola Tinubu for a comprehensive investigation, Minister of Interior Olubunmi Tunji Ojo announced the suspension of the Niger State Commandant alongside 20 other personnel involved in station guard, arrest, investigation, intelligence, legal, and guard duties. Tunji Ojo also inaugurated a 10-member independent investigative committee.

The panel is chaired by retired Department of State Services Deputy Director General Jonathan Kure, with former Nigerian Law School Director General Prof. Isa Hayatu Chiroma serving as secretary. Other committee members include retired AIG Hosea Hassan Karma, Prof. Olayinka Buhari, representatives of the Minna Emirate Council and Niger State Government, Miners Association of Nigeria National Secretary Liman Sulaiman, human rights lawyer Deji Adeyanju, journalist Zainab Suleiman Okino, and public affairs analyst George Agbakahi.

The committee has been mandated to establish the identities of the deceased, investigate the circumstances of their arrest, detention, and subsequent deaths, determine responsibility, complicity, negligence, or misconduct, and recommend appropriate actions, including compensation where applicable.

Next Steps and Victim Identification

The International Human Rights Commission (IHRC) reported that it compiled an initial list of the deceased from General Hospital, Minna records, with additional names provided by relatives. The investigative panel has been given two weeks to complete its assignment, submit its report, and recommend measures to prevent future occurrences.
