Federal Government Suspends 20 NSCDC Officers and Niger State Commandant Over Custodial Deaths of 37 Suspected Miners

The Federal Government has suspended 20 personnel of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps, including the Niger State Commandant, following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners held in custody in Minna.

The Federal Government has suspended 20 personnel of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC), alongside the Niger State Commandant, following the tragic deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners held in the Corps' custody in Minna. The development follows operations conducted on September 15 and 16, 2026, around the Lt Gen Mohammed Inuwa Wushishi Estate in Minna, where scores of suspects were initially arrested (Punch Nigeria).

Suspensions and Investigation Panel

Minister of Interior Olubunmi Tunji Ojo announced the suspensions on Saturday, following a directive from President Bola Tinubu for a thorough investigation into the incident, which occurred in the early hours of Thursday, September 17, 2026 (The Reporter Media). The suspended personnel include the Niger State Commandant, Suberu Aniviye, as well as officers involved in station guard, arrest, investigation, intelligence, legal, and guard duties (The Reporter Media).

To investigate the matter, the government constituted a 10-member independent investigative committee chaired by retired Department of State Services Deputy Director General Jonathan Kure, with former Nigerian Law School Director General Prof. Isa Hayatu Chiroma serving as secretary (The Reporter Media). Other committee members include retired AIG Hosea Hassan Karma, Prof. Olayinka Buhari, representatives from the Minna Emirate Council and the Niger State Government, Miners Association of Nigeria National Secretary Liman Sulaiman, human rights lawyer Deji Adeyanju, journalist Zainab Suleiman Okino, and public affairs analyst George Agbakahi (The Reporter Media).

The committee has been tasked with establishing the identities of the deceased, uncovering the circumstances of their arrest and detention, and determining the direct cause of the deaths. Furthermore, the panel will assess responsibility, check for negligence or misconduct, and recommend appropriate corrective measures, including compensation where applicable (The Reporter Media). The panel has been given two weeks to complete its assignment and submit its report (The Reporter Media).

Emerging Details and Victim Identification

While the former state commandant previously attributed the deaths to a suspected outbreak of disease within the facility (Punch Nigeria), official confirmation regarding the final cause of death remains pending as the independent probe continues (The Reporter Media).

In the wake of the incident, the International Human Rights Commission (IHRC) compiled a comprehensive list of the 37 victims from records obtained at the General Hospital, Minna, with additional names provided by families of missing relatives (Punch Nigeria). According to Awaisu Giwa, International Peace Ambassador at the IHRC, this documentation will also assist in facilitating appropriate compensation for the bereaved families (Punch Nigeria).
