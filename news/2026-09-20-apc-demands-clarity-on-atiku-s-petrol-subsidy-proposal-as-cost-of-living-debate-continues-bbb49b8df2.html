APC Demands Clarity on Atiku's Petrol Subsidy Proposal as Cost of Living Debate Continues

The All Progressives Congress has challenged Atiku Abubakar to explain the legal and fiscal framework of his proposed petrol production subsidy, following Atiku's call for President Bola Tinubu to adopt measures to lower pump prices.

The All Progressives Congress Presidential Campaign Council has challenged former Vice President Atiku Abubakar to provide a comprehensive legal and fiscal framework for his proposed production subsidy on locally refined petroleum products. The political exchange follows Atiku's recent public call for President Bola Tinubu to adopt interventionist measures to lower pump prices and alleviate cost-of-living pressures across the country.

Atiku's Proposal and Government Response

In an address concerning fuel prices, palliatives, and the broader cost of living, Atiku urged President Tinubu to adopt his market intervention proposal to reduce petrol and diesel prices, suggesting the administration could even rename the policy and take credit for it if it eased the burden on citizens The Reporter Media. Atiku outlined that his proposed scheme would involve a transparent production subsidy specifically for petroleum products refined domestically and sold locally, backed by a fixed spending limit, National Assembly approval, and independent audits.

However, responding through a statement issued on Sunday by council spokesman Dele Alake, the Punch Newspapers reported that the APC-PCC challenged the feasibility of the plan. Alake argued that the proposal raises critical legal, fiscal, and practical questions, particularly regarding its compatibility with Section 205(1) of the Petroleum Industry Act 2021, which mandates unrestricted free-market pricing conditions for wholesale and retail petroleum products unless specific market failures are declared.

Fiscal Concerns and Regulatory Scrutiny

The APC-PCC questioned how public support to refiners would legally guarantee lower prices at filling stations without creating an enforceable pricing mechanism, warning that refiners might otherwise receive benefits while consumers continue paying market rates. Furthermore, the council estimated that based on publicly reported refinery throughput and domestic supply figures, the annual cost of the proposed subsidy could range between N17 trillion and N21 trillion, depending on parameters such as discount size and covered volumes.

The ruling party's campaign council also highlighted what it termed a policy contradiction, noting that Atiku previously pledged to complete the removal of the petrol subsidy regime—describing it as fraudulent during a November 2022 speech at the Lagos Business School—before later posting on X in August 2026 about restoring a form of subsidy Punch Newspapers. The APC maintained that the administration remains committed to a deregulated downstream market and alternative relief measures, such as expanding compressed natural gas and electric mass transit options to reduce transportation costs.
