Jehovah’s Witnesses Announce Adjustments to Blood Transfusion Guidelines

Jehovah's Witnesses have updated their policy regarding blood transfusions, allowing individual members to exercise personal conscience in deciding whether to accept or donate specific components like red cells, white cells, plasma, and platelets.

The Governing Body of Jehovah’s Witnesses has announced a significant adjustment to its long-standing blood transfusion policy. According to a statement issued from the organisation’s World Headquarters in Warwick, New York, and reported by The Reporter and Punch Newspapers, individual members are now permitted to exercise personal conscience when deciding whether to accept or donate specific components of blood.

Understanding the Policy Shift

Under the updated guidelines, individual Jehovah’s Witnesses can independently determine whether to accept or donate red cells, white cells, plasma, or platelets. Previously, strict organisational rules governed medical choices regarding these cellular components. The organisation clarified that because the Bible does not specifically address decisions concerning these four primary blood components, choices regarding them are appropriately left to the individual conscience of each believer.

International spokesman for Jehovah’s Witnesses, Paul Gillies, emphasised that blood remains deeply sacred to members, but underscored that medical choices involving these components are strictly matters of personal conscience between each adherent and their faith. Gillies noted, “We recognise that medical decisions involving blood components are a matter of conscience between each Witness and their God, Jehovah.”

Core Restrictions and Ongoing Practices

Despite the flexibility granted regarding individual blood components, the organisation’s core position prohibiting whole-blood transfusions remains firmly in place. The long-standing refusal of whole-blood transfusions is rooted in religious grounds, derived from the biblical command to “abstain from blood” (Acts 15:20).

Additionally, the organisation reiterated that Jehovah’s Witnesses actively seek professional medical treatment and do not engage in faith healing. The policy adjustment follows earlier clarifications introduced earlier in the year regarding blood fractions and the handling of a member’s own blood during surgical procedures. Under those prior guidelines, adherents were already permitted to decide whether their own blood could be withdrawn, stored, and subsequently reinfused during medical care.

Role of Local Congregations

To ensure personal autonomy is respected, the organisation confirmed that local congregations will not interfere in the personal medical decisions of their members. Gillies stressed that congregation leadership does not involve itself in choices that rightfully rest with individual judgment, affirming that local bodies will continue to support the personal decisions made by their fellow believers.

With a global community exceeding nine million members spanning more than 200 countries and territories, this policy update marks a notable evolution in how adherents navigate complex medical and religious intersections.
