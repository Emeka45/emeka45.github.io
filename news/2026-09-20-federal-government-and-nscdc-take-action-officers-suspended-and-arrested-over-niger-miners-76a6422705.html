Federal Government and NSCDC Take Action: Officers Suspended and Arrested Over Niger Miners' Deaths

The Federal Government and the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps have taken stringent measures, suspending 20 officers including the Niger State Commandant and arresting a total of 24 personnel following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody.

The Federal Government and the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC) have taken stringent disciplinary measures, placing a total of 24 personnel in custody following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners. The detainees tragically lost their lives while held in the custody of the Corps in Niger State on Thursday, September 17, 2026.

Government Action and Investigation Panel

Following a directive from President Bola Tinubu for a comprehensive, transparent, and unhindered investigation, Minister of Interior Olubunmi Tunji-Ojo announced the immediate suspension of all officers allegedly involved. On Saturday, the Federal Government constituted a 10-member independent investigative committee to examine the incident.

The presidential panel is tasked with establishing the identities of the deceased individuals, investigating the circumstances of their arrest and detention, and determining the direct cause of death. Furthermore, the committee will evaluate issues of responsibility, complicity, negligence, and misconduct, while formulating recommendations for appropriate actions, compensation where applicable, and preventive measures against future occurrences.

The investigative committee is chaired by retired Department of State Services Deputy Director General Jonathan Kure, with former Nigerian Law School Director General Prof. Isa Hayatu Chiroma serving as secretary. Other prominent members include retired AIG Hosea Hassan Karma, Prof. Olayinka Buhari, representatives from the Minna Emirate Council and the Niger State Government, Miners Association of Nigeria National Secretary Liman Sulaiman, human rights lawyer Deji Adeyanju, journalist Zainab Suleiman Okino, and public affairs analyst George Agbakahi. The panel has been granted a two-week window to complete its assignment and submit its report to the Minister of Interior.

NSCDC Arrests and Disciplinary Measures

Initially, the NSCDC reported the detention of 21 personnel. However, NSCDC Spokesman Assistant Commandant of Corps Babawale Afolabi disclosed that three additional personnel identified in connection with the tragedy were subsequently arrested and suspended, bringing the total number of detained officers to 24.

The suspended personnel—comprising individuals involved in station guard, arrest, investigation, intelligence, legal, and operational duties—include Niger State Commandant Subaru Siyaka Aniviye, Head of Intelligence and Investigation Ajayi Philips, Head of Operations John Agrahau Chagwa, Obafemi Elvis, and Officer-in-Charge of Legal Stephen Tsado, among others. Conducted jointly with the Commandant General’s Special Investigation Squad, the operation transferred all implicated officers to the NSCDC National Headquarters in Abuja for rigorous interrogation, administrative disciplinary action, and availability for the presidential panel and possible prosecution.

NSCDC Commandant General Ahmed Audi emphasized the Corps' commitment to due process, transparency, and accountability, declaring that any personnel found culpable will face the full weight of internal disciplinary measures and the law. As a mark of solemn mourning, the NSCDC ordered its flags to fly at half-mast across all formations nationwide for three days beginning Sunday, September 21, 2026, as reported by The Punch.

Directives and Oversight

Interior Minister Tunji-Ojo directed the leadership of the NSCDC and all relevant personnel to cooperate fully with the investigation while strictly preserving all material evidence and records connected to the incident. Authorities have issued stern warnings that any attempts to obstruct the probe, intimidate witnesses, or conceal evidence will be treated as grave offenses.
