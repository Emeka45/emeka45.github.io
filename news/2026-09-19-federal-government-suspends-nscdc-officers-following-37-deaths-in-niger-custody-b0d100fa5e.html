Federal Government Suspends NSCDC Officers Following 37 Deaths in Niger Custody

The Federal Government has suspended 20 Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps officers and the Niger State Commandant following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody, drawing condemnation and calls for a transparent investigation from political parties like the Allied Peoples Movement.

The Federal Government has suspended 20 officers of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC) alongside the Niger State Commandant following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody. Minister of Interior Olubunmi Tunji Ojo announced the suspensions on Saturday, establishing a 10-member independent committee to investigate the incident following a directive from President Bola Tinubu.

Background and Custody Incident

The 37 victims were among 66 suspected illegal miners arrested during separate operations conducted on September 15 and 16 in M.I. Wushishi and Lukoto villages in Niger State. They were subsequently detained in Minna. While state authorities previously pointed to a suspected disease outbreak, the Allied Peoples Movement (Punch Newspapers) and other critics highlighted severe overcrowding and alleged inhumane conditions, noting that 66 detainees were confined in a single cell.

The incident also sparked street protests in Minna, during which security agencies reportedly used ammunition and tear gas.

Investigation Panel and Terms of Reference

The newly formed 10-member investigative panel is chaired by retired Department of State Services Deputy Director General Jonathan Kure, with former Nigerian Law School Director General Prof. Isa Hayatu Chiroma serving as secretary. Other committee members include retired AIG Hosea Hassan Karma, Prof. Olayinka Buhari, representatives from the Minna Emirate Council and Niger State Government, Miners Association of Nigeria National Secretary Liman Sulaiman, human rights lawyer Deji Adeyanju, journalist Zainab Suleiman Okino, and public affairs analyst George Agbakahi.

According to The Reporter, the committee has been given a two-week window to complete its assignment. Its mandate includes:

  • Establishing the identities of the deceased.
  • Investigating the circumstances of their arrest and detention.
  • Determining the exact cause of death, responsibility, complicity, negligence, or misconduct.
  • Recommending appropriate disciplinary actions, compensation where applicable, and preventive safeguards.

Suspensions and Directives

The suspended personnel include the Niger State Commandant, Suberu Aniviye, as well as 20 other officers involved in station guard, arrest, investigation, intelligence, legal, and guard duties. Minister Tunji Ojo warned that any attempts to obstruct the investigation, intimidate witnesses, or conceal evidence will be treated as severe offenses, directing all relevant units to preserve records and material evidence.

Political bodies, including the Allied Peoples Movement, have called for an unrestricted, open, and transparent probe into both the detention fatalities and the handling of the subsequent protests.
