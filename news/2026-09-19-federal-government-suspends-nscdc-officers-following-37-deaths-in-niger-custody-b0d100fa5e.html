Federal Government Suspends NSCDC Officers Following 37 Deaths in Niger Custody

The Federal Government has suspended 20 Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps officers and the Niger State Commandant following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody, drawing condemnation and calls for a transparent investigation from political parties like the Allied Peoples Movement.

The Federal Government has suspended 20 officers of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC) alongside the Niger State Commandant following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody. The directive follows a presidential call for a comprehensive investigation into the incident, which occurred on Thursday, September 17, 2026.

Background to the Arrests and Detainee Deaths

According to disclosures by the NSCDC Niger State Commandant, Suberu Aniviye, the 37 deceased individuals were among suspected illegal miners arrested during separate operations conducted on September 15 and 16 in M.I. Wushishi and Lukoto villages in Niger State. While authorities initially claimed the detainees died following a suspected disease outbreak in the cell, the Allied Peoples Movement (Punch Newspapers) and other critics highlighted reports that 66 detainees were cramped into a single cell under inhumane conditions, suffering from excessive heat and alleged exposure to an unidentified substance.

Government Action and Independent Panel Formed

Minister of Interior Olubunmi Tunji Ojo announced the suspensions on Saturday, September 19, 2026, encompassing the Niger State Commandant and 20 other personnel involved in station guard, arrest, investigation, intelligence, legal, and guard duties (The Reporter). Concurrently, the Federal Government constituted a 10-member independent investigative committee chaired by retired Department of State Services Deputy Director General Jonathan Kure, with former Nigerian Law School Director General Prof. Isa Hayatu Chiroma serving as secretary.

Other members of the probe panel include retired AIG Hosea Hassan Karma, Prof. Olayinka Buhari, representatives from the Minna Emirate Council and the Niger State Government, Miners Association of Nigeria National Secretary Liman Sulaiman, human rights lawyer Deji Adeyanju, journalist Zainab Suleiman Okino, and public affairs analyst George Agbakahi.

Mandate and Next Steps

The committee is tasked with establishing the identities of the deceased, investigating the circumstances surrounding their arrest and detention, and determining the exact causes of death. The panel will also assign responsibility, examine potential negligence or misconduct, and recommend appropriate measures, including compensation where applicable and safeguards to prevent future occurrences.

Minister Tunji Ojo warned that any attempts to conceal evidence, intimidate witnesses, or obstruct the probe would be dealt with severely. The panel has been given a timeframe of two weeks to complete its assignment and submit its report.
