Federal Government Suspends NSCDC Officers Following 37 Deaths in Niger Custody

The Federal Government has suspended 20 Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps officers and the Niger State Commandant following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody, drawing condemnation and calls for a transparent investigation from political parties like the Allied Peoples Movement.

The Federal Government has taken decisive administrative action by suspending 20 officers of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC), alongside the Niger State Commandant, following the tragic deaths of 37 individuals held in custody. The deceased were reportedly arrested as suspected illegal miners in Niger State before losing their lives while in detention.

The incident has drawn sharp reactions from political bodies across the country. Notably, the Allied Peoples Movement (APM) has publicly condemned the loss of 37 lives in security custody. In its official response, the party strongly demanded a thorough, open, and transparent investigation to uncover the circumstances that led to the fatalities and to ensure accountability for those involved, as detailed by Punch Newspapers.

In response to the public outcry and the gravity of the incident, authorities have constituted an investigative panel. The suspension of the Niger State Commandant and the 20 personnel is intended to pave the way for an unhindered inquiry into the matter, according to reports by The Reporter.

As developments unfold, the investigative panel is expected to review the operational conduct of the officers on duty and determine the direct causes behind the deaths in custody. The public and various stakeholders remain expectant regarding the findings of the panel and the subsequent administrative or legal steps the government will take.
