Federal Government Suspends NSCDC Officers Following 37 Deaths in Niger Custody

The Federal Government has suspended 20 Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps officers and the Niger State Commandant following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody, drawing condemnation and calls for a transparent investigation from political parties like the Allied Peoples Movement.

The Federal Government has suspended 20 officers of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC) alongside the Niger State Commandant following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody. The incident, which drew widespread public concern and calls for transparency from political organizations such as the Allied Peoples Movement (Punch Newspapers), has prompted high-level administrative action.

Background and Government Action

The 37 victims were among a group of suspected illegal miners arrested during separate operations conducted on September 15 and 16 in M.I. Wushishi and Lukoto villages in Niger State. According to disclosures from the NSCDC state command, the suspects were being held in a detention facility in Minna before the fatalities occurred on Thursday, September 17, 2026. Following the incident, Minister of Interior Olubunmi Tunji Ojo announced the suspension of the 20 personnel involved in station guard, arrest, investigation, intelligence, legal, and guard duties, alongside the separate suspension of Niger State Commandant Suberu Aniviye (The Reporter).

The suspensions follow a directive by President Bola Tinubu for a comprehensive investigation into the events leading to the deaths. Political parties and public interest groups have raised questions regarding the conditions of detention, pointing to reports of overcrowding and alleged exposure to extreme heat and chemical substances inside the facility.

Independent Investigation Panel

To oversee the probe, the Federal Government constituted a 10-member independent investigative committee chaired by Jonathan Kure, a retired Deputy Director General of the Department of State Services, with Prof. Isa Hayatu Chiroma serving as secretary. Other prominent figures on the panel include retired AIG Hosea Hassan Karma, Prof. Olayinka Buhari, representatives of the Minna Emirate Council and the Niger State Government, Liman Sulaiman of the Miners Association of Nigeria, human rights lawyer Deji Adeyanju, journalist Zainab Suleiman Okino, and public affairs analyst George Agbakahi (The Reporter).

The committee's mandate includes:

  • Establishing the identities of the deceased detainees.
  • Investigating the circumstances surrounding their arrest and detention.
  • Determining the direct cause of death and evaluating potential negligence, misconduct, or complicity.
  • Recommending appropriate disciplinary actions, compensation where applicable, and structural safeguards to prevent future occurrences.

The Minister of Interior has directed all relevant agencies to preserve records and material evidence, warning that any obstruction, witness intimidation, or evidence tampering will be treated as a serious offense. The panel has been given a two-week timeframe to complete its assignment and submit its official report (The Reporter).
