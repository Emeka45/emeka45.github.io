Federal Government Suspends NSCDC Officers Following 37 Deaths in Niger Custody

The Federal Government has suspended 20 Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps officers and the Niger State Commandant following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody, drawing condemnation and calls for a transparent investigation from political parties like the Allied Peoples Movement.

The Federal Government has suspended 20 officers of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC) alongside the Niger State Commandant following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody. The decisive actions were announced by Minister of Interior Olubunmi Tunji-Ojo following a directive from President Bola Tinubu for a comprehensive investigation into the incident, which occurred on Thursday, September 17, 2026.

Background of the Arrests and Custody Conditions

The 37 victims were among a total of 66 suspects arrested during separate operations conducted on September 15 and 16 in M.I. Wushishi and Lukoto villages in Niger State. According to reports, the detainees were confined within a single cell. The Allied Peoples Movement (APM), through its National Publicity Secretary Abubakar Yusuf, condemned the fatalities as avoidable and criticized the conditions of confinement, noting that detainees were held for over two days in spaces that exposed them to excessive heat and alleged overcrowding. Authorities previously stated that the suspects died following a suspected disease outbreak in the cell, though critics and political organizations have pointed to the harsh conditions under which the detainees were held.

Government Action and Independent Investigation Panel

In response to the tragedy, Minister Olubunmi Tunji-Ojo constituted a 10-member independent investigative committee on Saturday, September 19, 2026. The panel is chaired by Jonathan Kure, a retired Department of State Services Deputy Director General, with Prof. Isa Hayatu Chiroma, former Director General of the Nigerian Law School, serving as secretary. Other prominent members include retired AIG Hosea Hassan Karma, Prof. Olayinka Buhari, representatives from the Minna Emirate Council and the Niger State Government, Miners Association of Nigeria National Secretary Liman Sulaiman, human rights lawyer Deji Adeyanju, journalist Zainab Suleiman Okino, and public affairs analyst George Agbakahi.

The committee's mandate includes:

  • Establishing the identities of the deceased detainees.
  • Investigating the circumstances surrounding their arrest and detention.
  • Determining the precise cause and circumstances of the deaths.
  • Assessing responsibility, complicity, negligence, or misconduct among personnel.
  • Recommending appropriate actions, compensation where applicable, and preventive safeguards.

The investigative panel has been given a two-week timeframe to complete its assignment and submit its report to the Minister of Interior. The committee is empowered to co-opt relevant experts, inspect locations connected to the incident, review necessary documents, and receive public memoranda. The Minister directed all relevant NSCDC personnel to fully cooperate and warned that any attempts to obstruct the probe, intimidate witnesses, or conceal evidence would be treated as severe offenses.

Suspension of Personnel and Public Reactions

The suspended personnel include Niger State Commandant Suberu Aniviye, alongside 20 other officers tied to station guard, arrest, investigation, intelligence, legal, and general guard duties. The incident has drawn widespread public attention and political commentary, including concerns raised regarding the use of security ammunition and tear gas against protesters who took to the streets in Minna following the announcement of the deaths.

Political parties such as the APM have demanded an open, transparent, and unrestricted inquiry into both the cell fatalities and the management of subsequent street protests, emphasizing that the dignity of citizens must be strictly upheld under the law.
