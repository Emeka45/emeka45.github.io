Federal Government Suspends 20 NSCDC Officers Over 37 Custodial Deaths in Niger State

The Federal Government has suspended 20 Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps officers and the Niger State Commandant following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody, as Niger State Governor Umar Bago stated the state was unaware of their arrest.

The Federal Government has suspended 20 officers of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps alongside the Niger State Commandant following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners held in custody. Minister of Interior Olubunmi Tunji Ojo announced the suspensions while establishing a 10-member independent investigative committee following a directive from President Bola Tinubu.

Background of the Custodial Deaths

The tragedy unfolded after operations against illegal mining conducted by NSCDC personnel on September 15 and 16 in the M.I. Wushishi and Lukoto areas of Niger State. A total of 67 artisanal and suspected illegal miners were taken into custody at an NSCDC facility in Minna, out of which 37 subsequently died on Thursday, September 17. The state government maintained it had no prior knowledge of the arrests or the detention facility operations until news of the fatalities and subsequent protests emerged.

Speaking during a television interview, Niger State Governor Mohammed Umaru Bago stated that the state authorities were completely uninformed about the operation because the security agencies involved operate under federal control. Bago noted that authorities are examining potential factors including overcrowding and possible exposure to hazardous substances, though a final official cause of death has yet to be established.

Federal Investigation and Interventions

To ensure accountability, Minister Tunji Ojo constituted an independent panel chaired by retired Department of State Services Deputy Director General Jonathan Kure, with former Nigerian Law School Director General Prof. Isa Hayatu Chiroma serving as secretary. The 10-member committee has been tasked with identifying the deceased, investigating the circumstances of their arrest and detention, determining the cause of death, assessing any negligence or misconduct, and recommending appropriate actions and compensation where applicable.

The suspended personnel include the Niger State Commandant, Suberu Aniviye, as well as 20 other officers involved in station guard, arrest, investigation, intelligence, legal, and guard duties. Additional internal disciplinary procedures have also seen a total of up to 27 personnel held at the NSCDC National Headquarters in Abuja for interrogation, with authorities pledging to make them available to the presidential investigative panel and other relevant bodies.

The independent committee has been given two weeks to complete its assignment, submit its report to the Minister of Interior, and recommend measures to prevent similar occurrences in the future. Meanwhile, Niger State Governor Mohammed Bago continues to draw attention to broader socio-economic challenges tied to artisanal mining, pointing out the lack of protective equipment, precarious living conditions, and the difficulties of shifting community dependence away from the lucrative mining sector.
