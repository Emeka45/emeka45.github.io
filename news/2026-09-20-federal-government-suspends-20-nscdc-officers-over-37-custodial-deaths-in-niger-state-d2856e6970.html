Federal Government Suspends 20 NSCDC Officers Over 37 Custodial Deaths in Niger State

The Federal Government has suspended 20 Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps officers and the Niger State Commandant following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody, as Niger State Governor Umar Bago stated the state was unaware of their arrest.

The Federal Government has suspended 20 officers of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC) alongside the Niger State Commandant following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners held in custody. The directive follows a presidential order for a comprehensive investigation into the incident, which occurred on Thursday, September 17, 2026, after operations conducted by NSCDC personnel.

Government Action and Investigation Panel

Minister of Interior Olubunmi Tunji Ojo announced the suspensions and constituted a 10-member independent investigative committee. The panel is mandated to establish the identities of the deceased, examine the circumstances surrounding their arrest and detention, determine the cause of death, and identify any negligence, complicity, or misconduct.

The committee is chaired by Jonathan Kure, a retired Deputy Director General of the Department of State Services, with Prof. Isa Hayatu Chiroma, former Director General of the Nigerian Law School, serving as secretary. Other members include retired AIG Hosea Hassan Karma, Prof. Olayinka Buhari, representatives of the Minna Emirate Council and the Niger State Government, Liman Sulaiman of the Miners Association of Nigeria, human rights activist Deji Adeyanju, journalist Zainab Suleiman Okino, and public affairs analyst George Agbakahi.

The panel has been given two weeks to complete its assignment and submit its report. Interior Minister Tunji Ojo warned that any attempt to destroy evidence, intimidate witnesses, or obstruct the probe would face severe legal consequences.

Niger State Government Position

Speaking on the development, Niger State Governor Mohammed Umaru Bago stated that the state government had no prior knowledge of the arrests or detention. Appearing on Channels Television, Governor Bago explained that the 67 artisanal and suspected illegal miners were picked up during operations in the M.I. Wushishi and Lukoto areas on September 15 and 16, 2026, but local authorities only learned of the situation after protests erupted following the fatalities.

“The state had no prior knowledge of the operation because the security agencies involved were under the control of the Federal Government,” Bago stated, highlighting ongoing concerns regarding security operations and the broader socioeconomic challenges tied to artisanal mining in the region.

Internal NSCDC Measures

The suspended personnel span various operational roles, including station guard, arrest, investigation, intelligence, legal, and general guard duties, alongside Niger State Commandant Suberu Aniviye. Further details can be found in the report by The Reporter Media. Additionally, reports from Punch indicate that dozens of personnel have been held at national headquarters for ongoing administrative interrogation ahead of further legal or disciplinary processing.
