Federal Government Suspends 20 NSCDC Officers Over 37 Custodial Deaths in Niger State

The Federal Government has suspended 20 Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps officers and the Niger State Commandant following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody, as Niger State Governor Umar Bago stated the state was unaware of their arrest.

The Federal Government has suspended 20 officers of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC), alongside the Niger State Commandant, following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in the Corps' custody. The tragedy, which unfolded in Niger State, has prompted high-level investigations and drawn sharp reactions from state authorities regarding coordination between federal security agencies and local governance.

Background of the Arrests and Custodial Deaths

The sequence of events began when NSCDC personnel conducted security operations against illegal mining in the M.I. Wushishi and Lukoto areas of Niger State on September 15 and 16, 2026. During these operations, 67 artisanal and suspected illegal miners were taken into custody and detained at an NSCDC facility in Minna. Out of those detained, 37 individuals subsequently lost their lives while in custody.

Niger State Governor Mohammed Umaru Bago revealed that the state government was completely unaware of the arrests and subsequent detentions until news of the fatalities became public and protests erupted. Speaking during an interview on Channels Television, Governor Bago noted that because the security agencies involved operate under federal control, local authorities had no prior knowledge of the operation.

Federal Government Intervention and Suspension of Officers

In response to the incident, Minister of Interior Olubunmi Tunji Ojo announced the immediate suspension of 20 NSCDC officers involved in station guard, arrest, investigation, intelligence, legal, and guard duties. The Niger State Commandant, Suberu Aniviye, was also suspended separately. Furthermore, reports indicate that dozens of personnel allegedly connected to the incident have been detained at the NSCDC National Headquarters in Abuja to undergo interrogation and administrative disciplinary procedures.

The swift administrative actions follow a directive by President Bola Tinubu ordering a comprehensive and independent investigation into the circumstances surrounding the deaths. To execute this, the Federal Government constituted a 10-member Presidential Independent Investigation Committee.

Mandate of the Investigation Panel

The newly formed independent panel is chaired by Jonathan Kure, a retired Deputy Director General of the Department of State Services, with Prof. Isa Hayatu Chiroma, former Director General of the Nigerian Law School, serving as secretary. Other prominent members include retired AIG Hosea Hassan Karma, Prof. Olayinka Buhari, representatives of the Minna Emirate Council and the Niger State Government, Liman Sulaiman of the Miners Association of Nigeria, human rights activist Deji Adeyanju, journalist Zainab Suleiman Okino, and public affairs analyst George Agbakahi.

The committee is tasked with:

  • Establishing the identities of the deceased.
  • Investigating the circumstances surrounding their arrest and detention.
  • Determining the precise cause of death, with overcrowding and possible exposure to hazardous substances among the factors examined.
  • Assessing responsibility, complicity, negligence, and misconduct.
  • Recommending appropriate disciplinary actions, compensation where applicable, and preventive measures.

Minister Tunji Ojo directed all relevant personnel to cooperate fully with the panel and safeguard all material evidence and records. He cautioned that any attempts to obstruct the probe, destroy evidence, or intimidate witnesses would be treated as severe offenses. The committee has been given a timeframe of two weeks to complete its assignment and submit its official report to the Ministry of Interior.
