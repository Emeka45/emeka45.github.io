Federal Government Suspends 20 NSCDC Officers Over 37 Custodial Deaths in Niger State

The Federal Government has suspended 20 Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps officers and the Niger State Commandant following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody, as Niger State Governor Umar Bago stated the state was unaware of their arrest.

The Federal Government has suspended 20 officers of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps, alongside the Niger State Commandant, following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners held in custody. The directive follows events that unfolded in Niger State, drawing high-level federal intervention and raising questions regarding custodial oversight and inter-governmental communication during security operations.

Background of the Custodial Deaths

The detainees were among 67 artisanal and suspected illegal miners taken into custody by NSCDC personnel during operations targeting illegal mining activities in the M.I. Wushishi and Lukoto areas of Niger State. The operations were conducted on September 15 and 16, 2026. Out of the 67 individuals detained at the facility in Minna, 37 subsequently died in custody.

Niger State Governor Mohammed Umaru Bago disclosed that the state government was completely uninformed about the arrests or the subsequent detention until news of the fatalities emerged and protests began. Speaking on the matter, Governor Bago noted that because security agencies operate under the control of the Federal Government, local authorities had no prior knowledge of the operation.

Federal Government Response and Suspensions

In response to the incident, Minister of Interior Olubunmi Tunji Ojo announced the suspension of 20 NSCDC personnel—including those assigned to station guard, arrest, investigation, intelligence, legal, and guard duties—as well as the separate suspension of Niger State Commandant Suberu Aniviye. Furthermore, reports noted that dozens of personnel allegedly connected to the incident faced detention and interrogation at the Corps' National Headquarters in Abuja.

President Bola Tinubu ordered a comprehensive investigation into the tragedy. To execute this directive, the Federal Government constituted a 10-member independent investigative committee chaired by Jonathan Kure, a retired Deputy Director General of the Department of State Services, with Prof. Isa Hayatu Chiroma serving as secretary.

Investigation Mandate and Next Steps

The independent committee is tasked with establishing the true identities of the deceased, uncovering the circumstances surrounding their arrest and detention, and determining the direct causes of death. The panel holds a two-week window to complete its assignment, evaluate potential negligence or misconduct, and submit its final report to the Minister of Interior with recommendations for appropriate actions and possible compensation.

Authorities have emphasized that all relevant records and material evidence must be preserved, warning that any obstruction, witness intimidation, or evidence concealment will be treated as a serious offense. While investigations continue into potential factors such as facility overcrowding and exposure to hazardous conditions, official determinations regarding criminal liability and final causes of death remain pending.
