Federal Government Suspends 20 NSCDC Officers Over 37 Custodial Deaths in Niger State

The Federal Government has suspended 20 Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps officers and the Niger State Commandant following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody, as Niger State Governor Umar Bago stated the state was unaware of their arrest.

The Federal Government has suspended 20 officers of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC), alongside the Niger State Commandant, following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody. The directive follows an initial arrest of 67 artisanal and suspected illegal miners by NSCDC personnel during operations conducted in the M.I. Wushishi and Lukoto areas of Niger State on September 15 and 16, 2026.

Government Reaction and State Position

Minister of Interior Olubunmi Tunji-Ojo announced the suspensions and constituted a 10-member independent investigative committee following a directive from President Bola Tinubu. Meanwhile, Niger State Governor Mohammed Umaru Bago stated that the state government had no prior knowledge of the arrests or detentions. Speaking during a television interview, Governor Bago explained that the state only became aware of the detentions after information about the fatalities surfaced and protests erupted, noting that the security agencies involved operate under federal control.

Independent Investigation Panel

The presidential independent investigation committee is chaired by Jonathan Kure, a retired Department of State Services Deputy Director General, with former Nigerian Law School Director General Prof. Isa Hayatu Chiroma serving as secretary. Other committee members include retired AIG Hosea Hassan Karma, Prof. Olayinka Buhari, representatives from the Minna Emirate Council and the Niger State Government, Miners Association of Nigeria National Secretary Liman Sulaiman, human rights lawyer Deji Adeyanju, Blueprint journalist Zainab Suleiman Okino, and public affairs analyst George Agbakahi.

The panel has been mandated to:

  • Establish the identities of the 37 deceased individuals.
  • Investigate the circumstances surrounding their arrest, detention, and subsequent deaths.
  • Determine responsibility, complicity, negligence, and misconduct.
  • Recommend appropriate actions, including compensation where applicable, and preventive measures.

The committee has been given two weeks to complete its assignment and submit its report to the Minister of Interior. Minister Tunji-Ojo warned that attempts to obstruct the investigation, destroy evidence, or intimidate witnesses would be treated as serious offenses.

Detention and Ongoing Actions

The suspended personnel include officers involved in station guard, arrest, investigation, intelligence, legal, and guard duties, while Niger State Commandant Suberu Aniviye was suspended separately. According to updates from the Punch, additional personnel have faced detention at the NSCDC National Headquarters in Abuja, where they are undergoing administrative disciplinary procedures and interrogation before being made available to investigative panels.
