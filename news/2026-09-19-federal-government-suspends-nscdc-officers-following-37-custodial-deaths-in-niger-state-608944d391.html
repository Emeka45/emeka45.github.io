Federal Government Suspends NSCDC Officers Following 37 Custodial Deaths in Niger State

The Federal Government has suspended 20 Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps officers and the Niger State Commandant following the tragic deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody. Political parties have demanded a thorough investigation into the incident.

The Federal Government has taken decisive administrative action by suspending 20 personnel of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC), alongside the Niger State Commandant, following the controversial deaths of 37 detainees in custody as reported by The Reporter. The victims were reportedly held on suspicion of involvement in illegal mining activities within Niger State before losing their lives while in detention.

In response to the tragedy, authorities have established an investigative panel to uncover the circumstances surrounding the fatalities. Custodial deaths involving such a high number of detainees have raised serious concerns regarding human rights standards, detention conditions, and operational protocols within security agencies operating across the country.

Political Condemnation and Calls for Transparency

The incident has drawn sharp reactions from political bodies. According to The Punch, the Allied Peoples Movement (APM) has strongly condemned the deaths of the 37 suspected illegal miners. The political party has called for a fully transparent and independent probe to ensure that accountability is enforced and justice is served for the victims and their families.

As the newly established panel begins its investigation, public scrutiny remains intense. Stakeholders and civil society groups are closely monitoring the proceedings to see whether the suspended officers will face prosecution if found culpable, and what systemic reforms will be implemented to prevent future occurrences within custodial facilities nationwide.
