Federal Government Suspends NSCDC Officers Following 37 Custodial Deaths in Niger State

The Federal Government has suspended 20 Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps officers and the Niger State Commandant following the tragic deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody. Political parties have demanded a thorough investigation into the incident.

The Federal Government has suspended 20 officers of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC) alongside the Niger State Commandant following the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in custody. The tragic incident, which unfolded on Thursday, September 17, 2026, prompted widespread public concern and demands for accountability from political organizations.

Background and Arrest Details

The 37 victims were among a group of suspects arrested during separate operations conducted on September 15 and 16 by the NSCDC in M.I. Wushishi and Lukoto villages in Niger State. According to the Niger State NSCDC Commandant, Suberu Aniviye, the individuals were detained following these operations. Reports surrounding the detention highlighted severe overcrowding, with claims that 66 detainees were confined to a single cell under harsh conditions.

While authorities initially pointed to a suspected disease outbreak within the cell, critics and political bodies have raised serious questions regarding the environment. The Allied Peoples Movement (APM), through its National Publicity Secretary Abubakar Yusuf, condemned the fatalities, describing them as avoidable and pointing to excessive heat and alleged exposure to an unidentified substance inside the facility as contributing factors.

Government Action and Independent Investigation Panel

Following a directive from President Bola Tinubu for a comprehensive inquiry, Minister of Interior Olubunmi Tunji Ojo announced the suspension of the Niger State Commandant, Suberu Aniviye, and 20 other personnel involved in station guard, arrest, investigation, intelligence, legal, and guard duties. To oversee the probe, the Minister constituted a 10-member independent investigative committee. The panel is chaired by Jonathan Kure, a retired Department of State Services Deputy Director General, with Prof. Isa Hayatu Chiroma, former Director General of the Nigerian Law School, serving as secretary. Other prominent members include retired AIG Hosea Hassan Karma, Prof. Olayinka Buhari, representatives of the Minna Emirate Council and the Niger State Government, Liman Sulaiman of the Miners Association of Nigeria, human rights lawyer Deji Adeyanju, journalist Zainab Suleiman Okino, and public affairs analyst George Agbakahi.

The committee has been mandated to:

  • Establish the identities of the deceased individuals.
  • Investigate the circumstances of their arrest, detention, and subsequent deaths.
  • Determine responsibility, complicity, negligence, and misconduct.
  • Recommend appropriate actions, potential compensation, and measures to prevent future occurrences.

The panel has been given two weeks to complete its assignment and submit its report. Minister Tunji Ojo warned that any attempts to obstruct the investigation, conceal evidence, or intimidate witnesses will face severe penalties.

Political Reactions and Public Unrest

The incident has drawn sharp criticism from opposition parties. The APM demanded an expeditious, open, and transparent investigation, criticizing the conditions under which detainees were held and expressing alarm over reports that security agencies utilized ammunition and tear gas against protesters who took to the streets after the news broke. The party cautioned that such security responses risk causing further casualties and emphasized that citizens' rights and dignity must be respected under the 1999 Constitution.
